You don't need a big team to run like one. **Marketing automation** lets a small business
capture leads, follow up, and keep customers coming back — without anyone remembering to hit
send. Here are seven automations worth setting up first.

## 1. Instant lead response

The fastest replier usually wins. Auto-respond to every form submission and inquiry within
seconds, then notify your team. Speed-to-lead is one of the highest-ROI changes you can make.

## 2. Email & SMS welcome sequence

When someone signs up or buys, trigger a short sequence that introduces your brand, sets
expectations, and points to your best content or offer. It runs once and works forever.

## 3. Abandoned cart / abandoned inquiry recovery

For e-commerce, recover carts automatically. For service businesses, follow up with people who
started a quote or booking but didn't finish. This recovers revenue you've already paid to
earn.

## 4. Review & referral requests

After a purchase or completed project, automatically ask happy customers for a review or
referral. More reviews mean more trust — and more inbound leads.

## 5. Lead scoring & routing

Automatically tag and route leads based on behavior or form answers, so your team spends time
on the ones most likely to convert.

## 6. Re-engagement campaigns

Automatically reach out to customers who've gone quiet with a check-in or offer. Reactivating
an existing customer is far cheaper than acquiring a new one.

## 7. Reporting on autopilot

Pipe your marketing and sales numbers into a single dashboard that updates itself. No more
manual spreadsheets — just a clear view of what's working.

## Where to start

Don't automate everything at once. Pick the one workflow tied to your biggest leak — usually
**lead response** or **follow-up** — set it up well, then expand. The goal isn't more tools;
it's fewer things falling through the cracks.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need expensive software?** No. Most small businesses can start with their existing CRM
or email tool. The strategy matters more than the platform.

**Will automation feel impersonal?** Done right, it feels *more* responsive — timely, relevant
messages beat silence every time.
