There's a tension at the heart of every international brand decision: adapt too little and you
feel foreign; adapt too much and you become generic. The best cross-border brands resolve this
by knowing exactly what is **fixed** and what is **flexible**.

## Separate the core from the expression

Your brand has a core — the promise, the values, the personality — and an expression: the
words, visuals, and references that carry it. The core travels. The expression often shouldn't.

> Coca-Cola feels like Coca-Cola everywhere. The ads look nothing alike.

## What stays fixed

- **The promise.** What you do for customers doesn't change at a border.
- **The personality.** Bold stays bold; warm stays warm.
- **The visual system.** Logo, color, and type are your passport — keep them recognizable.

## What flexes

- **Tone and idiom.** Humor, formality, and metaphor are deeply cultural.
- **Proof points.** The case studies and stats that persuade differ by market.
- **Channels and formats.** Where attention lives varies enormously by region.

## A test for every localization decision

Ask one question: *Does this change how the brand feels, or just how it's expressed?* Changing
the feeling is a strategic decision that needs sign-off. Changing the expression is craft —
and craft is exactly what local teams should own.

Get this split right and you scale a brand that feels native everywhere and consistent
everywhere — at the same time.
