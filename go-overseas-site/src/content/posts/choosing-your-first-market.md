“Where should we expand first?” is the most consequential question in international growth —
and the one most often answered by instinct. Here's the framework we use to replace gut feel
with evidence.

## Score markets on four axes

We rate every candidate market from 1–5 on four dimensions, then weight them to your reality.

| Axis | What it measures |
| --- | --- |
| **Demand** | Is there proven, addressable need for your category? |
| **Access** | How hard is it to reach buyers and partners? |
| **Friction** | Regulatory, tax, language, and operational drag |
| **Fit** | Alignment with your product, brand, and team strengths |

A market that scores 5 on demand but 1 on friction is a trap. A balanced 4-4-4-4 usually beats
a spiky 5-2-2-5.

## Weight to your constraints

A well-funded scale-up can absorb friction that would sink a bootstrapped company. Weight the
axes to your runway:

- **Short runway?** Over-index on *access* and *friction* — speed to revenue matters most.
- **Strategic bet?** Over-index on *demand* and *fit* — you're playing a longer game.

## Pressure-test the top two

Never decide on the spreadsheet alone. Take your top two markets and run a two-week
validation sprint: talk to ten prospective buyers, two potential partners, and one local
operator. The numbers tell you where to look; the conversations tell you what's real.

## Commit, then concentrate

Once you've chosen, commit fully. The single biggest predictor of a successful first market is
not which market you picked — it's whether you gave it enough to win.
