When a brand starts to feel off, businesses reach for one of two words: **rebrand** or
**refresh**. They're not the same thing — and choosing the wrong one wastes money or leaves
the real problem unsolved. Here's how to tell which you need.

## The difference in one line

- A **brand refresh** modernizes what you already have — logo polish, updated colors and type,
  a sharper website — without changing who you are.
- A **rebrand** rethinks the foundation — positioning, personality, sometimes the name —
  because who you are has changed.

| | Refresh | Rebrand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | Visual & surface | Strategic & deep |
| Changes the name? | No | Sometimes |
| Risk | Low | Higher |
| When | Looks dated | Meaning has shifted |

## When a refresh is enough

Choose a refresh when the **strategy still fits** but the execution has aged. Signs: your logo
feels stuck in time, your site looks inconsistent, or your brand no longer matches the quality
of your work. A refresh keeps your existing recognition while raising the bar.

## When you actually need a rebrand

Choose a rebrand when **what you stand for has changed**. Signs: you've pivoted your offering,
merged, outgrown your original audience, your name limits you, or the brand carries baggage you
need to leave behind. A rebrand resets perception — powerful, but not to be done lightly.

## What it costs to get wrong

Refresh when you needed a rebrand, and you've polished a message that no longer fits. Rebrand
when a refresh would do, and you've thrown away hard-won recognition. The decision is strategic
before it's creative — start there.

## How the process works

1. **Discovery** — audience, market, and where you actually stand.
2. **Strategy** — positioning and the story you want to own.
3. **Identity** — visual system, voice, and guidelines.
4. **Rollout** — website, social, and every touchpoint, consistently.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should we refresh?** Many brands refresh every 3–5 years and rebrand far less
often — only when strategy genuinely shifts.

**Will a rebrand hurt our SEO or recognition?** It can if handled carelessly. Done right — with
redirects, consistent naming, and a clear rollout — it protects what you've built.
