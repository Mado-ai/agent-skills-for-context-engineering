# The Little Founders Book Series

Four books, one per tier. Each is the learner-facing companion to that tier's ten core courses —
the thing a student actually holds, fills in, and takes home.

| Tier | Ages | Book | What it teaches | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🌱 Little Sprouts | 5–7 | **How Is It Made?** | Discover the world | ✅ Built — [`how-is-it-made.html`](how-is-it-made.html) |
| 🔨 Junior Builders | 8–10 | **Mission Control** | Learn to plan and organize | Planned |
| 🚀 Young Founders | 11–13 | **Reverse. Rethink. Rebuild.** | Learn to question and improve | ✅ Built — [`reverse-rethink-rebuild.html`](reverse-rethink-rebuild.html) |
| 👑 Founder CEOs | 14–17 | **GO OVERSEAS!** | Build it, brand it, take it into the world | ✅ Built — [`go-overseas.html`](go-overseas.html) |

The progression is deliberate: **discover → plan → question → build.** A learner who moves through
all four meets the same entrepreneurial cycle four times, each time with more independence and
higher real-world stakes.

## Book 1 — How Is It Made?

**Little Sprouts · Ages 5–7 · Discovery Series**

> Become a Maker Detective!

Ziggy the Fox guides learners through ten everyday objects, each one traced back to where it came
from. The book teaches one repeating pattern — **the Maker Pattern** — and then the founder
question that turns discovery into invention.

**The Maker Pattern**
1. Where does it come from?
2. What happens to it?
3. Who helps make it?
4. What does it become?

…and then: *"How could I make it even better?"*

**The ten journeys**

| # | Topic | Journey |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pencil | Tree → Wood → Graphite → Glue & shape → Paint → Pencil |
| 2 | Paper | Tree → Wood chips → Pulp → Press & dry → Paper |
| 3 | T-shirt | Cotton → Yarn → Fabric → Dye → Cut & sew → T-shirt |
| 4 | Bread | Wheat → Flour → Dough → Rise → Bake → Bread |
| 5 | Juice | Fruit → Wash → Squeeze → Juice → Bottle |
| 6 | Toy | Design → Plastic beads → Melt → Mould → Paint → Toy |
| 7 | Chocolate | Cacao pod → Beans → Roast → Grind → Add sugar → Chocolate |
| 8 | Book | Idea → Write → Print → Fold pages → Add cover → Book |
| 9 | Chair | Tree → Wood pieces → Shape → Join → Finish → Chair |
| 10 | Food to store | Farm → Harvest → Pack → Truck → Store → Home |

**In the digital version**

- Ziggy reads every page aloud (browser speech — no reading required to take part)
- Journeys animate one stage at a time, or play end to end
- Step cards are tap-to-reveal, so the learner predicts before they read
- A real drawing pad for every *Think like a founder* prompt, saved between visits
- 10 Maker Stars, earned by playing the whole journey and drawing an invention
- Maker Words glossary that unlocks word by word
- The Founder Card and a printable **Maker Detective** certificate
- Every invention the child drew, gathered into a gallery on the award page

Ends with: *Aligned to EntreComp · CASEL 5 · Ontario curriculum · 4Cs.*

## Book 4 — GO OVERSEAS!

**Founder CEOs · Ages 14–17 · Build Your First Brand**

> From an idea in your head to a brand people can recognise, trust and buy from.

**Mindset:** Think → Define → Brand → Price → Market → Connect → Automate → Launch → Grow

**Fourteen parts, twenty-three chapters**

| Part | Chapters | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Start with the idea | 1 My Business Idea | My Business in One Sentence |
| 2 Give it an identity | 2 Name · 3 Colours · 4 Fonts | Brand Style Card |
| 3 What does my brand believe? | 5 Mission · 6 Vision · 7 Core Values | Values defined as behaviours |
| 4 Know who you're building for | 8 Target Audience · 9 Customer Journey | Customer persona + 8-rung journey |
| 5 Price it | 10 What does it really cost? · 11 Pricing | Cost, price, gross profit, margin % |
| 6 Get people to notice you | 12 Awareness · 13 Consideration · 14 Conversion | Channels, proof, one call to action |
| 7 The marketing funnel | — | My First Marketing Funnel |
| 8 Understand the numbers | 15 Cost per lead · 16 Cost per conversion | A verdict, not a view count |
| 9 Build relationships | 17 CRM | A real customer list and pipeline |
| 10 Automation | 18 Work smarter | My First Automation Map |
| 11 Build the community | 19 Community | My Community Promise |
| 12 Protect the business | 20 Privacy · 21 Policies · 22 Terms | Data map + business rules checklist |
| 13 Put it together | 23 My Brand Blueprint | 24-section blueprint |
| 14 GO OVERSEAS! | — | Founder Passport → Certified Founder CEO |

**In the digital version**

- **Live brand preview** — the moment a colour, font, name, tagline, or call to action changes,
  a real brand card re-renders in those choices. Design decisions become visible immediately.
- **Name Storm scoring** — up to ten names scored 0–2 against six tests, with the leader
  highlighted automatically
- **Colour psychology picker** — twelve hues with what each one makes people feel, feeding four
  defined brand slots (primary, secondary, accent, background)
- **Font pairing** — three roles, six web-safe families that render identically for every viewer
- **Mission builder** — the *We help \_\_\_ to \_\_\_ by \_\_\_* template assembles live
- **Persona card** that builds itself from the eight audience questions
- **Cost calculator → pricing → margin**, with a written verdict when the margin is thin or
  negative
- **Unit economics engine** — CPL, cost per conversion, lead→customer rate, gross profit per
  customer, and a plain-language ruling on whether the advertising is working, with the
  arithmetic shown
- **CRM board** with a five-stage pipeline (Lead → Prospect → Customer → Returning → Advocate)
  and live counts
- **Automation map** — reorderable step flow
- **Privacy data map**, five policies, and a seven-point terms checklist
- **Brand Blueprint** — 24 sections that fill themselves from everything above; gaps stay visible
  on purpose
- **Founder Passport** — ten stamps (💡 🎨 ❤️ 👥 💰 📣 🔄 ⚡ 🌎 🚀) that award themselves when the
  underlying work is genuinely complete, ending in a printable **Certified Founder CEO**
  certificate for the Founder Finale

**A note on the legal chapters.** Part 12 is business literacy, not law. The workbook builds a
rules checklist and says plainly that real terms should be reviewed by an appropriate
professional before trading. The Canadian operating checks in
[`../05-standards-and-safeguarding.md`](../05-standards-and-safeguarding.md) apply.

## Book 2 — planned

**Mission Control** (8–10) — *Learn to plan and organize.* Junior Builders make and sell real
things, so this book should be the mission planner: choosing the job, listing what you need,
sequencing the work, assigning the crew, tracking the countdown, and running the launch. The
natural interactive spine is a mission board with a checklist, a supply manifest, a crew roster,
and a countdown to Market Day.

It follows the same rules as the built books: one repeating pattern taught by repetition, every
screen ends with something the learner made, and the certificate is criterion-based.

## Book 3 — Reverse. Rethink. Rebuild.

**Young Founders · Ages 11–13 · Look deeper. Think better. Build smarter.**

> Question. Rethink. Improve. Create.

Four steps, run on one real object the learner chooses:

| Step | | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Observe** | Train your curiosity. Notice details. Understand how the world works |
| 2 | **Ask better questions** | Go beyond the obvious. Ask "why," "what if," and "how else?" |
| 3 | **Rethink solutions** | Challenge the status quo. Explore creative possibilities |
| 4 | **Build better ideas** | Turn thinking into action. Build ideas that solve real problems |

**In the digital version**

- **Four worked teardowns** — a backpack, a water bottle, a bus stop, and the lunch queue, each
  pulled apart properly, so learners see what good looks like before doing their own
- **Parts and noticing lists** — you cannot rebuild what you have not noticed
- **The question ladder** — write the obvious question, then push it. Ten question starters that
  break assumptions fastest, and one question marked as *my best*
- **Six lenses** — lighter, stronger, cheaper, simpler, kinder, for-someone-else. The instruction
  is to use one you resist
- **Assumption tracker** — every belief sorted into *known* or *guess*, with a live count of the
  guesses
- **Test designer** — the pass threshold must be written *before* the test runs, because a
  success defined afterwards teaches nothing
- **Before → After** comparison, what changed and why, who it helps, and a sketch pad
- **Rebuild Report** and the **Reverse Thinker** certificate
