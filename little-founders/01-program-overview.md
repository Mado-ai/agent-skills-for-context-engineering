# Program Overview

## Why this program exists

Most young people meet "business" as a vocabulary list: profit, marketing, supply, demand.
They can define the words and do nothing with them. Little Founders inverts that. Learners
notice a real problem, make something for a real person, ask that person what they think,
and change it. The vocabulary arrives afterwards, attached to something they did.

The outcome we are after is not a child who can run a company. It is a child who believes
that when something is broken, *making it better is a thing a person like them can do* — and
who has the working habits to try.

## The promise

> Empowering kids with entrepreneurial skills, confidence, and a mindset to build the future.

Three commitments sit underneath it:

1. **Everything is real.** Real problems, real users, real feedback, real (small) money at
   Builder and Founder tiers. Simulations are used only where reality would be unsafe.
2. **Failure is data, not verdict.** The most-awarded badge in the system, *Keep Building*,
   can only be earned after something did not work.
3. **Nobody is a passenger.** Every session ends with each learner holding an artifact —
   a drawing, a number, a prototype, a piece of feedback — that they made.

## The learning model

Every module, at every tier, runs the same four-beat cycle. Learners learn the cycle by
repetition; by Module 5 they can name where they are in it without prompting.

```
   NOTICE  ──▶  MAKE  ──▶  SHOW  ──▶  CHANGE
      ▲                                  │
      └──────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Beat | What happens | Typical session |
|---|---|---|
| **Notice** | Observe, question, gather; find the problem before the solution | Session 1 |
| **Make** | Build the smallest version that can be reacted to | Session 2 |
| **Show** | Put it in front of someone who is not you | Session 3 |
| **Change** | Act on what you heard; decide what to keep | Session 4 |

This maps to the brand's four words — **Dream. Plan. Build. Grow.** — which learners see on
walls and workbooks. The teaching team uses NOTICE / MAKE / SHOW / CHANGE; learners use both
interchangeably.

### Why four sessions per module

Three is too few to include a real feedback loop; five loses the thread between weekly
sessions. Four gives every module one full cycle, so the *shape* of entrepreneurial work is
taught ten times over, not just its content. Module 9 (Startup Challenge) is the deliberate
exception at six sessions — it runs two cycles back to back.

## The three tiers

Tiers are not "the same lesson, easier." Each tier changes what the learner is responsible
for, what counts as evidence, and how much of the work is theirs alone.

### Spark — ages 6–8

- **Session:** 45 minutes. Movement every 12–15 minutes.
- **Group:** 8–12 learners, 1 adult per 6.
- **Money:** Play money only. No real transactions, ever.
- **Customer:** Classmates, family, class mascot. Feedback is a smiley/straight/sad face card.
- **Evidence:** Drawings, spoken sentences captured by an adult, photographs of builds.
- **Independence:** Adult scribes, learner decides.
- **Reading load:** Near zero. All handouts are picture-first with ≤ 8 words.

### Builder — ages 9–12

- **Session:** 60 minutes.
- **Group:** 10–16 learners, 1 adult per 8.
- **Money:** Real but capped. Class float of up to $50 per team, supervised. Proceeds are
  split by a rule the group agrees in Module 3.
- **Customer:** Other classes, school staff, families at a market stall.
- **Evidence:** Workbook pages, a one-page plan, simple spreadsheets, prototype photos.
- **Independence:** Teams of 3–4 self-organise; adult unblocks rather than directs.
- **Reading load:** Moderate. Handouts ~150 words.

### Founder — ages 13–16

- **Session:** 90 minutes.
- **Group:** 12–20 learners, 1 adult per 12.
- **Money:** Real, tracked properly. Ledger, unit economics, and a break-even calculation are
  required artifacts. Local rules on youth trading are checked before launch.
- **Customer:** Strangers. Public market, online store under adult supervision, local
  businesses.
- **Evidence:** Business model canvas, financial model, customer interview notes,
  a five-minute pitch on video.
- **Independence:** Learners set their own sprint goals and report against them.
- **Reading load:** Full. Case studies, terms sheets in plain language, real invoices.

### Choosing a tier

Age is the default, but place by readiness where they conflict:

- A 12-year-old who has never worked in a team → start Builder even if reading is strong.
- A capable 9-year-old joining a Founder cohort → only with a paired mentor and no money
  handling.
- Mixed-age groups → run Builder as the spine and use the Spark *Make* activities as the
  scaffold for younger members and the Founder *Change* activities as extension.

## Program outcomes

By the end of the full program a learner can:

**Think**
1. Find a problem worth solving by observing people, not by guessing.
2. Tell the difference between an idea, a product, and a business.
3. Read a simple profit-and-loss and say whether something is working.

**Make**
4. Build a rough version of a solution fast, in the cheapest material that will do.
5. Change a design based on what a user said, not what the maker preferred.

**Communicate**
6. Explain what they made, who it is for, and why it matters, in under two minutes.
7. Ask a customer an open question and listen without defending.

**Lead**
8. Take a role on a team, do it, and hand it over cleanly.
9. Give feedback that is specific and kind, and receive it without collapsing.

**Decide**
10. Choose to continue, change, or stop a project on evidence, and say why.

Outcome 10 is the hardest and the most important. Founder tier explicitly rewards a
well-argued decision to stop.

## Delivery formats

The 42-session tier fits several shapes:

| Format | Cadence | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weekly club** | 1 session/week | ~42 weeks | Best fit for a school year with holidays. Default. |
| **Semester course** | 2 sessions/week | 21 weeks | Modules land harder; keep Module 9 in one week. |
| **After-school** | 2 sessions/week | 21 weeks | Shorten Founder sessions to 60 min, drop to 3 sessions/module for Modules 2, 4, 8. |
| **Camp intensive** | 3 sessions/day | 14 days | Modules 1–5 in week one, 6–10 in week two. Demo Day on day 14. |
| **Single-module taster** | 4 sessions | 4 weeks | Module 1 or Module 5 stand alone well. Modules 3, 7, 9 do not. |

Do not compress Module 9 below five sessions in any format. The build-sell-learn loop is the
part learners remember.

## Roles

| Role | Who | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Guide** | Lead teacher/facilitator | Runs sessions, holds the cycle, awards badges |
| **Helper** | Assistant, parent volunteer, older learner | Circulates, scribes for Spark, unblocks |
| **Customer** | Anyone outside the team | Reacts honestly to work in progress |
| **Mentor** | Local business owner, alumnus | Founder tier only; 3 touchpoints (Modules 2, 7, 9) |
| **Family** | At home | One 10-minute home extension per module |

The Guide's hardest discipline is **not rescuing**. When a team's prototype fails on Session 3,
the failure is the lesson. The Guide's job is to make sure Session 4 exists.

## Family involvement

Every module has one home extension of about ten minutes, written directly to the family, in
plain language, with no purchases required. Families are also invited to three events:

- **Idea Fair** — after Module 1
- **Market Day** — during Module 9
- **Demo Day** — closing Module 10

Families receive a short note after each module explaining what was learned and one question
to ask at dinner. Sample notes appear at the end of each module file.

## What this program is not

- **Not a get-rich scheme.** Any real money is small, supervised, and mostly reinvested or
  donated by group decision.
- **Not a coding or STEM course.** Technology appears as a tool when a learner reaches for it.
- **Not a pitch competition.** Competition exists (Module 9), but badges are criterion-based:
  every learner can earn every badge.
- **Not age-locked.** A Spark learner who is ready for Builder work should get it.

## Integration with the brand system

Curriculum language is the brand language. The learning map, badge art, sticker sheet, and
dashboard copy all draw their vocabulary from these documents — module names, tier names,
badge names, and the four cycle words are fixed terms and should not be paraphrased in any
learner-facing surface. See [02-brand-and-voice.md](02-brand-and-voice.md).
