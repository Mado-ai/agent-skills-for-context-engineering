# Program Overview

## Why this program exists

Most young people meet "business" as a vocabulary list: profit, marketing, supply, demand.
They can define the words and do nothing with them. Little Founders inverts that. Learners
notice a real problem, make something for a real person, ask that person what they think, and
change it. The vocabulary arrives afterwards, attached to something they did.

The outcome we are after is not a child who can run a company. It is a child who believes that
when something is broken, *making it better is a thing a person like them can do* — and who has
the working habits to try.

## The promise

> Little Founders empowers children ages 5–17 with real-world skills to imagine, create and
> build a better future.

Three commitments sit underneath it:

1. **Everything is real.** Real problems, real users, real feedback, and real money from Junior
   Builders upward. Simulations are used only where reality would be unsafe.
2. **Failure is data, not verdict.** The most-awarded badge in the system, *Keep Building*, can
   only be earned after something did not work.
3. **Nobody is a passenger.** Every session ends with each learner holding something they made
   — a drawing, a number, a prototype, a piece of feedback.

## The learning model

Every course, at every tier, runs the same four-beat cycle. Learners learn the cycle by
repetition; by Course 5 they can name where they are in it without prompting.

```
   NOTICE  ──▶  MAKE  ──▶  SHOW  ──▶  CHANGE
      ▲                                  │
      └──────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Beat | What happens | Typical session |
|---|---|---|
| **Notice** | Observe, question, gather; find the problem before the solution | Session 1 |
| **Make** | Build the smallest version that can be reacted to | Session 2 |
| **Show** | Put it in front of someone who is not you | Session 3 |
| **Change** | Act on what you heard; decide what to keep | Session 4 |

Learners also meet it as **Dream. Plan. Build. Grow.** The two are interchangeable; the teaching
team uses the first, learner-facing surfaces use both.

### Why four sessions per course

Three is too few to include a real feedback loop; five loses the thread between weekly sessions.
Four gives every course one full cycle, so the *shape* of entrepreneurial work is taught ten
times over, not just its content. Course 9 (Startup Challenge) is the deliberate exception at
six sessions — it runs two cycles back to back.

## The spiral

This is a spiral curriculum: the same ten courses run at all four tiers, and each pass raises
what the learner is responsible for. Nothing is "done" at a tier — it comes back harder.

| | 🌱 Little Sprouts | 🔨 Junior Builders | 🚀 Young Founders | 👑 Founder CEOs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ages** | 5–7 | 8–10 | 11–13 | 14–17 |
| **Verb** | Explore | Build & sell | Plan & validate | Launch & lead |
| **Session** | 45 min | 60 min | 75 min | 90 min |
| **Group** | 8–12 | 10–16 | 12–18 | 12–20 |
| **Ratio** | 1 : 6 | 1 : 8 | 1 : 10 | 1 : 12 |
| **Money** | Play money only | Real, $50 CAD team float | Real, $100 CAD, tracked | Real, $150 CAD, full ledger |
| **Customer** | Classmates and family | Other classes, staff, families | The public at Market Day | Strangers, online, businesses |
| **Evidence** | Drawings, scribed sentences, photos | Cost sheets, prototypes, sales tally | One-page plan, validation data, pitch | Canvas, financial model, venture report |
| **Independence** | Adult scribes, learner decides | Teams of 3–4 with assigned roles | Teams self-organise; adult unblocks | Learners set and report their own goals |
| **Reading load** | Near zero, picture-first | Light, ~80 words per handout | Moderate, ~150 words | Full — case studies, real invoices |
| **Certification** | Junior Founder | Certified Builder | Certified Young Founder | Certified Young Entrepreneur |

### What separates the middle two tiers

Junior Builders and Young Founders are the pair most often blurred. Keep them distinct:

- **Junior Builders make things and sell them.** The learning is in production, pricing, and
  the moment a stranger hands over money. Planning is light and mostly verbal.
- **Young Founders plan, validate, and pitch.** They must show that someone wants it *before*
  they build much of it. Their Market Day stall is the test of a claim they made in Course 2,
  not the point of the year.

If a Young Founders cohort is behaving like Junior Builders — building first, asking later —
that is the sign to slow down and re-run the Course 1 validation work.

### Choosing a tier

Age is the default; place by readiness where they conflict.

- A 10-year-old who has never worked in a team → Junior Builders, even if reading is strong.
- A capable 7-year-old joining Junior Builders → fine, with a paired mentor and no money
  handling.
- A 14-year-old new to the program → Founder CEOs, but run the Course 1 and Course 3 Young
  Founders activities as a bridge in the first two weeks.
- Mixed-age groups → run the middle tier as the spine, use the tier below for scaffolding and
  the tier above for extension.

## Program outcomes

By the end of a tier a learner can:

**Think**
1. Find a problem worth solving by observing people, not by guessing.
2. Tell the difference between an idea, a product, and a business.
3. Read a simple profit-and-loss and say whether something is working.

**Make**
4. Build a rough version fast, in the cheapest material that will do.
5. Change a design based on what a user said, not what the maker preferred.

**Communicate**
6. Explain what they made, who it is for, and why it matters, in under two minutes.
7. Ask a customer an open question and listen without defending.

**Lead**
8. Take a role on a team, do it, and hand it over cleanly.
9. Give feedback that is specific and kind, and receive it without collapsing.

**Decide**
10. Choose to continue, change, or stop a project on evidence, and say why.

Outcome 10 is the hardest and the most important. Founder CEOs explicitly rewards a well-argued
decision to stop.

## Delivery formats

42 sessions per tier fits several shapes:

| Format | Cadence | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weekly club** | 1 session/week | ~42 weeks | Default. Fits a Canadian school year with breaks |
| **Semester course** | 2 sessions/week | 21 weeks | Keep Course 9 inside a single week |
| **After-school** | 2 sessions/week | 21 weeks | Trim Founder CEOs sessions to 60 min; 3 sessions for Courses 2, 4, 8 |
| **March Break / summer camp** | 3 sessions/day | 14 days | Courses 1–5 in week one, 6–10 in week two |
| **Single-course taster** | 4 sessions | 4 weeks | Courses 1 or 5 stand alone. Courses 3, 7, 9 do not |

Do not compress Course 9 below five sessions in any format. The build–sell–learn loop is the
part learners remember.

## Roles

| Role | Who | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Guide** | Lead teacher/facilitator | Runs sessions, holds the cycle, awards badges |
| **Helper** | Assistant, parent volunteer, alumnus | Circulates, scribes at Little Sprouts, unblocks |
| **Customer** | Anyone outside the team | Reacts honestly to work in progress |
| **Mentor** | Local business owner, alumnus | Young Founders and Founder CEOs only — three touchpoints (Courses 2, 7, 9) |
| **Family** | At home | One 10-minute home extension per course |

The Guide's hardest discipline is **not rescuing**. When a team's prototype fails on Session 3,
the failure is the lesson. The Guide's job is to make sure Session 4 exists.

## Bilingual delivery

Programs run in English, in French, or bilingually. In a bilingual cohort:

- Fixed terms carry both names on every printed and on-screen surface — see the glossary in
  [02-brand-and-voice.md](02-brand-and-voice.md).
- Learners work, pitch, and write in the language they think fastest in. A pitch delivered in
  French to an English audience with a translated one-line summary earns full credit.
- Handouts are produced in both languages at Little Sprouts and Junior Builders. From Young
  Founders up, learners may choose one.
- Guides do not correct language errors during business work. Language support happens
  separately, or it competes with the thinking the session is for.

## Family involvement

Every course has one home extension of about ten minutes, written to the family in plain
language, needing no purchases. Families are also invited to the three events: **Idea Fair**
after Course 1, **Market Day** inside Course 9, and **Demo Day** closing Course 10. A short note
goes home after Courses 3, 6, 9, and 10 with one question to ask at dinner.

## What this program is not

- **Not a get-rich scheme.** Real money is small, supervised, and mostly reinvested or given
  back by group decision.
- **Not a coding or STEM course.** Technology appears as a tool when a learner reaches for it.
- **Not a pitch competition.** Competition exists in Course 9, but badges are criterion-based:
  every learner can earn every badge.
- **Not age-locked.** A learner ready for the tier above should get it.
