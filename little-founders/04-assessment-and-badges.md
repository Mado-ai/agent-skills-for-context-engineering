# Assessment & Badges

There are no tests in Little Founders. Assessment is evidence-based and criterion-referenced:
a learner shows what they made and says what they learned, and that is measured against
published criteria every learner can meet.

Three principles hold everywhere in this system:

1. **Criterion, not comparison.** Every learner can earn every badge. Nobody's badge is made
   less valuable by someone else earning it.
2. **Evidence, not assertion.** "I learned about marketing" is not evidence. A poster, a test
   tally, and a sentence about what changed is.
3. **Failure counts.** Several badges can only be earned by something going wrong first. This
   is deliberate and should be said out loud, often.

---

## What gets assessed

| Layer | Frequency | Form | Recorded where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Session log** | Every session | One line: what I did, what I learned | Workbook / dashboard |
| **Peer feedback** | Every module, S3 | *I like… I wonder… What if…* | Artifact back / feedback log |
| **Module artifact** | Every module, S4 | The module's main output, against a 3-level rubric | Portfolio |
| **Badge check** | Every module, S4 | Criteria met / not yet | Learning Map |
| **Family note** | After M3, M6, M9, M10 | Short written progress note | Sent home |
| **Portfolio review** | Module 10 | Six curated pieces with captions | Demo Day |

"Not yet" is the only failing grade in the program, and it is always paired with what would
make it a yes.

## The rubric scale

Every module rubric uses the same three levels. There is no fourth level and no numeric score.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Emerging** | The learner attempted it; the work does not yet do its job |
| **Secure** | The work does its job. This is the target for every learner |
| **Strong** | The work does its job and shows judgement — evidence, honesty, or independence beyond what was asked |

Secure is the goal. Strong is not a prize and should not be chased; several Strong descriptors
require conditions (a failure, a hard question, a partner who said no) that a learner cannot
manufacture.

## Portfolio

The portfolio is the program's summative assessment. Six pieces, curated by the learner in
Module 10, each with a one-line caption saying what it is and what it shows.

**Required pieces (Builder and Founder):**

1. A problem artifact from Module 1
2. A plan or canvas from Module 2
3. A cost sheet with a defended price from Module 3
4. A marketing piece from Module 4 with its test results
5. A pitch recording or script from Module 5
6. The Startup Report or Venture Report from Module 9

**Plus, required at every tier:** at least one piece that did not work, with what was learned.

**Spark tier** substitutes a four-page founder book with three chosen pieces attached.

Portfolios may be paper, a folder, a slide deck, or a physical box. Format is the learner's
choice; the captions are not optional.

## Little Points

Little Points measure participation and effort — the things a learner controls — never quality.
They are how the Learning Map advances and how modules unlock.

### Earning

| Action | Points |
|---|---|
| Complete a session (present and produced the artifact) | 10 |
| Earn any badge | 25 |
| Earn **Little Founder** | 50 |
| Market Day (Module 9) | 50 |
| Demo Day (Module 10) | 50 |

Full-program maximum: 420 session points + 100 event points + 725 badge points = **1,245
lifetime points**.

### Balance vs. lifetime

The dashboard shows two different numbers and they should not be confused:

- **Lifetime earned** — never decreases. Used for recognition and for the Little Founder record.
- **Balance** — lifetime earned minus points spent on unlocks and rewards. This is the number
  shown on the Learning Map, and it is why a learner's visible balance is often lower than what
  they have earned in total.

### Spending

Points are spent on things that cost the program nothing and mean something to a learner:

| Reward | Cost |
|---|---|
| Choose the warm-up game for a session | 25 |
| Choose your team's role for the next module | 50 |
| Unlock a locked module early where the module rules allow | 100 |
| Physical badge pin (rather than sticker) | 100 |
| Name a class business, or design the cohort's stall banner | 150 |

Points are never deducted as punishment. A behaviour system that spends children's earned
credit teaches that effort is confiscatable.

### Unlock gates

| Module | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Open |
| 6 Leadership Lab | Modules 1–5 complete **and** 250 lifetime points |
| 7 Young CEO | Module 6 complete |
| 8 Community Builder | Module 7 complete |
| 9 Startup Challenge | Modules 1–8 complete + **Money Smart** + **I Can Pitch!** |
| 10 Future Founder Mastery | Module 9 complete |

The 250-point gate is set so that finishing Modules 1–5 (200 points) plus any two badges clears
it. A learner who has genuinely done the first five modules will not be blocked. If a cohort is
blocked, the Guide has been awarding session points too strictly, not the learners under-working.

| # | Badge | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | **Keep Building** | Something failed; they fixed it and went again. Awardable more than once in a program — record the first |
| 24 | **Idea Machine** | 50 problems or ideas logged cumulatively on the Idea Wall |
| 25 | **Show & Tell** | Presented five times to an audience outside their team |
| 26 | **Streak Star** | Attended and contributed to seven consecutive sessions |
| 27 | **Digital Safe** | Used an online channel following every rule in the online conduct agreement, disclosing no personal information |
| 28 | **Little Founder** | Completed all ten modules and holds a portfolio. Worth 50 points |

### Badge award rules

- **Award publicly, at the end of Session 4, every module.** Ceremony matters; thirty seconds
  per learner is enough.
- **Criteria are published to learners in advance.** They are printed on the back of the module
  divider in the workbook. Hidden criteria are not criteria.
- **"Not yet" always comes with a route.** Name the one thing that would make it a yes and when
  they can show it.
- **Never award a badge to soften something.** A badge given out of sympathy devalues every
  badge in the room, and children detect it immediately.
- **A badge can be earned late.** Learners may claim a badge in any later module by bringing the
  evidence.
- **Keep Building is the badge to be generous with** — but only where a real failure preceded it.

## Guide's assessment record

One page per learner per module. Six lines:

```
Learner:                                Module:                Tier:
Sessions attended:      /4               Artifact received: Y / N
Rubric: Problem ___  Evidence ___  Change ___  Voice ___     (E / S / St)
Badges awarded:
Not yet — and the route:
Note for family (one sentence):
```

Ten minutes per module per group of twelve, if you fill it during Session 4's Work block while
learners are working. Filling it afterwards from memory takes three times as long and is less
accurate.

## Moderation

Where more than one Guide runs the program, moderate twice a year:

1. Each Guide brings three anonymised artifacts — one they judged Emerging, one Secure, one
   Strong.
2. Others judge them blind against the same rubric.
3. Discuss only the disagreements. Adjust the shared understanding, not the rubric.

Most disagreement is about *evidence*, not quality: one Guide accepts a claim, another asks
"how do you know?" Converge on asking.

## Reporting to families

Family notes are sent after Modules 3, 6, 9, and 10. Each is three sentences plus a question:

1. What your child did.
2. What they can do now that they could not before.
3. What is next.
4. One question to ask at dinner.

No grades, no comparison to other learners, no "could try harder." Sample notes appear at the
end of each module file.

## Accessibility of assessment

Every assessment route in this program has an equivalent that does not require writing,
speaking aloud, or presenting to a large group. These are equivalent routes, not concessions,
and carry identical credit:

| Instead of | A learner may |
|---|---|
| Writing an artifact | Draw it, record it, or dictate it to a scribe |
| Presenting to a room | Present to one person, pre-record, sign, or have a partner read while they hold the prop |
| A spreadsheet | Use a paper tracker, or a pre-built template with formulas supplied |
| Making a real ask in person | Send a written ask |
| Reading a handout | Have it read aloud, or use the picture version |

The criterion is always the *capability*, never the format it arrives in.
