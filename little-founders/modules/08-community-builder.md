# Module 8 — Community Builder

**Icon:** Handshake · **Colour:** Sky · **Core badge:** Community Builder · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Module 7 · **Unlocks:** Module 9

> A business is a neighbour, not a stranger.

## Big idea

Every business sits inside a community that it takes from and gives to. Founders who see that
clearly build things people defend; founders who do not build things people tolerate until
something better arrives.

## Essential questions

- Who helps my business, and do they know I noticed?
- Who does my business affect who never chose to be affected?
- What would I do differently if the people affected were in the room?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Name people who help their business, thank them specifically, and choose something kind their business does |
| **Builder** | Map their stakeholders, set up one real partnership, and build a give-back into their plan |
| **Founder** | Analyse stakeholder impact including people with no voice, negotiate a real partnership with agreed terms, and make an ethical trade-off explicitly |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Helper | Someone who makes your business possible | Spark |
| Thank you | Saying what someone did and why it mattered | Spark |
| Community | The people around us | Spark |
| Partner | Someone we work with, both getting something | Builder |
| Stakeholder | Anyone affected by what we do | Builder |
| Give-back | What we return to the community | Builder |
| Externality | An effect on someone who is not in the deal | Founder |
| Terms | What each side agreed to do | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Who helps us?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Helper Chain.* Trace a loaf of bread back through everyone who touched it.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Nobody makes anything alone. Even a lemonade stand needs whoever bought
  the lemons, whoever lent the table, whoever let you stand there.
- **Work (25):** **Helper Web.** Learners draw their business in the middle of a page with lines
  out to every helper — family, teachers, the shop, the person who gave them cardboard. Target:
  six helpers.
- **Close (8):** Who is the helper nobody else thought of?
- **Artifact:** Helper web with ≥ 6 helpers.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Real thank-yous

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Good Thank You.* Compare "thanks" to "thank you for saving the big box for
  me — I used it for the roof." Which feels better to receive?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** A real thank-you says **what they did** and **what it made possible**.
- **Work (25):** Make three thank-you cards for real helpers using brand colours, each with both
  parts. Then deliver at least one in person, with a Helper.
- **Close (8):** How did the person react?
- **Artifact:** Three cards, one delivered in person.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Kind business

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Fair or Not?* Simple scenarios: charging a friend double, keeping the extra
  change, taking the last of a shared material.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Businesses make choices that affect other people. Kind businesses think
  about that before they decide.
- **Work (25):** **The Kind Choice.** Teams choose one kind thing their business will do —
  a free item for someone who cannot pay, tidying the space, sharing spare materials with
  another team — and do it this session.
- **Close (8):** Report what you did and who it helped.
- **Artifact:** Kind choice, done.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Our promise

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Promises We Keep.* What makes a promise real?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Writing down a promise makes it easier to keep.
- **Work (25):** Each team makes a **Community Promise** poster: one thing we will always do, one
  thing we will never do, one way we say thank you. Signed by all.
- **Close (8):** Badge check: **Community Builder**, **Kind Business**.
- **Artifact:** Signed Community Promise.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Stakeholder map

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Ripples.* Drop a stone in water; name four rings of people a school fair
  affects.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Stakeholders in three rings — **inside** (team), **close** (customers,
  suppliers, families), **wider** (school, neighbours, the environment). Every ring gets a "what
  do they give us / what do we give them" line.
- **Work (35):** Teams build a three-ring stakeholder map for their venture and fill both lines
  for at least eight stakeholders. Then mark any where the exchange is one-sided.
- **Close (10):** Name your most one-sided relationship. What would fix it?
- **Artifact:** Stakeholder map with give/get lines.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Find and prepare a partner

- **Warm-Up (5):** *What's In It For Them?* Practise pitching a partnership from the other side's
  point of view.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** A partnership is a trade, not a favour. Before you ask, know: what you
  want, what you offer, how long, and what happens if it does not work.
- **Work (35):** Identify three possible partners (another class, the school office, a local
  shop, a charity, a family business). Write a partnership ask on one page: what we want, what we
  offer, dates, and a fallback. Rehearse the ask in pairs.
- **Close (10):** Best partnership ask of the day, chosen by the room.
- **Artifact:** One-page partnership ask + rehearsal.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Make the ask

- **Warm-Up (5):** *No Is Fine.* Practise hearing "no" and asking one follow-up question.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Making the ask in person: introduce, explain in two sentences, make the
  ask, listen, agree next step in writing.
- **Work (35):** Teams make real asks — a visit to a local business with a Helper, an appointment
  with a staff member, a call with the Guide present. Every team makes at least one real ask.
  Record the outcome including refusals.
- **Close (10):** Count the room's asks and yeses. Normalise the ratio.
- **Artifact:** Ask made + outcome recorded.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Give-back built in

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Where Does the Money Go?* Revisit the Module 3 split rule.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Give-back can be money, time, product, or skill. It works best when it is
  connected to what your business already does.
- **Work (35):** Design the give-back for Module 9: what, to whom, how much, how measured. Get it
  agreed by the whole team and added to the operating plan. Then update the stakeholder map with
  the partnership that was agreed.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Community Builder**, **Deal Maker**, **Kind Business**,
  **Green Founder**.
- **Artifact:** Give-back plan + updated stakeholder map + agreed partnership.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Impact, including the silent ones

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Who Is Not in the Room?* For a delivery app, list everyone affected who
  never agreed to anything.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Externalities — costs and benefits landing on people outside the
  transaction. Then a power/interest grid: who is affected a lot but has little say? Those are
  the ones ethics is actually about.
- **Work (50):** Full stakeholder analysis: map, power/interest grid, and for the three
  highest-impact/lowest-power stakeholders, a written statement of the effect and what the
  venture will do about it. Include environmental impact as a stakeholder with no voice.
- **Close (15):** Read one externality you had not previously considered.
- **Artifact:** Stakeholder analysis + externality statements.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Ethical trade-offs, named

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Cheaper or Kinder?* A supplier is 30% cheaper with worse conditions. Argue
  both, then vote, then argue the side you did not pick.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Most business ethics are trade-offs, not villainy. The discipline is to
  name the trade-off, decide deliberately, and be able to say it out loud to the affected party.
  The test: *would I be comfortable if they read this decision record?*
- **Work (50):** Each team surfaces two real trade-offs in their own venture (pricing, sourcing,
  marketing claims, use of unpaid help), decides each with a written record, and applies the
  read-it-aloud test. Cross-team challenge panel on one decision each.
- **Close (15):** One decision you changed after the challenge.
- **Artifact:** Two ethical decision records.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Negotiate a real partnership

- **Warm-Up (10):** *BATNA.* What is your best option if this deal falls through? Knowing it is
  what stops a bad deal.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Negotiation basics for a small partnership: interests behind positions,
  trade what is cheap for you and valuable to them, agree terms in writing — scope, dates,
  responsibilities, exit.
- **Work (50):** Approach and negotiate a real partnership: a local business, a charity, a
  supplier, a venue for Market Day, another cohort. Terms written into a one-page agreement,
  signed by both sides. Adult present for any external commitment.
- **Close (15):** Read one term you traded away and one you held.
- **Artifact:** Signed one-page partnership agreement.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Community strategy for the venture

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Defensible or Tolerated?* Would your community miss you if you vanished
  tomorrow?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Community as strategy, not decoration — trust lowers your cost of getting
  customers, and partners give you reach you cannot buy. Then the give-back: connected to the
  business, measurable, and sustainable at scale.
- **Work (50):** Write the **community plan** for Module 9: stakeholders, partnership terms,
  give-back with a measurable target, ethical lines the venture will not cross, and how impact
  will be reported at Demo Day.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Community Builder**, **Deal Maker**, **Kind Business**,
  **Green Founder**.
- **Artifact:** One-page community plan.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Community Builder** (core) | Mapped who is affected and took a real action that benefited someone outside their team | 25 |
| **Deal Maker** | Agreed a real partnership or trade where both sides stated the terms and called it fair | 25 |
| **Kind Business** | Made a decision that cost them something in order to be fair to someone else, and can explain the trade-off | 25 |
| **Green Founder** | Identified an environmental impact of their venture and changed a decision because of it | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**Rubric — community:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Sees only customers | Maps direct stakeholders | Identifies affected people with no voice in the decision |
| Reciprocity | Takes, does not notice | Thanks and acknowledges | Designs a genuine two-way exchange |
| Ethics | Not considered | Names a trade-off | Names it, decides it deliberately, and stands behind it publicly |
| Action | Talked about it | Did one real thing | Built it into how the venture operates, with a measure |

## Differentiation & access

- **Communities without local businesses:** Partner internally — the school office, another year
  group, a sports club, a library, an online community with supervision. The learning is
  identical.
- **Shy learners making a real ask:** Pairs, a script, and one rehearsal with a Helper. The
  learner may make the ask by written letter and receive full credit.
- **Learners for whom charity framing is uncomfortable** (including those whose families have
  received support): Frame give-back as contribution and exchange, never as charity from a
  position above. Watch the language carefully in this module.
- **Extension (Builder):** Interview a local business owner about who helps them and who they
  help.
- **Extension (Founder):** Write the venture's impact report as if for a stakeholder who opposes
  you.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Tokenism.** A poster saying "we care about the planet" earns nothing. **Green Founder**
  requires a decision that actually changed.
- **Partnership as free stuff.** Teams will ask local businesses for donations. Push them to
  offer something real — publicity, help, a service, a thank-you wall.
- **Adults doing the asking.** The ask is the skill. Your job is to be present, not to speak.
  Brief the partner in advance that the young person will lead.
- **Charity framing that stings.** Some learners are on the receiving end of the goodwill being
  discussed. Language matters more here than anywhere in the program.
- **Ethics as a lecture.** If you tell them the answer, the module is worthless. Use the
  challenge panel; peers are more persuasive than adults on fairness.
- **External contacts without checks.** Any visit, call, or partnership outside the setting
  follows your safeguarding policy — approved adults, no unsupervised contact, no personal
  contact details exchanged. See [05-standards-and-safeguarding.md](../05-standards-and-safeguarding.md).

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Thank a Helper.* Pick a helper at home and thank them properly — what they did and
why it mattered.

**Builder:** *Local Map.* Walk your street or block. List five businesses and what each gives the
community beyond what it sells.

**Founder:** *The Silent Stakeholder.* Pick any product in your house. Who was affected in making
it who never agreed to be? Bring one example.
