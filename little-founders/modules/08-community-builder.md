# Course 8 — Community Builder

**Icon:** Handshake · **Colour:** Sky · **Core badge:** Community Builder · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Course 7 · **Français:** Bâtisseur de Communauté

> A business is a neighbour, not a stranger.

## Big idea

Every business sits inside a community that it takes from and gives to. Founders who see that
clearly build things people defend; founders who do not build things people tolerate until
something better arrives.

## Essential questions

- Who helps my business, and do they know I noticed?
- Who does my business affect who never chose to be affected?
- What would I do differently if the people affected were in the room?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| 🌱 **Little Sprouts** | Name people who help their business, thank them specifically, and choose something kind their business does |
| 🔨 **Junior Builders** | Map who helps and who is affected, and set up one real partnership with a class, staff member, or local business |
| 🚀 **Young Founders** | Analyse stakeholders, negotiate a partnership, and build a measurable give-back into the plan |
| 👑 **Founder CEOs** | Analyse impact including people with no voice, negotiate real terms, and make an ethical trade-off explicitly |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | From tier |
|---|---|---|
| Helper | Someone who makes your business possible | Sprouts |
| Thank you | Saying what someone did and why it mattered | Sprouts |
| Community | The people around us | Sprouts |
| Partner | Someone we work with, both getting something | Builders |
| Stakeholder | Anyone affected by what we do | Builders |
| Give-back | What we return to the community | Founders |
| Terms | What each side agreed to do | Founders |
| Externality | An effect on someone who is not in the deal | CEOs |

## 🌱 Little Sprouts (5–7) — 4 × 45 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Who helps us?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Helper Chain.* Trace a loaf of bread back through everyone who touched it.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Nobody makes anything alone. Even a lemonade stand needs whoever bought the
  lemons, lent the table, and let you stand there.
- **Work (25):** **Helper Web.** Draw your business in the middle with lines out to every helper —
  family, teachers, the shop, whoever gave you cardboard. Target: six.
- **Close (8):** Who is the helper nobody else thought of?
- **Artifact:** Helper web with ≥ 6 helpers.

### S2 · MAKE — Real thank-yous

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Good Thank You.* Compare "thanks" to "thank you for saving the big box — I
  used it for the roof."
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** A real thank-you says **what they did** and **what it made possible**.
- **Work (25):** Three thank-you cards for real helpers in brand colours, each with both parts.
  Deliver at least one in person, with a Helper.
- **Close (8):** How did the person react?
- **Artifact:** Three cards, one delivered in person.

### S3 · SHOW — Kind business

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Fair or Not?* Charging a friend double, keeping the extra change, taking the
  last of a shared material.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Businesses make choices that affect other people. Kind businesses think
  about that before they decide.
- **Work (25):** **The Kind Choice.** Choose one kind thing your business will do — a free item
  for someone who cannot pay, tidying the space, sharing spare materials — and do it this session.
- **Close (8):** Report what you did and who it helped.
- **Artifact:** Kind choice, done.

### S4 · CHANGE — Our promise

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Promises We Keep.*
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Writing a promise down makes it easier to keep.
- **Work (25):** A **Community Promise** poster: one thing we will always do, one we will never
  do, one way we say thank you. Signed by all.
- **Close (8):** Badge check: **Community Builder**, **Kind Business**.
- **Artifact:** Signed Community Promise.

## 🔨 Junior Builders (8–10) — 4 × 60 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Who is affected?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Ripples.* Drop a stone in water; name four rings of people a school fair
  affects.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Three rings — **inside** (team), **close** (customers, suppliers,
  families), **wider** (school, neighbours, the environment). Each ring gets a "what do they give
  us / what do we give them" line.
- **Work (35):** Build the three-ring map with both lines for at least eight people. Mark any
  where the exchange is one-sided.
- **Close (10):** Name your most one-sided relationship. What would fix it?
- **Artifact:** Stakeholder map with give/get lines.

### S2 · MAKE — Prepare the ask

- **Warm-Up (5):** *What's In It For Them?* Pitch a partnership from the other side's point of
  view.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** A partnership is a trade, not a favour. Before you ask, know: what you
  want, what you offer, how long, and what happens if it does not work.
- **Work (35):** Identify three possible partners — another class, the office, a local shop, a
  charity. Write a one-page ask: what we want, what we offer, dates, fallback. Rehearse in pairs.
- **Close (10):** Best ask of the day, chosen by the room.
- **Artifact:** One-page partnership ask + rehearsal.

### S3 · SHOW — Make the ask

- **Warm-Up (5):** *No Is Fine.* Practise hearing "no" and asking one follow-up question.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Introduce, explain in two sentences, make the ask, listen, agree the next
  step in writing.
- **Work (35):** Every team makes at least one real ask — a visit to a local business with a
  Helper, an appointment with a staff member, a call with the Guide present. Record outcomes
  including refusals.
- **Close (10):** Count the room's asks and yeses. Normalise the ratio.
- **Artifact:** Ask made + outcome recorded.

### S4 · CHANGE — Give-back

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Where Does the Money Go?* Revisit the Course 3 split rule.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Give-back can be money, time, product, or skill. It works best when it is
  connected to what your business already does.
- **Work (35):** Design the Course 9 give-back: what, to whom, how much. Get it agreed by the
  whole team. Update the map with the partnership agreed.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Community Builder**, **Deal Maker**, **Kind Business**, **Green
  Founder**.
- **Artifact:** Give-back plan + updated map + agreed partnership.

## 🚀 Young Founders (11–13) — 4 × 75 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Stakeholders and power

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Who Is Not in the Room?* For a delivery app, list everyone affected who never
  agreed to anything.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** The three rings, then a simple power/interest split: who is affected a lot
  but has little say? That is where ethics actually lives.
- **Work (42):** Full stakeholder map plus a power/interest grid. For the two highest-impact,
  lowest-power stakeholders, write what the effect is and what you will do about it. Include the
  environment as a stakeholder with no voice.
- **Close (13):** Read one effect you had not previously considered.
- **Artifact:** Stakeholder map + power/interest grid + two effect statements.

### S2 · MAKE — Prepare a real negotiation

- **Warm-Up (8):** *What's In It For Them?*
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Interests behind positions. Trade what is cheap for you and valuable to
  them. Then terms: scope, dates, responsibilities, exit.
- **Work (42):** Identify three partners, research what each actually wants, and draft a one-page
  agreement with terms already filled in. Rehearse the ask with a Helper playing hard-to-get.
- **Close (13):** Read the term you expect to have to give away.
- **Artifact:** Drafted one-page agreement + rehearsal.

### S3 · SHOW — Negotiate it

- **Warm-Up (8):** *No Is Fine.*
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Making the ask in person, agreeing terms in writing, and knowing your
  fallback before you walk in. An adult is present for every external commitment.
- **Work (42):** Make real asks. Where a partner says yes, agree and sign the one-page agreement
  on the spot. Where they say no, ask one question about why and record it.
- **Close (13):** Read one term you traded away and one you held.
- **Artifact:** Signed agreement, or a recorded refusal with the reason.

### S4 · CHANGE — Measurable give-back

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Tokenism Test.* Which of these give-backs is real? Five examples, two of them
  hollow.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** A real give-back is connected to the business, measurable, and
  sustainable if you did it every month. Then how you will report it at Demo Day.
- **Work (42):** Write the community plan for Course 9: partnership terms, give-back with a
  measurable target, ethical lines you will not cross, and the reporting method.
- **Close (13):** Badge check: **Community Builder**, **Deal Maker**, **Kind Business**, **Green
  Founder**.
- **Artifact:** One-page community plan.

## 👑 Founder CEOs (14–17) — 4 × 90 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Impact, including the silent ones

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Who Is Not in the Room?*
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Externalities — costs and benefits landing on people outside the
  transaction. Then the power/interest grid; the low-power, high-impact quadrant is what ethics is
  about.
- **Work (50):** Full stakeholder analysis with the grid. For the three highest-impact,
  lowest-power stakeholders, write the effect and the response. Environmental impact is a required
  entry.
- **Close (15):** Read one externality you had not considered.
- **Artifact:** Stakeholder analysis + externality statements.

### S2 · MAKE — Ethical trade-offs, named

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Cheaper or Kinder?* A supplier is 30% cheaper with worse conditions. Argue
  both sides, vote, then argue the side you did not pick.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Most business ethics are trade-offs, not villainy. Name the trade-off,
  decide deliberately, and be able to say it out loud to the affected party. The test: *would I be
  comfortable if they read this decision record?*
- **Work (50):** Surface two real trade-offs in your own venture — pricing, sourcing, marketing
  claims, use of unpaid help. Decide each with a written record. Apply the read-it-aloud test.
  Cross-team challenge panel on one decision each.
- **Close (15):** Name one decision you changed after the challenge.
- **Artifact:** Two ethical decision records.

### S3 · SHOW — Negotiate real terms

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Your Fallback.* What is your best option if this deal falls through? Knowing
  it is what stops a bad deal.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Interests behind positions; trading cheap-for-you, valuable-to-them; terms
  in writing — scope, dates, responsibilities, exit.
- **Work (50):** Approach and negotiate a real partnership — a local business, a charity, a
  supplier, a Market Day venue, another cohort. Terms written into a one-page agreement signed by
  both sides, adult present.
- **Close (15):** Read one term you traded away and one you held.
- **Artifact:** Signed one-page partnership agreement.

### S4 · CHANGE — Community as strategy

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Defensible or Tolerated?* Would your community miss you if you vanished
  tomorrow?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Trust lowers your cost of getting customers; partners give you reach you
  cannot buy. Then the give-back: connected, measurable, sustainable at scale.
- **Work (50):** Write the **community plan** for Course 9: stakeholders, partnership terms,
  give-back with a measurable target, ethical lines, and how impact will be reported at Demo Day.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Community Builder**, **Deal Maker**, **Kind Business**, **Green
  Founder**.
- **Artifact:** One-page community plan.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Community Builder** (core) | Mapped who is affected and took a real action benefiting someone outside their team | 25 |
| **Deal Maker** | Agreed a real partnership or trade where both sides stated the terms and called it fair | 25 |
| **Kind Business** | Made a decision that cost them something in order to be fair to someone else, and can explain the trade-off | 25 |
| **Green Founder** | Identified an environmental impact and changed a decision because of it | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the course).

**Rubric — community:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Sees only customers | Maps direct stakeholders | Identifies affected people with no voice in the decision |
| Reciprocity | Takes, does not notice | Thanks and acknowledges | Designs a genuine two-way exchange |
| Ethics | Not considered | Names a trade-off | Names it, decides deliberately, stands behind it publicly |
| Action | Talked about it | Did one real thing | Built it into how the venture operates, with a measure |

## Differentiation & access

- **Communities without local businesses:** Partner internally — the office, another year group, a
  sports club, a library, a supervised online community. The learning is identical.
- **Shy learners making a real ask:** Pairs, a script, and one rehearsal with a Helper. A written
  ask by letter earns full credit.
- **Learners for whom charity framing stings** — including those whose families have received
  support: frame give-back as contribution and exchange, never as charity from a position above.
  Language matters more here than anywhere in the program.
- **Bilingual settings:** Approach francophone and anglophone partners in their own language. This
  is often the tier's most successful lesson in customer focus.
- **Extension (Builders):** Interview a local business owner about who helps them and who they
  help.
- **Extension (CEOs):** Write the venture's impact report as if for a stakeholder who opposes you.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Tokenism.** A poster saying "we care about the planet" earns nothing. **Green Founder**
  requires a decision that actually changed.
- **Partnership as free stuff.** Teams will ask local businesses for donations. Push them to offer
  something real — publicity, help, a service, a thank-you wall.
- **Adults doing the asking.** The ask is the skill. Be present, not vocal. Brief the partner in
  advance that the young person will lead.
- **Charity framing that stings.** Watch the language.
- **Ethics as a lecture.** If you tell them the answer, the course is worthless. Use the challenge
  panel; peers are more persuasive than adults on fairness.
- **External contacts without checks.** Any visit, call, or partnership outside the setting follows
  your safeguarding policy — approved adults, no unsupervised contact, no personal contact details
  exchanged. See [05-standards-and-safeguarding.md](../05-standards-and-safeguarding.md).

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**🌱 Sprouts:** *Thank a Helper.* Pick a helper at home and thank them properly — what they did
and why it mattered.

**🔨 Builders:** *Local Map.* Walk your street. List five businesses and what each gives the
community beyond what it sells.

**🚀 Young Founders:** *The Ask, Rehearsed.* Practise your partnership ask on someone at home. Ask
them to say no once so you can practise the follow-up.

**👑 Founder CEOs:** *The Silent Stakeholder.* Pick any product in your house. Who was affected in
making it who never agreed to be?
