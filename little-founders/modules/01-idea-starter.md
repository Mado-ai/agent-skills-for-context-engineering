# Course 1 — Idea Starter

**Icon:** Lightbulb · **Colour:** Sunshine · **Core badge:** Big Ideas · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** none · **Français:** Lance-Idées

> Big ideas start young — and they start with small annoyances.

## Big idea

Ideas are not lightning strikes. They are what happens when you pay attention to something that
bugs people. Anyone who looks carefully can find one.

## Essential questions

- What bugs you? What bugs the people around you?
- Whose problem is it, really?
- What is the smallest thing I could make to find out if I am right?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| 🌱 **Little Sprouts** | Name three things that are annoying or hard; draw one way to fix one; say who it would help |
| 🔨 **Junior Builders** | Run a problem hunt, collect 10+ problems, choose one with a stated reason, and name three people who have it |
| 🚀 **Young Founders** | Interview eight people, write a problem statement backed by two quotes, and show which parts are still guesses |
| 👑 **Founder CEOs** | Interview strangers, separate the problem from their favourite solution, and make a persevere/pivot/park decision on evidence |

## Vocabulary

Concept first, word second — introduce each term only after learners have done the thing.

| Term | Plain meaning | From tier |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Something that is harder than it should be | Sprouts |
| Idea | A guess about how to fix a problem | Sprouts |
| Customer | The person the fix is for | Sprouts |
| Prototype | The rough first version | Builders |
| Feedback | What someone tells you after they try it | Builders |
| Validation | Checking whether people really want it | Founders |
| Assumption | Something you believe but have not checked | Founders |
| Problem statement | One sentence: who, what, why it matters | CEOs |
| Signal vs. noise | What people *did* vs. what they politely said | CEOs |

## 🌱 Little Sprouts (5–7) — 4 × 45 min

### S1 · NOTICE — "What bugs you?"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Grumble Circle.* Everyone names one annoying thing. Guide writes each on a
  Sunshine card. "My shoelaces come undone" is a perfect answer.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Hold up a knotted shoelace and a tangled cord. Show velcro shoes and a
  cord clip. *Someone noticed, then someone made.*
- **Work (25):** **Bug Hunt.** In pairs with a Helper, walk the room with a clipboard of ten
  empty boxes and draw everything annoying, broken, slow, or boring. Target: five drawings.
- **Close (8):** Pin your loudest bug to the Idea Wall.
- **Artifact:** Bug Hunt sheet, ≥ 3 drawings.

### S2 · MAKE — "Draw the fix"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Silly Fixes.* Guide names a problem; learners shout the wildest fix.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** "An idea is a guess. Guesses can be rough." Napkin scribble next to a
  finished object.
- **Work (25):** **My Big Idea page** — what it is, who uses it, what happens when they use it.
  Helpers scribe one spoken sentence in the learner's own words.
- **Close (8):** Two volunteers show. One clap for effort, not quality.
- **Artifact:** My Big Idea page with scribed sentence.

### S3 · SHOW — "Ask someone"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Face Cards.* Practise the three feedback faces: 😀 I like it, 🤔 I wonder,
  💡 What if.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Model it badly, then well. A Helper says "it's bad." Was that helpful?
  Redo with the faces.
- **Work (25):** **Idea Swap.** Rotating pairs, 4 minutes each, three rotations. Stickies go on
  the back of your page.
- **Close (8):** "Hands up if someone said something that surprised you."
- **Artifact:** ≥ 3 feedback stickies.

### S4 · CHANGE — "Make it better"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Before and After.* Two versions of a familiar toy.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** "Changing your idea is not losing. It means you listened."
- **Work (25):** **Version 2.** Redraw with one change from feedback. Circle the change in
  Energy orange and tell a Helper why.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Big Ideas**. Idea Fair invitations home.
- **Artifact:** Version 2 with the change circled and explained.

## 🔨 Junior Builders (8–10) — 4 × 60 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Problem hunt

- **Warm-Up (5):** *50 Problems in 5 Minutes.* One shared list, no judging. The good ones start
  around item 25.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Problems vs. solutions. "The bus is always late" = problem. "We need an
  app" = a solution wearing a costume.
- **Work (35):** **Problem Hunt** in three zones (classroom, corridor, home-from-memory). Log
  10+ problems with three columns: *what · who it bothers · how often.* Teams of 3.
- **Close (10):** Top three per team on the Idea Wall.
- **Artifact:** Problem Hunt sheet, ≥ 10 entries.

### S2 · MAKE — Choose and build rough

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Rank It.* Rare-but-awful, or small-but-constant? Make them argue.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The choosing rule — **Real** (someone has it), **Often** (more than
  once), **Mine** (I care enough to work on it).
- **Work (35):** Write it as *"[Who] struggles to [do what] because [why]"*, then build a
  cardboard prototype in 20 minutes. Hard stop at 20 — rough is the point.
- **Close (10):** Prototype parade, 20 seconds each.
- **Artifact:** Problem sentence + photographed prototype.

### S3 · SHOW — Real reactions

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Bad Question / Good Question.* "Do you like it?" vs. "What would you use
  this for?" Sort ten questions.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** How to shut up. After you ask, count to three before speaking again.
- **Work (35):** **Reaction Round.** Take prototypes to another class or to staff. Five people
  minimum. One asks, one writes, one watches faces. Rotate.
- **Close (10):** Sort feedback into *keep / change / confused* on the Wonder Wall.
- **Artifact:** Feedback log, ≥ 5 respondents.

### S4 · CHANGE — Version 2 and the decision

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Kill Your Darling.* Name the feature you love that nobody mentioned.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Iteration. Three versions of a real product — a chair, a controller, a
  water bottle.
- **Work (35):** Rebuild with two changes driven by feedback. Then a written decision: continue,
  change direction, or pick a new problem — with the reason.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Big Ideas**, **Problem Solver**, **Customer Listener**.
- **Artifact:** Version 2 + written decision.

## 🚀 Young Founders (11–13) — 4 × 75 min

Young Founders do not build first. This tier's whole job is proving somebody wants it before
committing effort.

### S1 · NOTICE — Ask before you assume

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Assumption Audit.* Write the business idea you already have, then three
  things you are assuming are true about it.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Ask about their life, not your idea. Ask about the past, not the future.
  Ask for specifics, not opinions. Show a bad interview and a good one, acted out.
- **Work (42):** Write a five-question script — all open, none mentioning your idea. Peer-test
  it. Then run two live interviews inside the building.
- **Close (13):** Report one surprising quote, verbatim, onto the Wonder Wall. No paraphrasing.
- **Artifact:** Interview script + 2 interview notes.
- **Between sessions:** six more interviews, at least two outside school. Target: eight total.

### S2 · MAKE — The problem statement

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Quote Sort.* Pool every quote; cluster on the wall. Which problems appeared
  more than twice?
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** *[Specific person] needs a way to [do something] because [insight], but
  today [what fails].* Three good examples, two vague ones.
- **Work (42):** Write and pressure-test the statement in pairs. Mark each part **known** (a
  quote backs it) or **guess**. Then build the smallest thing that could get a reaction — a
  paper mockup, a sign-up sheet, a service run once.
- **Close (13):** State your riskiest remaining guess out loud.
- **Artifact:** Problem statement with known/guess marking, backed by ≥ 2 quotes.

### S3 · SHOW — Test the guess

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Defence Drill.* Partner attacks your idea for 60 seconds; you may only say
  "tell me more."
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Politeness is not evidence. Set your pass number *before* you test, or
  you will move the goalposts afterwards.
- **Work (42):** Test with at least eight people outside the team. Record what they *did* —
  signed up, asked when, offered money, gave a contact — not only what they said.
- **Close (13):** Post your number next to the threshold you wrote down.
- **Artifact:** Test log with a pre-stated threshold.

### S4 · CHANGE — Validated or not

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Pivot Stories.* Two short real examples of companies that changed direction.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Validated, not validated, or not yet tested — three honest answers. Not
  validated is a result, not a failure, and earns the same points.
- **Work (42):** Write a one-page **Validation Brief**: problem statement, what you tested, the
  number, what changed, and what you would need to know to be sure. Then decide.
- **Close (13):** Two-minute briefs to the room. Badge check: **Big Ideas**, **Problem Solver**,
  **Customer Listener**, **Idea Lab**.
- **Artifact:** One-page Validation Brief.

## 👑 Founder CEOs (14–17) — 4 × 90 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Problem interviews with strangers

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Assumption Audit*, then rank the assumptions by how badly you would be hurt
  if each were wrong.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Why founders build the wrong thing. Interview technique, then the three
  traps: leading questions, friends as subjects, and hearing what you came to hear.
- **Work (50):** Write and peer-test the script. Run two live interviews, ideally with people
  outside the cohort. One learner observes and scores the interviewer on leading questions.
- **Close (15):** Read the quote that most damaged your original idea.
- **Artifact:** Script + 2 scored interviews.
- **Between sessions:** three more, all with strangers.

### S2 · MAKE — Statement and testable version

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Quote Sort*, then remove every quote that came from someone who knows you.
  What survives?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Writing the statement; then the difference between a prototype (tests
  whether it works) and a demand test (tests whether anyone wants it). You need the second first.
- **Work (50):** Write the statement, then build the smallest demand test — landing page,
  pre-order sheet, service delivered once by hand, or ten cold asks. Time-boxed to 30 minutes.
- **Close (15):** State the riskiest assumption and the test that would break it.
- **Artifact:** Problem statement + a demand test ready to run.

### S3 · SHOW — Signal vs. noise

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Defence Drill*, then swap and attack.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Politeness is noise. Behaviour is signal — did they ask for it, reach for
  it, offer to pay, give you a contact, come back? Rank the evidence types.
- **Work (50):** Run the test with at least five strangers. Record behaviour, not sentiment.
  Rate each piece of evidence: strong signal / weak signal / noise.
- **Close (15):** Report your strongest single piece of evidence and your weakest.
- **Artifact:** Test log with signal ratings.

### S4 · CHANGE — Evidence-based decision

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Sunk Cost.* Two scenarios where the honest answer is to stop.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Persevere / pivot / park. Parking an idea with a good reason is a
  success, and earns the same points as continuing.
- **Work (50):** Write a one-page **Idea Brief**: problem statement, evidence summary, what
  changed after testing, decision, and what you would need to know to be sure.
- **Close (15):** Two-minute briefs. Badge check: **Big Ideas**, **Problem Solver**, **Customer
  Listener**, **Idea Lab**, **Prototype Pro** where three versions exist.
- **Artifact:** One-page Idea Brief.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Big Ideas** (core) | All four artifacts complete; can state their problem and who has it | 25 |
| **Problem Solver** | Changed their idea in response to feedback, and can say which feedback caused it | 25 |
| **Customer Listener** | Feedback from ≥ 3 (Sprouts) / 5 (Builders) / 8 (Founders) / 5 strangers (CEOs) using open questions | 25 |
| **Idea Lab** | Founders and CEOs — ran a real test where the outcome could have proved them wrong | 25 |
| **Idea Machine** | 50 problems or ideas logged cumulatively on the Idea Wall (may complete later) | 25 |
| **Prototype Pro** | Three distinct rough versions, each changed by what the last one taught | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the course).

**Rubric — the course artifact:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Vague, or is really a solution | Names a problem and who has it | Specific person, specific moment, evidenced |
| Evidence | None | Asked people, recorded some | Multiple sources; distinguishes said from did |
| Change | No change, or unexplained | One change, reason given | Changes traced to specific feedback; darlings killed |
| Voice | Cannot explain unaided | Explains with prompts | Explains clearly and unprompted, including what is uncertain |

## Differentiation & access

- **Non-writers, early readers, EAL, French-first:** Every written artifact may be a drawing plus
  a scribed or recorded sentence, in either language. Full credit, every tier.
- **Learners who freeze on "think of an idea":** Do not ask for ideas. Ask what annoyed them this
  morning. The Bug Hunt exists for exactly this learner.
- **Learners who arrive with a fixed idea:** Common from Young Founders up. Require the
  interviews before any building; the Assumption Audit is designed to create the crack.
- **Sensory needs:** The Bug Hunt works from a window or from photographs.
- **Extension:** Run a second problem hunt in a completely different setting — a shop, a bus stop,
  a rink — and compare which problems appear in both.

## Guide watch-outs

- **The 25-minute mini-lesson.** The most common way this course fails. If you are still talking
  a third of the way in, stop mid-sentence and start the Work.
- **Rescuing the prototype.** A Helper who "just tidies it up" removes the learner's ownership
  and the failure that Session 4 needs.
- **Praising the idea, not the noticing.** Say "that's a sharp thing to notice" far more often
  than "great idea." Ideas are cheap; attention is the taught skill.
- **Polite feedback.** If every team hears "it's great," the protocol has collapsed. Re-run Bad
  Question / Good Question and insist on *I wonder*.
- **Sprouts copying each other.** Expected and fine at 5–7. Do not police it; ask the copier what
  *their* version does differently and accept a small answer.
- **Young Founders building before validating.** The single biggest tier-confusion. If a team has
  a beautiful prototype and two interviews, send them back to interviews.
- **Founder CEOs interviewing friends.** Friends confirm everything. Strangers only, or the
  evidence does not count.

## Materials

Sunshine and Sky index cards · clipboards · markers · sticky notes (3 colours) · cardboard,
paper, tape, string, foil · Bug Hunt sheets (Sprouts) · Problem Hunt sheets (Builders) ·
interview script templates (Founders, CEOs) · validation brief template · camera or tablet ·
Idea Wall space.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**🌱 Sprouts:** *Three Bugs at Dinner.* Everyone names one annoying thing from today. Draw the
best one.

**🔨 Builders:** *Problem Walk.* Ten minutes anywhere with a grown-up. Count problems out loud.
Bring back the top three.

**🚀 Young Founders:** *Two Interviews.* Ask two adults about the most annoying part of their
week. Five open questions each. No pitching. Bring verbatim quotes.

**👑 Founder CEOs:** *The Stranger Interview.* Interview someone you do not know well, with
permission and a grown-up nearby. Bring one quote that surprised you.

## Family note (after Session 4)

> This month your child learned that good ideas come from noticing problems, not from waiting for
> inspiration. They found a problem, made a rough version of a fix, showed it to real people, and
> — this is the part we care about — changed it based on what they heard.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What did someone tell you about your idea that surprised you?"*
>
> If they say "nothing," that is worth following up — it usually means the feedback was polite
> rather than useful, and noticing that together is a great next step.
