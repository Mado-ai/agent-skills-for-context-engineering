# Module 1 — Idea Starter

**Icon:** Lightbulb · **Colour:** Sunshine · **Core badge:** Big Ideas · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** none · **Unlocks:** counts toward Leadership Lab

> Big ideas start with small annoyances.

## Big idea

Ideas are not lightning strikes. They are what happens when you pay attention to something
that bugs people. Anyone who looks carefully can find one.

## Essential questions

- What bugs you? What bugs the people around you?
- Whose problem is it, really?
- What is the smallest thing I could make to find out if I am right?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Name three things that are annoying or hard; draw one way to fix one of them; say who it would help |
| **Builder** | Run a problem hunt, collect 10+ problems, choose one using a stated reason, and name three people who have it |
| **Founder** | Interview five people they do not know well, write a one-sentence problem statement with evidence, and separate the problem from their favourite solution |

## Vocabulary

Concept first, word second — introduce each term only after learners have done the thing.

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Something that is harder than it should be | Spark |
| Idea | A guess about how to fix a problem | Spark |
| Customer | The person the fix is for | Spark |
| Prototype | The rough first version | Builder |
| Feedback | What someone tells you after they try it | Builder |
| Assumption | Something you believe but have not checked | Founder |
| Problem statement | One sentence: who, what, why it matters | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — "What bugs you?"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Grumble Circle.* Everyone says one thing that is annoying. Guide writes each
  on a Sunshine card. No idea is too small; "my shoelaces come undone" is a perfect answer.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Guide holds up two cards — a knotted shoelace and a tangled headphone
  cord. "Somebody noticed this and made a fix." Show velcro shoes and a cord clip. *Someone
  noticed, then someone made.*
- **Work (25):** **Bug Hunt.** In pairs with a Helper, learners walk the room/playground with a
  clipboard of ten empty boxes and draw everything they see that is annoying, broken, slow, or
  boring. Target: five drawings each.
- **Close (8):** Each learner picks their loudest bug and pins it to the Idea Wall.
- **Artifact:** Bug Hunt sheet with ≥ 3 drawings.

### Session 2 · MAKE — "Draw the fix"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Silly Fixes.* Guide names a problem; learners shout the wildest fix. Laugh
  at all of them, write down two.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** "An idea is a guess. Guesses can be rough." Show a scribbled napkin
  drawing next to a finished object.
- **Work (25):** **My Big Idea page.** Learners draw their fix, large, on a single page: what it
  is, who uses it, what happens when they use it. Helpers scribe one spoken sentence at the
  bottom, in the learner's own words.
- **Close (8):** Two volunteers show; everyone claps once for effort, not quality.
- **Artifact:** *My Big Idea* page, scribed sentence attached.

### Session 3 · SHOW — "Ask someone"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Face Cards.* Practise the three feedback faces: 😀 I like it, 🤔 I wonder,
  💡 What if. Everyone holds up each one.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Model it badly, then well. Guide shows an idea, a Helper says "it's bad."
  Ask: was that helpful? Redo with the three faces.
- **Work (25):** **Idea Swap.** Rotating pairs, 4 minutes each. Show your page, listen to three
  faces, put the sticky notes on the back of your page. Three rotations.
- **Close (8):** "Hands up if someone said something that surprised you."
- **Artifact:** ≥ 3 feedback stickies on the back of the idea page.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — "Make it better"

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Before and After.* Show two versions of a familiar toy.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** "Changing your idea is not losing. It means you listened."
- **Work (25):** **Version 2.** Redraw the idea with one change that came from feedback. Learners
  circle the change in Energy orange and tell a Helper why.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Big Ideas**. Points logged. Idea Fair invitations go home.
- **Artifact:** Version 2 drawing with the change circled and explained.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Problem hunt

- **Warm-Up (5):** *100 Problems in 5 Minutes.* Whole group, one shared list, no judging. Get
  past the obvious ones — the good stuff starts around item 40.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Problems vs. solutions. Guide reads statements; learners call out which
  is which. "The bus is always late" = problem. "We need an app" = solution wearing a costume.
- **Work (35):** **Problem Hunt** in three zones (classroom, corridor/yard, home-from-memory).
  Log at least 10 problems on the Problem Hunt sheet with three columns: *what*, *who it
  bothers*, *how often*. Teams of 3.
- **Close (10):** Each team posts their top three on the Idea Wall.
- **Artifact:** Problem Hunt sheet, ≥ 10 entries.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Choose and build rough

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Rank It.* Which is worth solving: rare-but-awful, or small-but-constant?
  No right answer; make them argue.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The three-part choosing rule — **Real** (someone actually has it),
  **Often** (happens more than once), **Mine** (I care enough to work on it).
- **Work (35):** Teams choose one problem, write it as *"[Who] struggles to [do what] because
  [why]"*, then build a cardboard/paper prototype in 20 minutes. Hard stop at 20 — the point is
  rough.
- **Close (10):** Prototype parade, 20 seconds each.
- **Artifact:** Problem sentence + photographed prototype.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Get real reactions

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Bad Question / Good Question.* "Do you like it?" vs. "What would you use
  this for?" Learners sort ten questions into open and closed.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** How to shut up. The three-second rule: after you ask, count to three
  before speaking again.
- **Work (35):** **Reaction Round.** Teams take prototypes to another class or to staff. Five
  people minimum. One member asks, one writes, one watches faces. Rotate roles.
- **Close (10):** Sort the feedback into *keep / change / confused* on the Wonder Wall.
- **Artifact:** Feedback log, ≥ 5 respondents.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Version 2 and the decision

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Kill Your Darling.* Everyone names one feature of their prototype they love
  and nobody mentioned.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Iteration. Show three versions of a real product (a chair, a game
  controller, a water bottle).
- **Work (35):** Rebuild with two changes driven by feedback. Then a written decision:
  *continue*, *change direction*, or *pick a new problem* — with the reason.
- **Close (10):** Badge check for **Big Ideas** and, where earned, **Problem Solver** and
  **Customer Listener**.
- **Artifact:** Version 2 prototype + written decision with reason.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Problem interviews

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Assumption Audit.* Each learner writes a business idea they already have,
  then lists three things they are assuming are true about it.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Why founders build the wrong thing. The Mom Test in brief: ask about
  their life, not your idea; ask about the past, not the future; ask for specifics, not
  opinions.
- **Work (50):** Write an interview script (5 questions, all open, none mentioning your idea).
  Peer-test the script. Then conduct two live interviews inside the building.
- **Close (15):** Report one surprising quote. Post it on the Wonder Wall verbatim — no
  paraphrasing.
- **Artifact:** Interview script + 2 interview notes.
- **Between sessions:** three more interviews, at least two with people outside school.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Problem statement and a testable version

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Quote Sort.* Pool all interview quotes; cluster them on the wall. Which
  problems appeared more than twice?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Writing a problem statement: *[specific person] needs a way to [do
  something] because [insight], but today [what fails].* Show three good and two vague examples.
- **Work (50):** Write and pressure-test the statement in pairs; then build the smallest thing
  that could get a reaction — paper mockup, a script, a service run once, a landing page.
  Time-boxed to 30 minutes.
- **Close (15):** Each learner states their riskiest assumption out loud.
- **Artifact:** Problem statement backed by ≥ 2 quotes; rough testable version.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Test with strangers

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Defence Drill.* Practise hearing criticism without explaining. Partner
  attacks the idea for 60 seconds; you may only say "tell me more."
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Signal vs. noise. Politeness is noise. Behaviour is signal — did they
  ask for it, reach for it, offer to pay, give you a contact?
- **Work (50):** Test with at least five people outside the cohort. Record what they *did*, not
  only what they said.
- **Close (15):** Rate each piece of evidence: strong signal / weak signal / noise.
- **Artifact:** Test log with signal ratings.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Evidence-based decision

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Pivot Stories.* Two short real examples of companies that changed direction.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Persevere / pivot / park. Parking an idea with a good reason is a
  success, and earns the same points.
- **Work (50):** Write a one-page **Idea Brief**: problem statement, evidence summary, what
  changed after testing, decision, and what you would need to know to be sure.
- **Close (15):** Two-minute briefs to the room. Badge check: **Big Ideas**, **Problem Solver**,
  **Customer Listener**, and **Idea Lab** where a real test was run.
- **Artifact:** One-page Idea Brief.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Big Ideas** (core) | Completed all four artifacts; can state their problem and who has it | 25 |
| **Problem Solver** | Changed their idea in response to feedback, and can say which feedback caused it | 25 |
| **Customer Listener** | Collected feedback from ≥ 3 (Spark) / 5 (Builder) / 5 external (Founder) people using open questions | 25 |
| **Idea Machine** | Logged 50 problems or ideas cumulatively on the Idea Wall (may complete in a later module) | 25 |
| **Idea Lab** | Founder only — ran a real test where the outcome could have proved them wrong | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**Rubric for the module artifact** (Version 2 / Idea Brief):

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Vague or is really a solution | Names a problem and who has it | Specific person, specific moment, evidenced |
| Evidence | None | Asked people, recorded some | Multiple sources, distinguishes said from did |
| Change | No change, or unexplained | One change, reason given | Changes traced to specific feedback; darlings killed |
| Voice | Cannot explain unaided | Explains with prompts | Explains clearly and unprompted, including what is uncertain |

## Differentiation & access

- **Non-writers / EAL:** Every written artifact may be a drawing plus a scribed or recorded
  sentence. This is a full-credit route at every tier, not a fallback.
- **Learners who freeze on "think of an idea":** Do not ask for ideas. Ask what annoyed them
  this morning. The Bug Hunt exists for exactly this learner.
- **Learners who arrive with a fixed idea:** Common at Founder tier. Require the interviews
  before any building; the Assumption Audit is designed to create the crack.
- **Sensory needs:** The Bug Hunt can be done from a window or from photographs.
- **Extension:** Run a second problem hunt in a completely different setting (a shop, a bus
  stop) and compare which problems appear in both.

## Guide watch-outs

- **The 25-minute mini-lesson.** The most common way this module fails. If you are still
  talking when a third of the session is gone, stop mid-sentence and start the Work.
- **Rescuing the prototype.** A Helper who "just tidies it up" removes the learner's ownership
  and, worse, removes the failure that Session 4 needs.
- **Praising the idea, not the noticing.** Say "that's a sharp thing to notice" far more often
  than "great idea." Ideas are cheap; attention is the taught skill.
- **Letting feedback be polite.** If every team hears "it's great," your feedback protocol has
  collapsed. Re-run the Bad Question / Good Question warm-up and insist on *I wonder*.
- **Spark learners copying each other.** Expected and fine at 6–8. Do not police it; ask the
  copier what *their* version does differently and accept a small answer.
- **Founder learners interviewing friends.** Friends confirm everything. Require two interviews
  outside the cohort or the evidence does not count.

## Materials

Sunshine and Sky index cards · clipboards · markers · sticky notes (3 colours) · cardboard,
paper, tape, string, foil · Bug Hunt sheets (Spark) · Problem Hunt sheets (Builder) ·
interview script template (Founder) · camera or tablet for prototype photos · Idea Wall space.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Three Bugs at Dinner.* Everyone at the table names one thing that was annoying
today. The child draws the best one.

**Builder:** *Problem Walk.* Walk anywhere for ten minutes with a grown-up. Count problems out
loud. Bring back the top three.

**Founder:** *One Interview.* Interview an adult about the most annoying part of their week.
Five open questions. No pitching your idea. Bring one verbatim quote.

## Family note (send after Session 4)

> This month your child learned that good ideas come from noticing problems, not from waiting
> for inspiration. They found a problem, made a rough version of a fix, showed it to real
> people, and — this is the part we care about — changed it based on what they heard.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What did someone tell you about your idea that surprised you?"*
>
> If they say "nothing," that's worth a follow-up — it usually means the feedback was polite
> rather than useful, and that is a great thing to notice together.
