# Module 6 — Leadership Lab

**Icon:** People · **Colour:** Sky · **Core badge:** Future Leader · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Modules 1–5 complete **and** 250 Little Points · **Unlocks:** Module 7

> Leaders make the people around them stronger.

## Big idea

Leadership is not being in charge. It is making sure the work gets done, the team stays whole,
and everyone knows what they are doing — including when people disagree. It is a set of
behaviours that can be practised, not a personality you either have or do not.

## Essential questions

- What does a team need from a leader that it cannot give itself?
- What do I do when two people I like want opposite things?
- How do I ask for help without feeling small?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Take a job on a team, do it, say kind and true things to teammates, and let someone else have a turn |
| **Builder** | Hold a defined role, run a 5-minute stand-up, help write a team agreement, and use it when something goes wrong |
| **Founder** | Delegate work with a clear handover, run a decision when the team disagrees, give hard feedback kindly, and name their own leadership weakness |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Team | People working on the same thing | Spark |
| Job / role | The part that is yours | Spark |
| Turn-taking | Everyone gets their go | Spark |
| Team agreement | Rules we wrote together | Builder |
| Stand-up | A short daily check-in | Builder |
| Delegation | Giving work away properly | Founder |
| Accountability | Being the one it comes back to | Founder |
| Consent decision | Nobody objects strongly, so we go | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Everybody has a job

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Human Machine.* The group builds a machine where each learner is one moving
  part. Stop when one part quits — what happened?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Teams work when everyone knows their job. Show four job cards: **Maker**,
  **Teller**, **Timekeeper**, **Tidier**.
- **Work (25):** **Job Cards.** Teams of four build a paper tower with assigned job cards, then
  swap all cards and build again. Which round felt better, and why?
- **Close (8):** Which job would you like next time?
- **Artifact:** Job card held + tower photo.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Kind and true

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Compliment Ball.* Throw a soft ball; say one true good thing about the
  catcher's work — not their appearance.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Kind words that are also true are the useful ones. "Nice" is kind. "You
  kept trying when the tower fell" is kind *and* true.
- **Work (25):** **Star Notes.** Each learner writes or draws a star note for every teammate,
  naming one true thing they did. Notes are delivered and read aloud.
- **Close (8):** Notes go into the workbook. Nobody leaves without at least three.
- **Artifact:** Star notes received and given.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Turn-taking under pressure

- **Warm-Up (5):** *One Pencil.* Four learners, one pencil, one drawing to finish.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** When everyone wants the same thing: take turns, share, or swap. Three
  hand signals for the three options.
- **Work (25):** **The Squeeze.** Teams complete a build with deliberately scarce materials —
  one pair of scissors, three pieces of tape. Helpers observe and record who used which
  strategy, without intervening unless someone is upset.
- **Close (8):** Helpers report what they saw. Name the good moves.
- **Artifact:** Observed strategy noted by a Helper.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Our team rules

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Rules That Work.* Which class rules actually help?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Teams that make their own rules follow them.
- **Work (25):** Each team writes four picture rules on a poster and everyone signs with a
  handprint or name.
- **Close (8):** Badge check: **Future Leader**, **Team Player**, **Helping Hand**.
- **Artifact:** Signed team rules poster.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Roles that mean something

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Who Did What?* Recall the last team task; list who did what. The gaps are
  the lesson.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Four roles with real duties — **Lead** (holds the goal, unblocks),
  **Maker** (builds), **Money** (tracks spend), **Voice** (talks to customers). Every role has
  one thing only they may decide.
- **Work (35):** Assign roles by discussion, not by who shouts. Each learner writes their own
  role card: *what I decide · what I do each session · what I need from others.* Then run one
  20-minute task with roles live.
- **Close (10):** Anyone whose role card was wrong in practice rewrites one line.
- **Artifact:** Role card + observed role in action.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Stand-ups and the team agreement

- **Warm-Up (5):** *60-Second Update.* Practise: what I did, what I'm doing, what's in my way.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The stand-up is five minutes, standing, no problem-solving — blockers get
  named and taken away for later. Then the team agreement: how we decide, how we disagree, what
  we do when someone does not do their job, how we split the money.
- **Work (35):** Run a real stand-up (Lead facilitates, Guide times). Then draft the team
  agreement in the team's own words, including a written consequence they choose themselves.
- **Close (10):** Agreements read aloud; every member signs.
- **Artifact:** Signed team agreement + first stand-up run.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Conflict, on purpose

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Two Truths.* Two learners argue opposite sides of a trivial question for 60
  seconds, then swap sides and argue again.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Disagreements about *the work* are healthy; disagreements about *people*
  are not. Separating them is the leadership move. Three steps: say what you see, say what you
  need, ask what they think.
- **Work (35):** **Scenario Lab.** Teams receive real scenarios — a member who never finishes, a
  price two people cannot agree on, someone who took over everything — and role-play the
  conversation. Then apply it: each team names one real friction in their own team and holds the
  actual conversation, with the Guide nearby.
- **Close (10):** What was harder than expected?
- **Artifact:** Scenario response notes + one real conversation held.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Lead a session

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Handover.* Explain a task to a partner in 30 seconds so they could do it.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Rotating leadership: everyone leads something before Module 9.
- **Work (35):** Each team plans and runs a 10-minute work block led by a member who has not led
  before. The Guide steps fully back and takes notes.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Future Leader**, **Team Player**, **Helping Hand**. Rotation
  schedule for Module 9 agreed.
- **Artifact:** Led a work block + peer feedback on it.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Leadership as behaviour

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Best and Worst.* Describe the best and worst team leader you have
  experienced. Extract the behaviours, not the personalities.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Four behaviours that carry most of leadership: set a clear goal, remove
  blockers, close the loop, take the blame and share the credit. Then styles — directive,
  coaching, consensus — and when each fits.
- **Work (50):** Self-assessment against the four behaviours with evidence from Modules 1–5.
  Peers add one observation each. Then each learner writes their leadership weakness in one
  sentence and one thing they will do differently this module.
- **Close (15):** Weaknesses read aloud voluntarily. The Guide goes first, honestly — this sets
  the ceiling for the room.
- **Artifact:** Self-assessment + named weakness + commitment.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Delegation and decision rights

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Bad Handover.* Guide delegates a task terribly; the room lists what was
  missing.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Handover contains: outcome, constraints, deadline, decision rights,
  check-in point. Then decision rights — who decides what, and which decisions are consent-based
  ("no strong objection") versus owner-decided. Consensus on everything is how teams stall.
- **Work (50):** Build a team **decision map**: list the next 15 decisions of the venture and
  assign each an owner and a type. Then delegate three real tasks with full handovers, written.
- **Close (15):** Read one handover; the room checks all five parts are present.
- **Artifact:** Decision map + three written handovers.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Hard conversations

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Feedback Sandwich Is Dead.* Rewrite three sandwich-style feedbacks as direct
  ones that stay kind.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Situation → behaviour → impact → request. Practise on a low-stakes
  example first. Then receiving: listen, ask one clarifying question, thank, decide later.
- **Work (50):** Structured peer feedback round: every learner gives and receives feedback from
  two teammates using the frame. The Guide circulates and coaches phrasing in the moment. Then
  each team runs a retrospective on their Module 5 pitch week: what to keep, drop, try.
- **Close (15):** One thing you heard that you did not enjoy but think is true.
- **Artifact:** Feedback given and received, logged + retro output.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Operating rhythm for Module 9

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Failure Modes.* Predict the three ways your team will fail during Startup
  Challenge.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** An operating rhythm: daily stand-up, mid-point check, end-of-sprint
  retro. Plus an escalation rule — what happens when a team cannot resolve something itself.
- **Work (50):** Write the team's **operating agreement** for Module 9: roles, rotation, stand-up
  time, decision map, split rule (from Module 3), conflict process, escalation rule. Then a
  premortem: assume it failed, write why, and fix the two most likely causes now.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Future Leader**, **Team Player**, **Helping Hand**, **Deal
  Maker** where a negotiated agreement resolved a real conflict.
- **Artifact:** Signed operating agreement + premortem.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Future Leader** (core) | Led a real piece of work, and teammates can describe what that leadership did for them | 25 |
| **Team Player** | Held a role reliably across the module and completed what they took on, or renegotiated it openly | 25 |
| **Helping Hand** | Gave specific, useful feedback or unblocked a teammate on 10 occasions (cumulative) | 25 |
| **Deal Maker** | Resolved a disagreement with an arrangement both sides called fair | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**Rubric — leadership:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reliability | Work does not arrive | Does their part | Does their part and flags risk early |
| Clarity | Others are unsure what to do | Communicates the task | Handovers others can act on without asking |
| Repair | Avoids conflict or escalates it | Raises issues calmly | Raises it, resolves it, and the relationship survives |
| Self-knowledge | Cannot name a weakness | Names one | Names one, acts on it, and asks for feedback on the change |

## Differentiation & access

- **Quiet learners are not "not leaders."** The four behaviours can all be done quietly. Make
  sure the Lead role rotation reaches learners who will not volunteer.
- **Learners who dominate:** Give them the Timekeeper or Money role, where the job is to serve
  someone else's work. Name the pattern privately, not publicly.
- **Learners who have experienced conflict as unsafe:** Scenario Lab is opt-in for role-play;
  observing and writing the analysis carries equal credit.
- **Neurodivergent learners:** Write the stand-up format on a card and keep it visible. Explicit
  structure helps everyone and is essential for some.
- **Extension:** Lead a team that is not your own for one session, then compare.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Leadership as a popularity contest.** Never elect leaders by vote at Builder tier — you will
  select for confidence and social status. Assign or rotate.
- **The Guide who cannot step back.** Session 4 fails entirely if you keep talking. Sit down.
  Take notes. Let the block go slightly wrong.
- **Team agreements written to please the adult.** If every agreement says "be kind, try hard,"
  push for specifics: *what happens when someone does not do their part?* Make them write the
  uncomfortable line.
- **Frictionless teams.** A team with no conflict is usually a team where one person is doing
  everything or nobody cares. Look for it in Session 3.
- **Feedback that becomes a pile-on.** Structure is the protection. Two feedback partners, named
  in advance, using the frame. Never open-floor criticism of an individual.
- **Real conflicts that need an adult.** Anything involving exclusion, cruelty, or safeguarding
  is not a learning scenario. Stop the exercise and follow the setting's policy.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Jobs at Home.* Name everyone's job in your household. What happens when one is not
done?

**Builder:** *Run the Stand-up.* Run a 5-minute family stand-up: what everyone did, is doing,
and needs help with.

**Founder:** *Ask for Feedback.* Ask one adult who knows you well: "what is one thing I do that
makes teamwork harder?" Listen. Do not defend. Write it down.
