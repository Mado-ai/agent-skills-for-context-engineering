# Course 2 — Business Basics

**Icon:** Briefcase · **Colour:** Navy · **Core badge:** Business Brain · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Course 1 · **Français:** Les Bases des Affaires

> A business is a promise you keep, over and over.

## Big idea

An idea becomes a business when two things are true: somebody wants it enough to give something
up for it, and you can do it again tomorrow. Everything else — logos, names, premises — is
decoration on top of that trade.

## Essential questions

- What makes someone give you something in return?
- What is the difference between a thing I made and a business?
- What has to be true for this to work more than once?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| 🌱 **Little Sprouts** | Trade in a game, explain why someone accepted, and name what their business gives and gets |
| 🔨 **Junior Builders** | List everything needed to make twenty of something, name their supplier and their bottleneck |
| 🚀 **Young Founders** | Complete a one-page plan, scan three competitors, and test one core claim from it |
| 👑 **Founder CEOs** | Fill a business model canvas, identify the riskiest assumption, and design and run a test of it |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | From tier |
|---|---|---|
| Trade | I give you something, you give me something | Sprouts |
| Value | How much something is worth to *you* | Sprouts |
| Business | Making something people want, again and again | Sprouts |
| Product / Service | A thing you get / something done for you | Builders |
| Supplier | Where your stuff comes from | Builders |
| Bottleneck | The one thing that limits how many you can make | Builders |
| Competitor | Someone solving the same problem another way | Founders |
| Business model | How the whole thing makes sense together | CEOs |
| Assumption risk | The belief that, if wrong, breaks everything | CEOs |

## 🌱 Little Sprouts (5–7) — 4 × 45 min

### S1 · NOTICE — The Trading Game

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Would You Swap?* Silly swaps — a pencil for a sock. Thumbs up or down, fast.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** "A trade works when *both* people feel good." Two Helpers act out a happy
  trade and a grumpy one.
- **Work (25):** **Trading Game.** Mystery bag of three small items each (buttons, ribbons,
  shells, stickers). Ten minutes of free trading in two rounds. Between rounds: what made people
  say yes?
- **Close (8):** Chart the most-wanted items. Why those?
- **Artifact:** *Why they said yes* — one scribed sentence.

### S2 · NOTICE — Gives and Gets

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Shop Sort.* Picture cards; sort into "gives you a thing" and "does something
  for you."
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Every business has a *give* and a *get*. Draw the two-arrow diagram — it
  reappears in every course.
- **Work (25):** **My Gives and Gets.** Draw the Course 1 idea in the two-arrow frame: what you
  give the customer, what they give back.
- **Close (8):** Three learners present their arrows.
- **Artifact:** Two-arrow page.

### S3 · MAKE / SHOW — The Helper Shop

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Job Charades.*
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Some businesses sell *help*, not things.
- **Work (25):** **Helper Shop.** In pairs, a 10-minute pretend service stall — drawing
  portraits, telling jokes, wrapping parcels. Others "buy" with three play tokens. Everyone
  sells once and buys once.
- **Close (8):** Which stalls ran out of tokens? What did they share?
- **Artifact:** Token tally per stall.

### S4 · CHANGE — Do it again tomorrow

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Could You Do It Again?* Name a thing you could do 100 times, and one you
  could not.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** If you only have one shell, you cannot sell shells.
- **Work (25):** Redraw your stall as if it opened every day: what do you need more of? Who helps
  you? Colour it on a picture map.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Business Brain**.
- **Artifact:** "Every day" picture map.

## 🔨 Junior Builders (8–10) — 4 × 60 min

This tier's job is *making and selling real things*, so Business Basics is about production
reality, not paperwork.

### S1 · NOTICE — Product vs. business

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Is It a Business?* Ten cards: a lemonade stand, a cake for your sister, a
  raffle, a channel with ads. Vote and argue.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Three tests: does someone *want* it, will they *give* something for it,
  can you *repeat* it? All three, or it is a project.
- **Work (35):** Run the three tests on your Course 1 idea, honestly. Where a test fails, write
  what would have to change.
- **Close (10):** Each team names the test they failed.
- **Artifact:** Three-test sheet.

### S2 · MAKE — Make twenty

- **Warm-Up (5):** *One vs. Twenty.* Time yourself making one. Multiply by twenty. React.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Inputs, suppliers, and the bottleneck — the one thing that runs out
  first, whether that is glue, table space, or Tuesday afternoons.
- **Work (35):** List every input for twenty units. Mark each *have it / free / must buy / no
  idea*. Price the must-buys from a catalogue or supervised search. Circle the bottleneck.
- **Close (10):** Post every team's bottleneck. Most are time, and most teams are surprised.
- **Artifact:** Input list with sources, rough costs, and a circled bottleneck.

### S3 · SHOW — Test the making

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Production Line.* Pass a paper craft down a line of four, one step each.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Batching, and what "good enough" means. Agree the quality standard now,
  in writing, or it drifts when you are tired.
- **Work (35):** Make five real units against a timer. Record actual minutes per unit and how
  many failed the quality standard. Show the five to another team for a quality check.
- **Close (10):** Recalculate: how long will twenty actually take?
- **Artifact:** Five real units + a time-and-quality record.

### S4 · CHANGE — The plan that survives

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Fix the Flaw.* Guide reads a broken plan; teams find the hole.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Fair trade: good for the customer, good for you, and not bad for anyone
  else. Introduce the third question — *who else does this affect?*
- **Work (35):** Rewrite the plan with real numbers from Session 3. Simplify the product if the
  time-per-unit is impossible. Add a one-line fairness check.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Business Brain**, **Team Player**, **Kind Business**.
- **Artifact:** Revised plan with a fairness check.

## 🚀 Young Founders (11–13) — 4 × 75 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Who else is solving this?

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Nobody Has No Competition.* Each team claims uniqueness; the room tries to
  break it.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Direct competitors, indirect competitors, and the status quo. The status
  quo usually wins — people are already coping somehow.
- **Work (42):** Scan three alternatives, including "do nothing." For each: what it costs, what
  it does well, what it does badly. Then write one line on why a customer would switch.
- **Close (13):** Read the switching line; the room flags any that a competitor could also say.
- **Artifact:** Competitor scan + switching line.

### S2 · MAKE — The one-page plan

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Elevator Words.* Describe a famous business in five words.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Walk the template: **Who** it's for · **What** they get · **Why us** ·
  **What we need** · **How we know it works**. Show a completed example for a dog-walking
  business.
- **Work (42):** Draft the plan. Mark every line **known** or **guess** using Course 1 evidence.
  Guide circulates asking one question only: "how do you know?"
- **Close (13):** Swap plans; each team writes one *I wonder* on the other's biggest guess.
- **Artifact:** One-page plan v1 with known/guess marking.

### S3 · SHOW — Test the core claim

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Cheapest Possible Test.* Guide names a claim; teams race to design a test
  costing under $5 CAD and taking under an hour.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Claim → test → threshold. Write the pass number before you run it.
- **Work (42):** Pick the biggest guess on the plan and test it for real — a sign-up sheet, a
  pre-order list, a price probe, ten direct asks.
- **Close (13):** Thresholds written on the wall where they cannot move.
- **Artifact:** Test design with a pre-stated threshold, running.

### S4 · CHANGE — Plan v2

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Results Reveal.* Report the number. No spin.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Revising honestly — which lines move when one guess breaks? Then the
  fairness check and who else the business affects.
- **Work (42):** Rewrite the plan with results, add the fairness check, and write a half-page
  "what we learned and what we changed."
- **Close (13):** Badge check: **Business Brain**, **Idea Lab**, **Kind Business**, **Team
  Player**.
- **Artifact:** One-page plan v2 + learning note.
- **Mentor touchpoint:** A local business owner joins the last 20 minutes to react to two plans.
  Brief them: questions, not advice.

## 👑 Founder CEOs (14–17) — 4 × 90 min

### S1 · NOTICE — The canvas

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Reverse-Engineer It.* Map a known business onto nine blank boxes without
  being told the labels. Then reveal them.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** The nine blocks — segments, value proposition, channels, relationships,
  revenue, activities, resources, partners, costs. Ten minutes maximum; it is a worksheet, not a
  theory.
- **Work (50):** Fill the canvas in pencil for your own venture. Score every box 1–5 for
  confidence, based on evidence rather than feeling.
- **Close (15):** Name your lowest-confidence box out loud.
- **Artifact:** Canvas v1 with confidence scores.

### S2 · NOTICE — Competitors and positioning

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Steal the Line.* Five real taglines; which promise something specific?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Competitive mapping on two axes you choose. Then positioning: *"For
  [who], we are the only [category] that [difference], because [proof]."*
- **Work (50):** Scan five alternatives, map them, write the positioning line, and find the proof
  for it in your Course 1 evidence. No proof, no claim.
- **Close (15):** Read the lines; the room flags any a competitor could also say.
- **Artifact:** Competitor map + evidenced positioning line.

### S3 · MAKE / SHOW — Test the riskiest assumption

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Cheapest Possible Test*, under $5 CAD, under an hour.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Assumption → hypothesis → test → threshold, with the threshold set first.
  Then a worked example of a test that was designed to succeed and therefore proved nothing.
- **Work (50):** Design and launch a real test of the lowest-confidence box: fake-door page,
  pre-order, price probe, ten cold asks, a service delivered by hand.
- **Close (15):** Thresholds posted publicly.
- **Artifact:** Test design with pre-stated threshold; test running.

### S4 · CHANGE — Canvas v2

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Results Reveal.* Beat the threshold or not. No spin.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Which boxes move when one assumption breaks? Worked example: a failed
  channel test changing both revenue and cost blocks.
- **Work (50):** Redraw the canvas in ink with margin justifications. Write the "what we learned
  and changed" note. Add the ethics line: what this business will not do.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Business Brain**, **Idea Lab**, **Kind Business**, **Deal
  Maker** where a real supply arrangement was negotiated.
- **Artifact:** Canvas v2 + learning note.
- **Mentor touchpoint:** Mentor reacts to two canvases. Questions only.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Business Brain** (core) | Can explain their give/get, name their customer, and state one thing that must be true for the business to repeat | 25 |
| **Kind Business** | Identified who else the business affects and changed something because of it | 25 |
| **Team Player** | Held a role across all four sessions and can say what teammates did | 25 |
| **Idea Lab** | Founders and CEOs — ran a test that could have proved them wrong | 25 |
| **Deal Maker** | Negotiated a trade or supply arrangement both sides called fair | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the course).

**Rubric — the plan / canvas:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer | "Everyone" | A named group | A specific group with evidence they exist and care |
| Value | Describes the product | Describes what the customer gains | The gain is stated in the customer's own words |
| Repeatability | Not considered | Lists what is needed | Bottleneck identified with a plan for it |
| Honesty | Claims with no basis | Marks what is unknown | Tests the unknown and reports the result, including failures |

## Differentiation & access

- **Maths-light route:** All costing at Junior Builders can be done with a catalogue and a
  calculator; no mental arithmetic required.
- **Learners without a viable Course 1 idea:** Offer three house ideas — a stationery refill
  service, a playground equipment library, a birthday-card studio. Adopting one carries no
  penalty.
- **Extension (Builders):** Make twenty for real and compare the estimate to the actual.
- **Extension (Founders):** Add a second customer segment and compare the plan for each.
- **Extension (CEOs):** Model the venture as a non-profit and compare which blocks change.

## Guide watch-outs

- **The canvas becomes an exam.** It is a thinking tool. If teams are filling boxes to please
  you, take the template away and ask them to draw the business instead.
- **"Everyone is my customer."** The most common Junior Builders answer. Do not correct it — ask
  for three real names and watch it collapse on its own.
- **Competitor denial.** Founders will insist nothing else exists. If people currently do
  nothing, that is what you are up against.
- **Play money leaking into real expectations.** Sprouts will ask if they can keep the tokens.
  Decide before the session and say it clearly at the start.
- **Skipping the fairness check.** It is where Course 8 is planted. Teams that skip it here find
  Course 8 much harder.
- **Builders over-promising production.** A team planning 200 units in four sessions has not done
  Session 3 honestly. Make them time five real units.

## Materials

Mystery trade bags · play tokens · one-page plan templates · business model canvas (A3) ·
sticky notes · supplier catalogues or supervised device access · competitor map sheets ·
production timing sheets · timers.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**🌱 Sprouts:** *Give and Get.* Pick a shop you both know. Draw the two arrows.

**🔨 Builders:** *Kitchen Supply Chain.* Pick one food item. Trace back where each part came
from, as far as you can get in ten minutes.

**🚀 Young Founders:** *The Switch Question.* Ask an adult about something they recently switched
to. Why did they switch? That is competition, in real life.

**👑 Founder CEOs:** *Ask an Owner.* Ask anyone who runs or works in a small business: what is
the one thing that would sink it if it stopped working? That is their key resource.

> am not sure about." That second half is the real skill.
