# Module 2 — Business Basics

**Icon:** Briefcase · **Colour:** Navy · **Core badge:** Business Brain · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Module 1 · **Unlocks:** counts toward Leadership Lab

> A business is a promise you keep, over and over.

## Big idea

An idea becomes a business when two things are true: somebody wants it enough to give
something up for it, and you can do it again tomorrow. Everything else — logos, names,
premises — is decoration on top of that trade.

## Essential questions

- What makes someone give you something in return?
- What is the difference between a thing I made and a business?
- What has to be true for this to work more than once?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Trade in a game, explain why someone accepted their trade, and name what their business gives and gets |
| **Builder** | Complete a one-page plan (who, what, why me, what I need) and explain why their idea is a business and not just a product |
| **Founder** | Fill a business model canvas, identify the riskiest assumption in it, and design and run a test of that assumption |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Trade | I give you something, you give me something | Spark |
| Value | How much something is worth to *you* | Spark |
| Business | Making something people want, again and again | Spark |
| Product / Service | A thing you get / something you do for someone | Builder |
| Supplier | Where your stuff comes from | Builder |
| Competitor | Someone solving the same problem another way | Builder |
| Business model | How the whole thing makes sense together | Founder |
| Assumption risk | The belief that, if wrong, breaks everything | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — The Trading Game

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Would You Swap?* Guide offers silly swaps — a pencil for a sock, a sticker
  for a paperclip. Thumbs up or down, fast.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** "A trade works when *both* people feel good." Two Helpers act out a
  happy trade and a grumpy one.
- **Work (25):** **Trading Game.** Every learner gets a mystery bag of three small items
  (buttons, ribbons, shells, stickers). Ten minutes of free trading in two rounds. Between
  rounds, ask: what made people say yes?
- **Close (8):** Chart the most-wanted items. Why those?
- **Artifact:** *Why they said yes* — one scribed sentence.

### Session 2 · NOTICE — Gives and Gets

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Shop Sort.* Picture cards of shops; sort into "gives you a thing" and "does
  something for you."
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Every business has a *give* and a *get*. Draw the two-arrow diagram on
  the board — it reappears in every module.
- **Work (25):** **My Gives and Gets.** Learners draw their Module 1 idea in the two-arrow
  frame: what they give the customer, what the customer gives back (money, a smile, a thank
  you, help).
- **Close (8):** Three learners present their arrows.
- **Artifact:** Two-arrow page.

### Session 3 · MAKE / SHOW — The Helper Shop

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Job Charades.* Act out a job; others guess.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Some businesses sell *help*, not things.
- **Work (25):** **Helper Shop.** In pairs, learners set up a 10-minute pretend service stall —
  drawing portraits, telling jokes, wrapping parcels, sorting pencils. Others "buy" with three
  play tokens each. Everyone gets one turn as seller and one as customer.
- **Close (8):** Which stalls ran out of tokens? What did they have in common?
- **Artifact:** Token tally per stall.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Do it again tomorrow

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Could You Do It Again?* Name a thing you could do 100 times, and one you
  could not.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** A business has to work more than once. If you only have one shell, you
  cannot sell shells.
- **Work (25):** Learners redraw their stall as if it opened every day: what do they need more
  of? Who helps them? Colour the answer on a simple picture map.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Business Brain**.
- **Artifact:** "Every day" picture map.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Product vs. business

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Is It a Business?* Ten cards: a lemonade stand, a birthday cake you baked
  for your sister, a YouTube channel with ads, a school raffle. Vote and argue.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Three tests: does someone *want* it, will they *give* something for it,
  can you *repeat* it? All three, or it is a project.
- **Work (35):** Teams run the three tests on their Module 1 idea, honestly. Where a test fails,
  they write what would have to change. Then a quick competitor scan: who else solves this?
  Find three, including "do nothing" as a competitor.
- **Close (10):** Each team names the competitor that scares them most.
- **Artifact:** Three-test sheet + competitor scan.

### Session 2 · MAKE — The one-page plan

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Elevator Words.* Describe a famous business in five words.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Walk through the one-page plan template: **Who** it's for · **What** they
  get · **Why us** · **What we need** · **How we know it works**. Show a completed example for a
  dog-walking business.
- **Work (35):** Teams draft their plan. Guide circulates asking one question only: "how do you
  know?"
- **Close (10):** Swap plans with another team; each writes one *I wonder* on a sticky.
- **Artifact:** One-page plan v1.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Supply chain and reality check

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Where Does It Come From?* Trace a pencil back four steps.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Suppliers and inputs. Everything you sell was something else first, and
  someone had to give it to you.
- **Work (35):** Teams list every input their business needs, mark each as *have it / can get it
  free / must buy / no idea*, then price the "must buy" items using a catalogue or supervised
  search. Present the plan to a second team for challenge.
- **Close (10):** Which input is the hardest to get? That is your bottleneck — name it.
- **Artifact:** Input list with sources and rough costs.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Plan v2

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Fix the Flaw.* Guide reads a broken plan; teams find the hole.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Fair trade: your business should be good for the customer, good for you,
  and not bad for anyone else. Introduce the third question — *who else does this affect?*
- **Work (35):** Rewrite the plan with what was learned. Add a "fairness check" line.
- **Close (10):** Badge check for **Business Brain**; **Kind Business** where the fairness check
  changed a decision.
- **Artifact:** One-page plan v2 with fairness check.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — The canvas

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Reverse-Engineer It.* In teams, map a known business (a local café, a
  streaming service) onto nine blank boxes without being told what the boxes are. Then reveal
  the canvas labels.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** The nine blocks — customer segments, value proposition, channels,
  relationships, revenue, key activities, key resources, partners, costs. Ten minutes maximum;
  the canvas is a worksheet, not a theory.
- **Work (50):** Fill the canvas for their own venture in pencil. Then mark every box with a
  confidence score 1–5 based on evidence, not feeling.
- **Close (15):** Name your lowest-confidence box out loud.
- **Artifact:** Canvas v1 with confidence scores.

### Session 2 · NOTICE — Competitors and positioning

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Nobody Has No Competition.* Every team claims uniqueness; the room tries
  to break it.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Direct competitors, indirect competitors, and the status quo. The status
  quo usually wins — people are already coping.
- **Work (50):** Competitive scan of five alternatives on two axes the team chooses. Then write
  a positioning line: *"For [who], we are the only [category] that [difference], because
  [proof]."*
- **Close (15):** Read positioning lines aloud; the room flags any that could be said by a
  competitor. If it could, it is not positioning.
- **Artifact:** Competitor map + positioning line.

### Session 3 · MAKE / SHOW — Test the riskiest assumption

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Cheapest Possible Test.* Guide names an assumption; teams race to design a
  test costing under $5 and taking under an hour.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Assumption → hypothesis → test → threshold. Set the pass/fail number
  *before* running the test, or you will move the goalposts.
- **Work (50):** Design and launch the test for their lowest-confidence box: a sign-up sheet, a
  pre-order list, a price probe, a fake-door page, ten cold asks.
- **Close (15):** State your threshold publicly. Written on the wall so it cannot move.
- **Artifact:** Test design with pre-stated pass threshold; test running.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Canvas v2

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Results Reveal.* Each team reports the number and whether it beat their
  threshold. No spin allowed.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Revising a model honestly. Which boxes move when one assumption breaks?
  Show a worked example where a failed channel test changed both revenue and cost blocks.
- **Work (50):** Redraw the canvas in ink with changes justified in the margin. Write a
  half-page "what we learned and what we changed" note.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Business Brain**, **Kind Business**, **Idea Lab**.
- **Artifact:** Canvas v2 + learning note.
- **Mentor touchpoint:** Invite a local business owner to the last 20 minutes to react to two
  canvases. Brief them: ask questions, do not give advice.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Business Brain** (core) | Can explain their give/get, name their customer, and state one thing that must be true for the business to repeat | 25 |
| **Kind Business** | Identified who else their business affects and changed something because of it | 25 |
| **Deal Maker** | Negotiated a trade or supply arrangement that both sides agreed was fair (may be earned in Module 8) | 25 |
| **Team Player** | Held a role across all four sessions and can say what their teammates did | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**Rubric — the plan / canvas:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer | "Everyone" | A named group | A specific group with evidence they exist and care |
| Value | Describes the product | Describes what the customer gains | Gain is stated in the customer's words |
| Repeatability | Not considered | Lists what is needed | Bottleneck identified with a plan for it |
| Honesty | Claims with no basis | Marks what is unknown | Tests the unknown and reports the result, including failures |

## Differentiation & access

- **Maths-light route:** All costing at Builder tier can be done with a catalogue and a
  calculator; no mental arithmetic is required.
- **Learners without a viable Module 1 idea:** Offer three "house ideas" (school stationery
  refill service, playground equipment library, birthday-card studio) that any team may adopt.
  Adopting a house idea carries no penalty.
- **Extension (Builder):** Add a second customer segment and compare the plan for each.
- **Extension (Founder):** Model the business as a non-profit and compare which canvas blocks
  change.

## Guide watch-outs

- **The canvas becomes an exam.** It is a thinking tool. If teams are filling boxes to please
  you, take the template away and ask them to draw the business instead.
- **"Everyone is my customer."** The single most common Builder answer. Do not correct it —
  ask them to name three real people and watch the answer collapse on its own.
- **Competitor denial.** Founders will insist nothing else exists. The status quo is always a
  competitor; if people currently do nothing, that is what you are up against.
- **Play money leaking into real expectations.** Spark learners will ask if they can keep the
  tokens. Decide the answer before the session, say it clearly at the start.
- **Skipping the fairness check because it is soft.** It is the block where Module 8 is planted.
  Teams that skip it here find Module 8 much harder.

## Materials

Mystery trade bags · play tokens · one-page plan templates · business model canvas (A3) ·
sticky notes · supplier catalogues or supervised device access · competitor map sheets ·
timers.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Give and Get.* Pick a shop you both know. Draw the two arrows: what do they give,
what do they get?

**Builder:** *Kitchen Supply Chain.* Pick one item of food. Trace back where each part came
from, as far as you can get in ten minutes.

**Founder:** *Ask an Owner.* Ask any adult who runs or works in a small business: what is the
one thing that would sink it if it stopped working? That is their key resource.

## Family note (send after Session 4)

> Your child moved from "I have an idea" to "here is why it could work — and here is the part
> I am not sure about." That second half is the real skill.
