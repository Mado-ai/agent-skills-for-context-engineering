# Module 9 — Startup Challenge

**Icon:** Rocket · **Colour:** Energy · **Core badge:** Launch Crew · **Sessions:** 6
**Prerequisites:** Modules 1–8 complete; **Money Smart** and **I Can Pitch!** badges held
**Unlocks:** Module 10

> Build it. Sell it. Learn from it.

## Big idea

Everything so far has been preparation. This module is the real thing: a product, real
customers, real money at Builder and Founder tiers, and a public deadline that does not move.
Two cycles of Notice–Make–Show–Change run back to back, so a bad first day is recoverable.

**Milestone:** Market Day sits between Session 4 and Session 5.

## Essential questions

- Can we actually deliver what we promised, on the day, to strangers?
- What did the real world tell us that our plan did not?
- What would we do differently with one more week?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Take part in a class shop with a job they hold, sell to a real visitor, and say what they learned |
| **Builder** | Run a stall from a $50 float, hold a role, serve real customers, keep the money right, and report profit honestly |
| **Founder** | Launch a venture with real customers, operate to their plan, keep an accurate ledger, adapt during the sprint, and report unit economics against forecast |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Stall / shop | Where we sell | Spark |
| Customer | Someone who really buys | Spark |
| Float | The money we start with | Builder |
| Stock | How many we have to sell | Builder |
| Takings | All money received | Builder |
| Sprint | A short push with a fixed end | Founder |
| Actual vs. forecast | What happened vs. what we said would happen | Founder |
| Stop-rule | The line at which we halt | Founder |

---

## Sprint shape (all tiers)

| Session | Beat | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | NOTICE | Final prep: what we are selling, to whom, at what price, who does what |
| 2 | MAKE | Production and setup — make the stock, build the stall, write the signs |
| 3 | SHOW | Dry run with a friendly audience; fix what breaks |
| 4 | CHANGE | Fix, restock, final checks, money and safety brief |
| — | **MARKET DAY** | Real customers, real money |
| 5 | NOTICE | Count everything: money, stock, feedback, what happened |
| 6 | CHANGE | Reconcile, analyse, decide what you would do next |

Dry run in Session 3 is non-negotiable. Every single failure that appears on Market Day
appeared in the dry run first, in a form somebody chose to ignore.

## Spark (6–8) — 6 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — What are we selling?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Shop Memory.* Describe the best shop you have visited.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Our class shop will have real visitors. We need things to sell, prices,
  signs, and jobs.
- **Work (25):** Whole-group decisions with picture voting: what we sell (2–3 items maximum),
  what each costs in tokens or coins, and who does which job. Job cards from Module 6 are reused.
- **Close (8):** Everyone says their job out loud.
- **Artifact:** Job card + agreed product list.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Making the stock

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Production Line.* Pass a paper craft down a line of four; each does one step.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Making lots of the same thing is easier in steps.
- **Work (25):** Production. Set a group target ("we need 30") and count as you go on a big
  chart. Quality check: one Helper is the "customer" who inspects.
- **Close (8):** Count the stock. How many more do we need?
- **Artifact:** Stock counted against target.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Dry run

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Polite Practice.* "Hello, would you like…" · "Thank you, that's two coins."
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Practise the shop before the real visitors come.
- **Work (25):** Set up the shop; another class visits with play coins. Everyone does their job.
  Helpers note what went wrong.
- **Close (8):** What broke? Three fixes chosen by the group.
- **Artifact:** Dry run completed + three fixes.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Fix and finish

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Sign Check.* Can you read our signs from the door?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Last checks: enough stock, clear prices, everyone knows their job.
- **Work (25):** Apply the three fixes, finish stock, make the final signs, set out the shop.
- **Close (8):** Invitations home; the shop rules read aloud together.
- **Artifact:** Shop ready.

### 🚀 MARKET DAY — Class Shop (45–60 min)

Real visitors — families, other classes, staff. Adults handle any real money; learners handle
tokens or supervised coins. Every learner does their job for the whole session. Photos taken
against the consent list.

### Session 5 · NOTICE — Count it up

- **Warm-Up (5):** *How Did It Feel?* One word each.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Counting tells us how it went.
- **Work (25):** Count takings, count leftover stock, count how many visitors came. Build a big
  picture chart of all three.
- **Close (8):** What sold out? What is left over?
- **Artifact:** Three-count chart.

### Session 6 · CHANGE — What we learned

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Best Moment.* Share one.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Every shop learns something for next time.
- **Work (25):** Draw *Next Time* pages: one thing to keep, one to change. Then decide together
  what happens to the money using the Module 3 envelopes — spend, save, share.
- **Close (8):** Badge check: **Launch Crew**, **Young CEO**, **Keep Building**.
- **Artifact:** *Next Time* page + money decision.

## Builder (9–12) — 6 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Final plan and float

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Sell-Out or Left-Over?* Which is the worse problem?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Locking the plan: product, price (from Module 3), quantity, roles (from
  Module 6), marketing (from Module 4), give-back (from Module 8). Then the float: up to $50,
  signed out, every dollar accounted for.
- **Work (35):** Complete the **Market Day plan** on one page. Calculate how many units are
  needed to break even and set a stretch target. Sign out the float, two signatures.
- **Close (10):** Each team states its break-even number of units.
- **Artifact:** Market Day plan + float signed out.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Production and stall build

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Batch It.* Time one unit made alone vs. four made in a batch.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Production planning: how long one takes × how many needed = the hours you
  do not have. Decide what to cut now, not on the night before.
- **Work (35):** Produce stock against a tracked target; build the stall layout and signage.
  Money role sets up the cash tin, float count, and takings sheet.
- **Close (10):** Stock count vs. target posted publicly.
- **Artifact:** Stock at ≥ 60% of target + stall built.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Dry run with real feedback

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Awkward Customer.* Practise: someone haggles, someone wants a refund,
  someone has no change.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The dry run tests the whole system — product, price, signage, service,
  money handling — not just the product.
- **Work (35):** Run the stall for 20 minutes with a real friendly audience (staff, another
  class) using real small money if permitted. Then a hard debrief: what broke, what confused
  people, what took too long.
- **Close (10):** Each team writes three fixes, ranked.
- **Artifact:** Dry run takings + three ranked fixes.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Fix, restock, brief

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Worst Case.* Name what will go wrong tomorrow and what you will do.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The money brief: who handles cash, two-person counting, where the tin
  lives, what to do if the amount is wrong. Then the safety brief per the setting's policy.
- **Work (35):** Apply fixes, finish stock, final pricing check, print signs, pack the kit box
  with a checklist.
- **Close (10):** Kit checklist signed. Roles confirmed for the day.
- **Artifact:** Kit checklist signed + fixes applied.

### 🚀 MARKET DAY — Stall (90–120 min)

Real customers, real money, real deadline. Roles held throughout with one rotation at the
midpoint. Money counted at start, midpoint, and end, always by two people with an adult
witness. Teams record one line of notes every 20 minutes — what sold, what people asked, what
they walked away from.

### Session 5 · NOTICE — Reconcile and analyse

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Guess Before You Count.* Everyone writes their guess at total takings.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Reconciliation: float + takings − spend = what should be in the tin. If
  it does not match, you find out why; you do not adjust the number.
- **Work (35):** Count and reconcile. Calculate actual profit, actual cost per unit, and units
  sold vs. break-even. Compare to the Module 3 forecast. Chart forecast vs. actual.
- **Close (10):** Who was closest to their forecast? What did they do differently?
- **Artifact:** Reconciled accounts + forecast vs. actual chart.

### Session 6 · CHANGE — Learn and decide

- **Warm-Up (5):** *One Thing.* The single change with the biggest effect.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Separating luck from skill: what would have happened with different
  weather, a different spot, a different day? Then the split rule from Module 3 is applied, and
  the give-back from Module 8 is delivered.
- **Work (35):** Write the **Startup Report**: what we sold, what we made, what we learned, what
  we would change, what we did with the money. Deliver the give-back.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Launch Crew**, **Young CEO**, **Money Smart**, **Keep
  Building**, **Team Player**, **Community Builder**.
- **Artifact:** Startup Report + give-back delivered.

## Founder (13–16) — 6 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Lock the sprint

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Scope Cut.* Cut 30% of your plan right now. What goes?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Sprint discipline: fixed end date, fixed budget, variable scope. Review
  the operating plan from Module 7 including the stop-rule, and set the sprint's three numbers.
- **Work (50):** Finalise: offer, price, channel, production plan, roles and rotation, cash plan,
  legal and safety checks (food handling, trading permission, data rules — see safeguarding).
  Confirm the venue and any partnership terms from Module 8.
- **Close (15):** Each team states its sprint goal, budget, and stop-rule.
- **Artifact:** Locked sprint plan with stop-rule.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Build and go live

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Critical Path.* Identify the one task that, if late, delays everything.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Bottlenecks and buffer. Then quality standards: define what "acceptable"
  means before production, in writing, or it drifts under time pressure.
- **Work (50):** Production, channel setup, pre-orders open. Ledger live from the first
  transaction. Marketing goes out per the Module 4 plan with response tracking.
- **Close (15):** Dashboard updated. First real numbers on the board.
- **Artifact:** Stock/service ready + live ledger + marketing running.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Soft launch

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Failure Injection.* Remove one team member for the first ten minutes. Does
  the system survive?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** A soft launch is a test with real stakes and a small blast radius: real
  customers, real money, small volume, where a failure costs you an apology rather than a
  reputation.
- **Work (50):** Serve real customers at low volume. Capture: conversion (asked vs. bought),
  time per transaction, objections heard, quality failures. One member does nothing but observe
  and record.
- **Close (15):** Read the top three objections. These become the fixes.
- **Artifact:** Soft launch data + objection list.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Iterate and scale up

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Price Check.* Given soft-launch data, does the price move?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** What to change between soft launch and full launch — and what not to.
  Changing everything means learning nothing. Two changes maximum, both justified by data.
- **Work (50):** Apply two changes. Scale production. Update the cash plan with actuals. Run a
  full operational rehearsal end to end with timings. Confirm the give-back logistics.
- **Close (15):** Dashboard against targets. Stop-rule checked honestly — is any team past it?
- **Artifact:** Two justified changes + rehearsal + updated cash plan.

### 🚀 MARKET DAY — Full launch (half day)

Public trading. Roles rotate on schedule. Ledger updated live, not reconstructed afterwards.
Hourly dashboard reading. Two-person money handling with adult witness. One member per team
runs continuous customer observation. Photo and video per consent list.

### Session 5 · NOTICE — Reconcile, measure, hear it

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Forecast vs. Feeling.* Write down what you think happened, then look.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Full reconciliation and honest variance analysis: revenue, unit cost,
  contribution, cash position, conversion, and time. Variance is explained, never rounded away.
- **Work (50):** Reconcile the ledger to the last cent. Calculate actual unit economics and
  compare to the Module 3 model. Analyse customer observations for patterns. Calculate the
  response rate for each marketing channel.
- **Close (15):** Each team states its biggest variance and its best guess at the cause.
- **Artifact:** Reconciled ledger + variance analysis + customer insight summary.

### Session 6 · CHANGE — Report and decide

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Continue, Pivot, or Stop?* Initial gut answer, written and sealed.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Writing an honest venture report: what we set out to do, what happened,
  what the numbers say, what we learned, what we recommend. Bad news goes near the top.
- **Work (50):** Write the **Venture Report** (2 pages maximum). Apply the split rule; deliver
  the give-back with its measured impact. Then make and record the continue/pivot/stop decision
  against the stop-rule set in Session 1 — and compare it to the sealed gut answer.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Launch Crew**, **Young CEO**, **Money Smart**, **Keep
  Building**, **Deal Maker**, **Community Builder**, **Green Founder**.
- **Artifact:** Venture Report + recorded decision + delivered give-back.
- **Mentor touchpoint:** A mentor reads two Venture Reports and responds to the decision, not
  the results.

## Market Day operations

### Before

- Venue, tables, power, weather plan, and permissions confirmed one week ahead.
- Risk assessment per the setting's policy. Food ventures need the local food-handling rules
  checked — this is not optional and can end a venture concept; check in Session 1, not later.
- Float signed out, denominations checked, cash tin and a secure location agreed.
- Consent list checked for photography.
- Adult ratios: Spark 1:6, Builder 1:8, Founder 1:12, plus one adult on money at all times.

### During

- Money is counted at open, midpoint, and close. Two learners count, one adult witnesses, all
  three initial the sheet.
- No learner is ever alone with the tin or with an unfamiliar adult.
- Every team logs one observation line every 20 minutes.
- The Guide does not sell, price, or rescue. The Guide keeps people safe and takes photographs.

### After

- Reconcile the same day if possible; memory decays fast and disputes grow.
- Leftover stock is logged, not binned quietly — it is data about forecasting.
- Thank every partner, visitor, and helper within a week (Module 8 skill, applied).

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch Crew** (core) | Held a role through the full sprint and traded with real customers on Market Day | 25 |
| **Young CEO** | Made a decision during the sprint based on live data | 25 |
| **Money Smart** | Money reconciled accurately with the discrepancy explained, if any | 25 |
| **Keep Building** | Something failed, and they fixed it and went again within the sprint | 25 |
| **Team Player** | Covered for a teammate or handed over cleanly under time pressure | 25 |
| **Community Builder** | Delivered the give-back as planned | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (60 for the module). Market Day itself: 50 points.

**Rubric — the venture:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Did not trade, or adults carried it | Traded with real customers | Traded and adapted live to what was happening |
| Money | Not reconciled | Reconciled, minor gaps explained | Accurate to the cent; variance analysed against forecast |
| Learning | "It was fun" | Names what worked and what did not | Traces outcomes to specific decisions, including their own errors |
| Teamwork | Roles collapsed | Roles held | Roles held and flexed; team covered gaps without instruction |
| Integrity | Overclaimed results | Reported results accurately | Reported bad news first and unprompted |

## Differentiation & access

- **Learners overwhelmed by public trading:** Behind-the-scenes roles — production, stock,
  money, observation, restocking — are full **Launch Crew** roles. Customer-facing is not
  required.
- **Sensory load on Market Day:** Plan a quiet station and a scheduled break for any learner who
  needs it, arranged before the day, not improvised.
- **Teams whose product fails in the dry run:** They pivot in Session 4 to a simpler offer.
  This is the best possible learning outcome and should be celebrated, not treated as a setback.
- **No trading permitted in your setting:** Run Market Day with an internal token economy.
  Every badge except the money-reconciliation criterion applies unchanged; substitute a token
  reconciliation.
- **Extension (Founder):** Run a second trading occasion with the changes applied and compare the
  two sets of unit economics.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Adults running the stall.** The most common failure of this module. It will be slower,
  scruffier, and less profitable when learners run it. That is the correct outcome.
- **Skipping the dry run to make more stock.** Every year, someone asks. Refuse. Under-stocked
  and rehearsed beats fully-stocked and chaotic.
- **Money going missing.** Almost always a process failure, not theft. Two-person counting at
  three fixed points prevents nearly all of it. Never accuse; investigate the process out loud
  and fix it.
- **Food safety assumed rather than checked.** Check local rules in Session 1. Discovering the
  problem the night before Market Day is a preventable disaster.
- **Profit becoming the whole point.** The team that lost money and can explain exactly why has
  learned more than the team that sold out by luck. Say this before Market Day, not after, or it
  sounds like consolation.
- **The exhausted crash.** Market Day is emotionally enormous. Session 5 should start with
  feelings before numbers, and the Guide should expect some tears in every cohort.
- **Comparing teams publicly on takings.** Do not. Badges are criterion-based for exactly this
  reason.

## Home extension

**Spark:** *Tell the Story.* Tell someone at home what your job at the shop was and what
happened.

**Builder:** *Family Debrief.* Explain your takings, costs, and profit to a family member. Can
they follow it?

**Founder:** *The Honest Version.* Tell someone at home the part that went wrong and what you
would do differently. Practising honest reporting out loud makes the written report better.

## Family note (send after Session 6)

> Your child built something, sold it to real people, handled real money, and then sat down and
> worked out what actually happened. Some of it went wrong. That is the part that teaches.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What went wrong on Market Day, and what did you do about it?"*
>
> If they can answer that clearly, they have learned more this month than any profit figure
> would show.
