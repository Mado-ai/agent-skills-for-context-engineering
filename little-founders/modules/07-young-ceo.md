# Module 7 — Young CEO

**Icon:** Growth chart / crown · **Colour:** Teal · **Core badge:** Young CEO · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Module 6 · **Unlocks:** Module 8

> Having the idea is the easy part. Running it is the job.

## Big idea

A CEO's real work is a loop: decide what matters, watch a small number of honest numbers, make
a call, and be accountable for what happens next. Learners have made things and led people —
this module is about steering.

## Essential questions

- If I could only see three numbers each week, which three?
- What does this number tell me to *do*?
- What do I do when the number says the thing I did not want to hear?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Say what the person in charge does, check on their own job, and make one choice for their group and explain it |
| **Builder** | Choose and track three numbers weekly, run a weekly review, and make one decision that a number caused |
| **Founder** | Build a simple dashboard, run a weekly review against goals, decide to add/cut/keep something on evidence, and own the outcome publicly |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| In charge | The person who decides and checks | Spark |
| Checking | Looking to see if it worked | Spark |
| Goal | What we are trying to reach by when | Builder |
| Measure | A number that shows how we are doing | Builder |
| Weekly review | The meeting where we look at the numbers | Builder |
| Dashboard | The few numbers, in one place | Founder |
| Trade-off | Choosing one thing means giving up another | Founder |
| Accountability | The outcome is mine to answer for | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — What does the boss do?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Boss or Not?* Guide describes actions ("shouts at everyone", "checks
  everyone has what they need"); learners vote thumbs for good-boss behaviour.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** The person in charge **decides**, **checks**, and **helps**. Three hand
  actions to remember it.
- **Work (25):** **Captain for the Day.** In teams of four, one learner is Captain for a 10-minute
  build, then hands over. Two rounds, so two learners captain. Captains practise all three
  actions; Helpers prompt quietly.
- **Close (8):** What was hard about being Captain?
- **Artifact:** Captain's three-action card, ticked.

### Session 2 · NOTICE — Counting how we did

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Count It.* Count something in the room three ways.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Grown-ups in business count things to see if their idea is working. We can
  count too — customers, smiles, coins, things made.
- **Work (25):** **Our Three Counts.** Teams pick three things to count during a 15-minute
  pretend shop and tally them on a big chart with pictures.
- **Close (8):** Which count surprised you?
- **Artifact:** Picture tally chart.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Look at it together

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Bigger or Smaller?* Compare two bar heights on the chart.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Looking at your counts helps you choose what to do next.
- **Work (25):** **Team Check-In.** Each team stands at their chart. Guide asks three questions:
  what went up? what went down? what shall we do differently? Answers are drawn on a *Next Time*
  card.
- **Close (8):** Read the *Next Time* cards aloud.
- **Artifact:** *Next Time* card.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Make the change

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Same Shop, New Plan.* Recall your Next Time card.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** A good boss actually does the thing they decided.
- **Work (25):** Re-run the pretend shop with the change. Count the same three things again and
  compare charts side by side.
- **Close (8):** Badge check: **Young CEO**, **Number Cruncher**.
- **Artifact:** Two charts, before and after, with the change named.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Goals and the three numbers

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Which Number Matters?* A stand sold 40 cups but lost money. Which number did
  they watch, and which should they have?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** A goal has a number and a date. Then choose three measures — one for
  **money** (profit so far), one for **customers** (how many, or how many came back), one for
  **work** (things made, or tasks done).
- **Work (35):** Each team writes a 4-week goal and picks its three numbers, defining exactly how
  each is counted. Ambiguous definitions are sent back — "customers" means what, precisely?
- **Close (10):** Post goals and definitions on the wall. They stay up through Module 9.
- **Artifact:** Goal + three defined measures.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Build the tracker and the rhythm

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Guess the Trend.* Show three weeks of numbers; predict week four.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** A tracker is a table, not an art project: date, three numbers, one note.
  The note is where you record *why* — future you will not remember.
- **Work (35):** Build the tracker (paper or spreadsheet), enter what is already known from
  Modules 3–5, and set the weekly review slot. Then run the first review using the four
  questions: *Where are we? What changed? Why? What will we do?*
- **Close (10):** Each Lead states the team's headline number.
- **Artifact:** Tracker with entries + first weekly review notes.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Make a decision from data

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Data Says No.* You love your product, but nobody bought it twice. What now?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Three moves when a number is bad: change the thing, change the price,
  change who you are selling to. Do one at a time or you will not know what worked.
- **Work (35):** Each team makes one real decision driven by a number, writes it on a **decision
  card** (what we saw, what we decided, what we expect to happen, when we will check), and
  starts acting on it. Then teams present decisions to each other for challenge.
- **Close (10):** Every decision card pinned on the wall with its check date.
- **Artifact:** Decision card, in progress.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Review, adjust, own it

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Predictions Revisited.* Compare Session 2's guesses to reality.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Owning it: "I decided that, here is what happened, here is what I learned."
  The Guide models this with a real decision of their own that went badly.
- **Work (35):** Weekly review number two. Check the decision card against what actually
  happened. Write a short **CEO note**: goal status, what I decided, what happened, what I will
  do next.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Young CEO**, **Number Cruncher**, **Keep Building**.
- **Artifact:** CEO note + updated tracker.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — What actually matters

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Vanity vs. Value.* Sort ten metrics into ones that flatter and ones that
  inform. Followers vs. repeat purchases; page views vs. conversion.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** One north-star metric plus three to five supporting measures spanning
  money, customer, and operations. Leading indicators (predict the future) vs. lagging ones
  (report the past) — you need both, and only leading ones let you act in time.
- **Work (50):** Choose the north star with a written justification. Define every metric to the
  level where two people would count it identically. Set targets for the Module 9 sprint.
- **Close (15):** Defend your north star to a challenging peer team.
- **Artifact:** Metric definitions + targets + justification.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Dashboard and operating cadence

- **Warm-Up (10):** *One Screen.* Sketch everything you need to know about your venture on a
  single index card.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** A dashboard is one screen, updated on a fixed day, that answers "are we
  on track?" in ten seconds. Then the cadence: daily stand-up, weekly review, sprint retro —
  each with a different question.
- **Work (50):** Build the dashboard in a spreadsheet, linked to the Module 3 ledger so money
  numbers are not retyped. Populate with real data to date. Run weekly review one against
  targets.
- **Close (15):** Screens shown side by side; steal one good idea from another team.
- **Artifact:** Live dashboard + weekly review one.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Add, cut, or keep

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Kill a Feature.* Each team names the part of their product they would defend
  hardest. Then the room argues for cutting it.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Trade-offs and opportunity cost: every yes is a no to something else.
  Introduce a simple decision record — context, options considered, decision, expected outcome,
  review date, owner.
- **Work (50):** Each team makes one substantive add/cut/keep decision on evidence, records it,
  and communicates it to the team members it affects using the Module 6 handover format. Then a
  cross-team review panel challenges two decisions per team.
- **Close (15):** Any decision that could not survive challenge is reopened, not defended.
- **Artifact:** Two decision records + evidence.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Accountability and the Module 9 plan

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Own It.* Each learner states one thing that went wrong that was theirs.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Accountability without blame: the outcome is mine, the cause is the
  system, the fix is specific. Then plan the sprint: goal, metrics, roles, cadence, budget,
  stop-rule — the condition under which you would halt.
- **Work (50):** Write the **Startup Challenge operating plan** combining Module 6's operating
  agreement, Module 3's cash plan, and this module's dashboard. Include the stop-rule, in
  writing, before there is any emotional investment in continuing.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Young CEO**, **Number Cruncher**, **Keep Building**,
  **Future Leader** where a learner led a review well.
- **Artifact:** Startup Challenge operating plan with stop-rule.
- **Mentor touchpoint:** A mentor reviews two dashboards and asks: "what would you do tomorrow
  if this number halved?"

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Young CEO** (core) | Tracked their measures for at least two review cycles and made one decision the data caused | 25 |
| **Number Cruncher** | Produced a correct, self-checked calculation that changed what the team did | 25 |
| **Keep Building** | Reported a bad number honestly and acted on it rather than hiding or explaining it away | 25 |
| **Future Leader** | Ran a weekly review that the team found useful, without the Guide | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**Rubric — running it:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Measures | Counts whatever is easy | Three defined, relevant measures | North star justified; leading and lagging both present |
| Honesty | Reports only good news | Reports all numbers | Volunteers the bad number first and unprompted |
| Decision | Decides on preference | Decision traceable to a number | States expected outcome and review date in advance |
| Ownership | Blames circumstances | Accepts the outcome | Owns the outcome, diagnoses the cause, changes the system |

## Differentiation & access

- **Paper tracking is fully valid at every tier.** A wall chart updated by hand is often better
  than a spreadsheet nobody opens.
- **Learners who find spreadsheets a barrier:** Pair with a template pre-built with formulas;
  the assessed skill is interpretation, not formula authoring.
- **Anxiety around "bad numbers":** Frame explicitly and repeatedly — a bad number found early is
  a good outcome. Award **Keep Building** publicly for honest bad news, every single time.
- **Extension (Builder):** Track a fourth measure of your choice and argue whether it earned its
  place.
- **Extension (Founder):** Build a simple forecast and compare predicted to actual each week; the
  gap is more instructive than either number.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Metric theatre.** Beautiful dashboards nobody uses. Ask every week: "what did this number
  make you do?" If the answer is nothing, the metric is wrong.
- **Retyping numbers.** Where the money numbers come from the Module 3 ledger, link or copy once.
  Divergent versions of the truth cause pointless arguments.
- **The Guide making the decision.** Your instinct will be to steer them off a bad call. Let
  small bad calls happen; that is the entire point of Session 4.
- **Counting only good things.** Teams will track sales and never track cost or returns. Require
  one measure the team would prefer not to see.
- **Weekly review as a report to the adult.** It is a meeting the team runs for itself. Sit
  outside the circle.
- **Stop-rules written after emotional investment.** Write it in Session 4, before Module 9
  starts. A stop-rule invented mid-sprint is never used.

## Materials

Tally charts and big paper · tracker templates (paper and spreadsheet) · decision card stock ·
weekly review question cards · spreadsheet access (Founder) · the Module 3 ledger · wall space
for goals and decision cards.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Count Something.* Choose one thing at home to count for three days. Draw the chart.

**Builder:** *Family Numbers.* Pick three numbers that would tell you how your week went. Track
them for a week.

**Founder:** *Your Own Dashboard.* Choose three measures for something you care about outside
business — training, practice, sleep. Track for a week and make one change from what you see.

## Family note (send after Session 4)

> Your child has moved from making things to running things. They chose what to measure, looked
> at numbers they did not always like, and made a decision because of what they saw.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What number are you watching, and what is it telling you to do?"*
