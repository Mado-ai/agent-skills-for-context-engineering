# Module 5 — Pitch Like a Pro

**Icon:** Microphone · **Colour:** Sky · **Core badge:** I Can Pitch! · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Modules 1–4 · **Unlocks:** completes the Module 6 gate; required for Module 9

> A pitch is a clear promise, said out loud, by someone who means it.

## Big idea

Pitching is not performance. It is making one other person understand something quickly enough
that they can decide. Nerves are normal; being unclear is the only real failure.

## Essential questions

- What is the one thing they must remember if they forget everything else?
- What will they ask me, and do I know the answer?
- How do I sound like myself and not like a television advert?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Say three sentences about their idea to a friendly adult, loudly enough to be heard, looking up at least once |
| **Builder** | Deliver a 90-second pitch with a prop to an unfamiliar audience and answer two questions |
| **Founder** | Deliver a 5-minute pitch with visuals to outside judges, handle Q&A including a hostile question, and self-assess from video |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch | Telling someone about your idea so they get it | Spark |
| Hook | The first line that makes them listen | Builder |
| The ask | What you want them to do | Builder |
| Q&A | The questions after | Builder |
| Traction | Proof that it is already working | Founder |
| Objection | The reason they might say no | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Big voice, kind face

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Whisper to Roar.* Say your name at five volumes. Find "the back of the room"
  voice.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Three things a good teller does: **look up**, **speak out**, **smile once**.
  Guide demonstrates each one badly, then well. The bad versions get big laughs and do real work.
- **Work (25):** **Three Sentences.** Learners fill a picture frame with three prompts —
  *I made… · It helps… · You should try it because…* — and practise in pairs, three rounds,
  swapping partners.
- **Close (8):** Everyone says sentence one to the whole room, together, then three volunteers
  solo.
- **Artifact:** Three-sentence card.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Show and tell the prop

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Mystery Box.* Describe a hidden object so others can draw it.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Holding your thing up helps people understand. Show it, then talk about it
  — not both at once.
- **Work (25):** Learners practise with their prototype: hold it up, count to two, then speak.
  Rehearse in trios with a Helper giving one piece of praise and one *I wonder*.
- **Close (8):** Best "hold it up" moment of the day, chosen by the room.
- **Artifact:** Rehearsal photo or Helper's note.

### Session 3 · SHOW — The friendly grown-up

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Wobble Practice.* Everyone shakes out their nerves; name the feeling.
  "Wobbles mean you care."
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** If you forget, look at your card. Everyone forgets. Guide deliberately
  forgets mid-sentence and recovers, calmly.
- **Work (25):** Pitch to three friendly adults (staff, volunteers, older learners) in rotation.
  Each adult gives one *I like* and one *I wonder*, using the face cards.
- **Close (8):** Which adult asked the hardest question?
- **Artifact:** Feedback faces collected from three adults.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Better version

- **Warm-Up (5):** *One Small Fix.* Everyone names one thing to improve.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Practising is what makes it easier — nobody is born good at this.
- **Work (25):** Improve one sentence, rehearse twice, then perform to the whole group. Everyone
  performs. Applause for every single learner.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **I Can Pitch!** and **Show & Tell**.
- **Artifact:** Final three sentences + performance.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Anatomy of a pitch

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Rate the Pitch.* Watch or hear two short pitches, one clear and one rambling.
  Score them on "could you repeat it back?"
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The five beats in 90 seconds — **Hook** (10s) · **Problem** (20s) ·
  **Solution** (25s) · **Proof** (20s) · **Ask** (15s). Write the timings on the board and leave
  them there all module.
- **Work (35):** Draft the pitch on a five-box planner. Proof comes from Modules 1–4: what a
  customer actually said, the price test result, the three-second test score.
- **Close (10):** Hooks only, round the room. Ten seconds each. Weak hooks get one rewrite on
  the spot.
- **Artifact:** Five-box pitch planner.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Prop, timing, delivery

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Cut 20 Seconds.* Read your pitch, then remove 20 seconds of it.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Delivery mechanics: pace, pause, eye contact across three points in the
  room, hands still unless holding the prop. Then the prop rule — one prop, used once, at the
  moment it explains something words cannot.
- **Work (35):** Rehearse in threes with a timer: perform, receive one *I like* / one *I wonder*
  / one *What if*, swap. Three full rounds so everyone performs three times.
- **Close (10):** Fastest improvement of the day, named by peers.
- **Artifact:** Timed rehearsal log with peer notes.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Unfamiliar audience

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Question Storm.* Predict every question you might be asked; write each on a
  sticky.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Answering: repeat the question, answer in one sentence, stop. "I don't
  know, but I'll find out" is a strong answer and scores full marks here.
- **Work (35):** Pitch to another class or a panel of staff. Every learner pitches. Two
  questions each. A peer records the questions asked.
- **Close (10):** Which predicted questions came up? Which surprised you?
- **Artifact:** Pitch delivered + question log.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Rebuild and re-pitch

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Best Line.* Steal the best line you heard from someone else's pitch and say
  why it worked.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Revising from questions: a question asked three times means the pitch has
  a hole. Fill the hole, do not add a slide.
- **Work (35):** Rewrite, rehearse, re-pitch to a different small group. Compare the question log
  to last time.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **I Can Pitch!**, **Storyteller**, **Show & Tell**.
- **Artifact:** Pitch v2 + comparison of question logs.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Structure and evidence

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Teardown.* Watch a real 3-minute founder pitch. Map it to a structure.
  Identify the moment the audience would decide.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Five-minute structure — hook, problem with evidence, solution demo,
  market and model, traction, team, ask. Traction is the section that separates a plan from a
  business: what has actually happened, in numbers.
- **Work (50):** Build the outline. Every claim must have a source from Modules 1–4 written next
  to it. Claims without a source are struck through, not softened.
- **Close (15):** Read your traction slide only. The room challenges any unsourced number.
- **Artifact:** Sourced pitch outline.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Visuals and demo

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Slide or Sentence?* For five pieces of information, decide which needs a
  visual and which does not.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Slide discipline: one idea per slide, six words maximum on screen,
  numbers big, brand type and colour, no slide you have to read aloud. Then demo discipline:
  under 60 seconds, rehearsed, with a fallback if the demo fails.
- **Work (50):** Build the deck (max 7 slides) and the demo. Record a first full run on video.
- **Close (15):** Watch 60 seconds of your own video. Write down two things you did not know you
  did.
- **Artifact:** 7-slide deck + demo plan + video v1.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Judges and hostile Q&A

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Objection Drill.* In pairs, one attacks — "nobody would pay for this",
  "someone bigger will copy you", "your numbers are wrong" — the other answers in one calm
  sentence.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Handling Q&A: acknowledge, answer, evidence, stop. Never argue with the
  premise before answering the question. Never make up a number.
- **Work (50):** Full pitches to outside judges — mentors, local business owners, senior staff.
  Five minutes plus five minutes Q&A. Video recorded with consent. Judges score on a published
  rubric and give one strength and one gap.
- **Close (15):** Judges' overall observation to the room.
- **Artifact:** Recorded pitch + judge scores + Q&A notes.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Self-assess and finalise

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Two Minutes of Tape.* Each learner watches two minutes of their own pitch
  in silence.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Self-assessment against the rubric. Identify the weakest of: clarity,
  evidence, delivery, Q&A. Fix one, deeply, rather than four, shallowly.
- **Work (50):** Revise and deliver a final 2-minute version to peers, then record a 60-second
  version for the portfolio and Demo Day trailer.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **I Can Pitch!**, **Storyteller**, **Show & Tell**, **Keep
  Building** where a learner re-pitched after a hard round.
- **Artifact:** Final 60-second recorded pitch + written self-assessment.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **I Can Pitch!** (core) | Delivered a pitch to an audience outside their team and answered at least one question | 25 |
| **Storyteller** | Structured the pitch so a listener could repeat the problem and solution back correctly | 25 |
| **Show & Tell** | Presented five times across the program (cumulative; often completes here) | 25 |
| **Keep Building** | Went again after a pitch that went badly, with a specific change | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**I Can Pitch! is a prerequisite for Module 9 at Builder and Founder tiers.**

**Rubric — the pitch:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Listener cannot say what it is | Listener can repeat problem and solution | Listener can repeat it *and* who it is for, unprompted |
| Evidence | Claims only | Some claims sourced | Every claim sourced; unknowns named honestly |
| Delivery | Inaudible or unbroken reading | Audible, some eye contact, roughly on time | Paced, present, on time, sounds like themselves |
| Q&A | Freezes or invents | Answers plainly, admits gaps | Answers with evidence; uses a hard question to improve the pitch |

## Differentiation & access

- **Selective mutism, severe anxiety, or speech differences:** A pre-recorded pitch, a
  signed pitch, a pitch delivered to one person with the group listening from outside the room,
  or a pitch read by a partner while the learner holds the prop all earn full **I Can Pitch!**
  credit. The criterion is *communicated to an audience outside your team*, not *spoke aloud on
  a stage*.
- **Cue cards are always allowed**, at every tier, including Founder. Reading from cards is not
  a deduction; reading *only* from cards without ever looking up is a delivery note.
- **EAL learners:** Pitch in a first language with a translated one-line summary, or use a
  bilingual partner. Full credit.
- **Extension (Builder):** Pitch the same idea to two different audiences and change the hook
  for each.
- **Extension (Founder):** Deliver the pitch in 60 seconds, then in 20 seconds. The 20-second
  version reveals what actually matters.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Scripted, memorised, and dead.** Memorising word-for-word is fragile and sounds robotic.
  Teach the five beats and let the words vary each time.
- **Praising confidence over clarity.** The loudest, most theatrical pitch is often the least
  informative. Score against the rubric, out loud, so learners see what is valued.
- **The pile-on.** Peer feedback on pitches can turn cruel fast. The three-part frame is
  mandatory and the Guide interrupts anything outside it immediately.
- **Forcing a reluctant learner onto a stage.** It does lasting damage and is never necessary —
  use the access routes above. Nobody is publicly opted out; offer choices to everyone.
- **Judges who lecture.** Brief outside judges in writing: one strength, one gap, questions
  not advice, under 90 seconds. Otherwise Session 3 becomes forty minutes of adult monologue.
- **Video consent.** Check the media consent list *before* recording, not after. One
  unconsented learner means the camera stays off for that pitch.

## Materials

Timers · index cards for cue cards · props (their prototypes) · microphone if the space needs it
· video camera or tablet + tripod · judge rubric sheets · pitch planner templates · projector
(Founder) · consent list.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Pitch the Family.* Say your three sentences at dinner. The family gives one *I like*
and one *I wonder*.

**Builder:** *90 Seconds, Two Audiences.* Pitch to a grown-up and to a younger child. Which words
had to change?

**Founder:** *The Hostile Question.* Ask someone at home to give you the hardest question they
can about your business. Write down your answer, then improve it.

## Family note (send after Session 4)

> Pitching frightens most adults. Your child stood up, explained their idea to people they did
> not know, took questions, and then did it again better.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What was the hardest question anyone asked you — and what did you say?"*
>
> If the answer was "I don't know, I'll find out," that is a win. We teach that as the correct
> answer when it is the true one.
