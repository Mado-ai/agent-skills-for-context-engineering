# Course 5 — Pitch Like a Pro

**Icon:** Microphone · **Colour:** Sky · **Core badge:** I Can Pitch! · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Courses 1–4 · **Required for Course 9** · **Français:** Présente comme un Pro

> A pitch is a clear promise, said out loud, by someone who means it.

## Big idea

Pitching is not performance. It is making one other person understand something quickly enough
that they can decide. Nerves are normal; being unclear is the only real failure.

## Essential questions

- What is the one thing they must remember if they forget everything else?
- What will they ask me, and do I know the answer?
- How do I sound like myself and not like a television advert?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| 🌱 **Little Sprouts** | Say three sentences to a friendly adult, loudly enough to be heard, looking up at least once |
| 🔨 **Junior Builders** | Deliver a 60-second pitch with a prop to another class and answer one question |
| 🚀 **Young Founders** | Deliver a 3-minute pitch with proof from Courses 1–4 to a visiting panel and handle two questions |
| 👑 **Founder CEOs** | Deliver a 5-minute pitch with visuals to outside judges, handle a hostile question, and self-assess from video |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | From tier |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch | Telling someone about your idea so they get it | Sprouts |
| Hook | The first line that makes them listen | Builders |
| The ask | What you want them to do | Builders |
| Q&A | The questions after | Builders |
| Proof | The evidence behind a claim | Founders |
| Traction | Proof that it is already working | CEOs |
| Objection | The reason they might say no | CEOs |

## 🌱 Little Sprouts (5–7) — 4 × 45 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Big voice, kind face

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Whisper to Roar.* Say your name at five volumes. Find "the back of the room."
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Three things a good teller does: **look up**, **speak out**, **smile
  once**. Guide demonstrates each badly, then well. The bad versions get big laughs and do real
  work.
- **Work (25):** **Three Sentences.** Fill a picture frame with three prompts — *I made… · It
  helps… · You should try it because…* — and practise in pairs, three rounds, swapping partners.
- **Close (8):** Everyone says sentence one together, then three volunteers solo.
- **Artifact:** Three-sentence card.

### S2 · MAKE — Show and tell the prop

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Mystery Box.* Describe a hidden object so others can draw it.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Hold it up, count to two, then talk. Not both at once.
- **Work (25):** Practise with your prototype in trios; a Helper gives one *I like* and one *I
  wonder*.
- **Close (8):** Best "hold it up" moment of the day, chosen by the room.
- **Artifact:** Rehearsal note from a Helper.

### S3 · SHOW — The friendly grown-up

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Wobble Practice.* Shake out the nerves. "Wobbles mean you care."
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** If you forget, look at your card. Everyone forgets — Guide forgets
  mid-sentence on purpose and recovers calmly.
- **Work (25):** Pitch to three friendly adults in rotation. Each gives one *I like* and one *I
  wonder* using the face cards.
- **Close (8):** Which adult asked the hardest question?
- **Artifact:** Feedback faces from three adults.

### S4 · CHANGE — Better version

- **Warm-Up (5):** *One Small Fix.*
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Practising is what makes it easier. Nobody is born good at this.
- **Work (25):** Improve one sentence, rehearse twice, perform to the whole group. Everyone
  performs. Applause for every single learner.
- **Close (8):** Badge check: **I Can Pitch!**, **Show & Tell**.
- **Artifact:** Final three sentences + performance.

## 🔨 Junior Builders (8–10) — 4 × 60 min

### S1 · NOTICE — What a pitch is made of

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Rate the Pitch.* Two short pitches, one clear and one rambling. Score them on
  "could you repeat it back?"
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Four beats in 60 seconds — **Hook** (10s) · **Problem** (15s) ·
  **Solution** (20s) · **Ask** (15s). Write the timings on the board and leave them up all
  course.
- **Work (35):** Draft on a four-box planner. Then say it once, timed, and cut whatever pushed it
  over.
- **Close (10):** Hooks only, round the room, ten seconds each. Weak hooks get one rewrite on the
  spot.
- **Artifact:** Four-box planner + a timed run.

### S2 · MAKE — Prop and delivery

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Cut Ten Seconds.*
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Pace, pause, eye contact at three points in the room, hands still unless
  holding the prop. One prop, used once, at the moment words cannot do the job.
- **Work (35):** Rehearse in threes with a timer: perform, receive one *I like* / one *I wonder*
  / one *What if*, swap. Three rounds so everyone performs three times.
- **Close (10):** Biggest improvement of the day, named by peers.
- **Artifact:** Timed rehearsal log with peer notes.

### S3 · SHOW — Another class

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Question Storm.* Predict every question you might be asked.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Repeat the question, answer in one sentence, stop. "I don't know, but
  I'll find out" is a strong answer and scores full marks.
- **Work (35):** Pitch to another class. Everyone pitches. One question each. A peer records the
  questions asked.
- **Close (10):** Which predicted questions came up?
- **Artifact:** Pitch delivered + question log.

### S4 · CHANGE — Rebuild and re-pitch

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Best Line.* Steal the best line you heard from someone else and say why it
  worked.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** A question asked three times means the pitch has a hole. Fill the hole;
  do not add a sentence.
- **Work (35):** Rewrite, rehearse, re-pitch to a different small group. Compare question logs.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **I Can Pitch!**, **Storyteller**, **Show & Tell**.
- **Artifact:** Pitch v2 + comparison of question logs.

## 🚀 Young Founders (11–13) — 4 × 75 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Proof is the difference

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Claim or Proof?* Sort ten statements. "Kids love it" vs. "9 of 12 signed up."
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Five beats in three minutes — **Hook** (15s) · **Problem with evidence**
  (40s) · **Solution** (50s) · **Proof** (45s) · **Ask** (30s). The Proof section is what
  separates this tier from Junior Builders.
- **Work (42):** Build the outline. Every claim gets its source written beside it, drawn from
  Courses 1–4: an interview quote, a price test result, a response rate. Unsourced claims are
  struck through, not softened.
- **Close (13):** Read your Proof section only. The room challenges anything unsourced.
- **Artifact:** Sourced pitch outline.

### S2 · MAKE — Rehearse it into shape

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Twenty Seconds.* Tell the whole thing in twenty. What survived is what
  matters.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Memorise the beats, not the words — word-for-word scripts are fragile and
  sound robotic. Then the prop or one visual, used once.
- **Work (42):** Three timed rehearsal rounds in threes with the feedback frame. Record one run on
  audio and listen back.
- **Close (13):** Name the one sentence you will change before Session 3.
- **Artifact:** Rehearsal log + audio run.

### S3 · SHOW — A visiting panel

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Question Storm*, then rank the questions by how much they scare you.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Acknowledge, answer, evidence, stop. Never argue with the premise before
  answering. Never invent a number.
- **Work (42):** Pitch to a visiting panel — staff, mentors, older learners. Three minutes plus
  two questions each. A peer logs every question asked across the room.
- **Close (13):** The panel names the strongest proof it heard.
- **Artifact:** Pitch delivered + question log + panel notes.

### S4 · CHANGE — Fix the hole

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Three Times Means a Hole.* Find the question the room heard most.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Revising from questions rather than from taste. Then the 60-second version
  for Market Day, where nobody has three minutes.
- **Work (42):** Rewrite, then produce and rehearse the 60-second Market Day version. Re-pitch
  both to a different group.
- **Close (13):** Badge check: **I Can Pitch!**, **Storyteller**, **Show & Tell**, **Keep
  Building**.
- **Artifact:** Pitch v2 + a rehearsed 60-second version.

## 👑 Founder CEOs (14–17) — 4 × 90 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Structure and traction

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Teardown.* Watch a real 3-minute founder pitch. Map its structure. Identify
  the moment the audience would decide.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Five-minute structure — hook, problem with evidence, solution demo,
  market and model, traction, team, ask. Traction separates a plan from a business: what has
  actually happened, in numbers.
- **Work (50):** Build the outline. Every claim gets a source. Unsourced claims are struck
  through.
- **Close (15):** Read your traction slide only; the room challenges unsourced numbers.
- **Artifact:** Sourced pitch outline.

### S2 · MAKE — Visuals and demo

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Slide or Sentence?*
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** One idea per slide, six words on screen, numbers big, brand type and
  colour, no slide you have to read aloud. Demo under 60 seconds, rehearsed, with a fallback if
  it fails.
- **Work (50):** Build the deck (max 7 slides) and the demo. Record a full run on video.
- **Close (15):** Watch 60 seconds of your own video. Write two things you did not know you did.
- **Artifact:** Seven-slide deck + demo plan + video v1.

### S3 · SHOW — Judges and hostile Q&A

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Objection Drill.* One partner attacks — "nobody would pay for this",
  "someone bigger will copy you", "your numbers are wrong" — the other answers in one calm
  sentence.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Acknowledge, answer, evidence, stop. Handling the question you cannot
  answer without inventing anything.
- **Work (50):** Full pitches to outside judges — mentors, local business owners, senior staff.
  Five minutes plus five of Q&A. Video recorded with consent. Judges score on a published rubric
  and give one strength and one gap.
- **Close (15):** Judges' overall observation to the room.
- **Artifact:** Recorded pitch + judge scores + Q&A notes.

### S4 · CHANGE — Self-assess and finalise

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Two Minutes of Tape*, watched in silence.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Self-assess against the rubric. Identify the weakest of clarity, evidence,
  delivery, Q&A. Fix one deeply rather than four shallowly.
- **Work (50):** Revise, deliver a final 2-minute version to peers, then record a 60-second
  version for the portfolio and the Demo Day trailer.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **I Can Pitch!**, **Storyteller**, **Show & Tell**, **Keep
  Building** where a learner re-pitched after a hard round.
- **Artifact:** Final 60-second recorded pitch + written self-assessment.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **I Can Pitch!** (core) | Delivered a pitch to an audience outside their team and answered at least one question | 25 |
| **Storyteller** | Structured it so a listener could repeat the problem and solution back correctly | 25 |
| **Show & Tell** | Fifth presentation completed (cumulative across the program) | 25 |
| **Keep Building** | Went again after a pitch that went badly, with a specific change | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the course).

**I Can Pitch! is a prerequisite for Course 9** from Junior Builders upward.

**Rubric — the pitch:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Listener cannot say what it is | Listener can repeat problem and solution | …and who it is for, unprompted |
| Evidence | Claims only | Some claims sourced | Every claim sourced; unknowns named honestly |
| Delivery | Inaudible, or unbroken reading | Audible, some eye contact, roughly on time | Paced, present, on time, sounds like themselves |
| Q&A | Freezes or invents | Answers plainly, admits gaps | Uses a hard question to improve the pitch |

## Differentiation & access

- **Selective mutism, severe anxiety, speech differences:** A pre-recorded pitch, a signed pitch,
  a pitch to one person with the group listening from outside the room, or a partner reading
  while the learner holds the prop all earn full **I Can Pitch!** credit. The criterion is
  *communicated to an audience outside your team*, not *spoke aloud on a stage*.
- **Cue cards are always allowed**, at every tier, including Founder CEOs. Reading from cards is
  not a deduction; never looking up is a delivery note.
- **Bilingual and EAL learners:** Pitch in the language you think fastest in, with a translated
  one-line summary. Full credit. Judges are briefed not to score language.
- **Extension (Builders):** Pitch to two different audiences and change the hook for each.
- **Extension (Founders):** Deliver in 60 seconds, then 20. The 20 reveals what actually matters.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Scripted, memorised, and dead.** Teach the beats and let the words vary each time.
- **Praising confidence over clarity.** The loudest pitch is often the least informative. Score
  against the rubric out loud so learners see what is valued.
- **The pile-on.** Peer feedback on pitches turns cruel fast. The three-part frame is mandatory
  and the Guide interrupts anything outside it immediately.
- **Forcing a reluctant learner onto a stage.** It does lasting damage and is never necessary.
  Offer the access routes to everyone so nobody is visibly opted out.
- **Judges who lecture.** Brief outside judges in writing: one strength, one gap, questions not
  advice, under 90 seconds. Otherwise Session 3 becomes forty minutes of adult monologue.
- **Video consent.** Check the consent list *before* recording. One unconsented learner means the
  camera is off for that pitch.

## Materials

Timers · index cards for cue cards · props (their prototypes) · video camera or tablet + tripod ·
judge rubric sheets · pitch planner templates · projector (Founders, CEOs) · consent list ·
microphone if the room needs it.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**🌱 Sprouts:** *Pitch the Family.* Say your three sentences at dinner. One *I like*, one *I
wonder*.

**🔨 Builders:** *Sixty Seconds, Two Audiences.* Pitch to a grown-up and to a younger child. Which
words had to change?

**🚀 Young Founders:** *Find the Proof.* Ask someone at home which part of your pitch they
believed least. Then find evidence for it.

**👑 Founder CEOs:** *The Hostile Question.* Ask someone at home for the hardest question they can
about your business. Write your answer, then improve it.

## Family note (after Session 4)

> Pitching frightens most adults. Your child stood up, explained their idea to people they did not
> know, took questions, and then did it again better.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What was the hardest question anyone asked you — and what did you say?"*
>
> If the answer was "I don't know, I'll find out," that is a win. We teach that as the correct
> answer when it is the true one.
