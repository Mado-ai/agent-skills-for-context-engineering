# Course 3 — Money Smarts

**Icon:** Growth chart · **Colour:** Teal · **Core badge:** Money Smart · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Courses 1–2 · **Required for Course 9** · **Français:** Génie de l'Argent

> Money in, money out, money kept. Everything else is detail.

## Big idea

Price is not a guess, and profit is not the same as the pile of cash in your hand. If you know
what one unit costs you and what one customer pays, you know whether you have a business.

All money in this course is Canadian dollars. Learners handle real coins and bills from Junior
Builders upward, under the rules in
[05-standards-and-safeguarding.md](../05-standards-and-safeguarding.md).

## Essential questions

- Where does money come from and where does it go?
- What does one of my things actually cost me to make?
- How much should I charge, and how do I explain that number to someone who thinks it is too
  much?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| 🌱 **Little Sprouts** | Sort money into *in* and *out*, count small amounts, and explain saving vs. spending with their own example |
| 🔨 **Junior Builders** | Calculate the cost of one unit, set a price, work out profit per unit, and count real takings correctly |
| 🚀 **Young Founders** | Calculate margin, forecast three sales scenarios, test a price on real people, and revise it |
| 👑 **Founder CEOs** | Build unit economics, calculate break-even, keep a ledger, and explain why a profitable business can still run out of cash |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | From tier |
|---|---|---|
| Money in / money out | What you receive / what you spend | Sprouts |
| Saving | Money you keep for later on purpose | Sprouts |
| Cost | What it takes to make one | Builders |
| Price | What the customer pays | Builders |
| Profit | Price minus cost. The money you keep | Builders |
| Margin | Profit as a share of the price | Founders |
| Forecast | What we think will happen, in numbers | Founders |
| Fixed / variable cost | Stays the same / changes with how many you make | CEOs |
| Break-even | How many you must sell before you stop losing money | CEOs |
| Cash flow | Money actually moving, and when | CEOs |

## 🌱 Little Sprouts (5–7) — 4 × 45 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Two jars

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Coin Rain.* Sort a pile of Canadian coins by size and colour, then by value
  with help.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Two jars: **IN** (Teal) and **OUT** (Energy). Guide narrates a day at a
  lemonade stand, dropping coins into each.
- **Work (25):** **My Two Jars.** Decorate paper jars, then play five rounds: a card is drawn
  ("you sold three cakes", "you bought flour"), coins move. Count the IN jar.
- **Close (8):** Which jar do you want to be bigger? Why?
- **Artifact:** Decorated jars + final count drawing.

### S2 · NOTICE — Where money goes

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Price Guess.* Guess the price of five everyday objects. Celebrate the wildest
  guess.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Everything you sell costs you something first — even lemonade needs
  lemons.
- **Work (25):** **What Does It Cost?** Using picture-cards priced in whole coins, build a simple
  recipe for your product and count what one costs.
- **Close (8):** Hands up: whose thing costs more than three coins?
- **Artifact:** Picture recipe with total cost.

### S3 · MAKE / SHOW — Setting a price

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Too Much, Too Little, Just Right.*
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Your price has to be *more* than your cost, or you are giving things away.
  Show it with the two jars.
- **Work (25):** Choose a price with coin cards, then run a mini market with play money — five
  buyers each. Count what came in.
- **Close (8):** Compare the IN pile to the cost. Bigger or smaller?
- **Artifact:** Price card + sales tally.

### S4 · CHANGE — Saving and choosing

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Want or Need?* Sort ten picture cards.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Three choices for money you keep: spend it, save it, share it.
- **Work (25):** Split play earnings into three envelopes — **Spend**, **Save**, **Share** — and
  draw what each is for. The Share envelope is the first taste of Course 8.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Money Smart**.
- **Artifact:** Three envelopes with drawings.

## 🔨 Junior Builders (8–10) — 4 × 60 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Cost of one

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Hidden Costs.* List everything a birthday party costs. Race to 20 items.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Cost of *one unit*. Bulk buying is a beginner's trap: you bought 100
  cups, but one cup costs the total ÷ 100 — and the other 99 are money sitting on a shelf.
- **Work (35):** Build a **Cost Sheet**: every input, quantity, pack price, cost per unit.
  Include your time at a rate the group agrees (e.g. $5/hour) — it is not free.
- **Close (10):** Post each team's cost per unit. Reactions are usually loud.
- **Artifact:** Cost sheet with cost per unit.

### S2 · MAKE — Setting a price

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Price Ladder.* Same item at four prices; where do you stop buying?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Two ways to price — **cost-plus** (cost × a number) and **compare** (what
  similar things cost). Use both, then choose.
- **Work (35):** Set a price both ways, compare the two numbers, pick one with a written reason.
  Calculate profit per unit and profit if you sell 20.
- **Close (10):** Price defence — one team presents, the room challenges. The Guide protects the
  presenter from a pile-on.
- **Artifact:** Price with working and a written defence.

### S3 · SHOW — Handling real money

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Make the Change.* Practise giving change from a $10 bill, fast.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The money rules: two people count, one adult witnesses, all three
  initial the sheet. Count at the start, the middle, and the end. A discrepancy is investigated,
  never adjusted.
- **Work (35):** Run a 20-minute practice sale to another class with a real float. Handle real
  money, give real change, keep a takings sheet.
- **Close (10):** Count and reconcile together. Did it match?
- **Artifact:** Takings sheet, reconciled.

### S4 · CHANGE — Money map and the split rule

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Profit or Cash?* Two short scenarios; which team has money problems?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Money map: float in → costs out → sales in → profit. Then the group
  **split rule** for Market Day proceeds: what share is reinvested, shared, and given away. The
  group votes; the decision is recorded and holds through Course 9.
- **Work (35):** Rebuild the cost sheet and price after the practice sale. Forecast: if we sell
  10 / 25 / 50, how much do we keep?
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Money Smart**, **Number Cruncher**. Split rule on the wall.
- **Artifact:** Revised cost sheet + forecast + signed split rule.

## 🚀 Young Founders (11–13) — 4 × 75 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Cost, price, margin

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Guess the Margin.* Five real products; guess what share of the price is
  profit. Coffee shocks everyone.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Cost per unit including labour and waste, then margin = profit ÷ price.
  Why a thin margin means one mistake wipes out the year.
- **Work (42):** Build a cost sheet with a waste line (things that get ruined) and a labour line
  at minimum wage. Calculate profit per unit and margin.
- **Close (13):** Post margins. Whose is too thin to survive a mistake?
- **Artifact:** Cost sheet with margin.

### S2 · MAKE — Price with a strategy

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Raise or Cut?* Thin margins, slow sales. Argue both moves.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Three pricing methods — cost-plus, compare, and **value** (what it is
  worth to them). Best answers triangulate all three. Then bundles and tiers as options.
- **Work (42):** Price by all three methods, compare the three numbers, choose one, and write the
  defence referencing your Course 2 switching line.
- **Close (13):** Two teams defend; the room challenges.
- **Artifact:** Three-method pricing with a written defence.

### S3 · SHOW — Test the price

- **Warm-Up (8):** *The Wince.* Practise noticing the face people make when a price is too high.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Never ask "would you buy this?" Ask "what would you expect to pay?" then
  "would you buy it at [your price]?" and watch the face.
- **Work (42):** Price test with at least 12 people outside the team. Record expected price,
  yes/no at your price, and the wince. Then recalculate profit at the price people accepted.
- **Close (13):** Which prices survived? Which must change?
- **Artifact:** Price test log, 12+ responses.

### S4 · CHANGE — Forecast and split rule

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Forecast Roulette.* What happens to profit if you sell half what you hoped?
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** A three-scenario forecast — low, expected, high — and why the low one is
  the one to plan against. Then the split rule vote.
- **Work (42):** Rebuild costs and price after testing. Build the three-scenario forecast. Agree
  and sign the split rule for Course 9 proceeds.
- **Close (13):** Badge check: **Money Smart**, **Number Cruncher**, **Kind Business**.
- **Artifact:** Revised pricing + three-scenario forecast + signed split rule.

## 👑 Founder CEOs (14–17) — 4 × 90 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Unit economics

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Guess the Margin*, then reveal real figures across five industries.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Fixed vs. variable costs. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost.
  Margin = contribution ÷ price.
- **Work (50):** Build a unit economics sheet in a spreadsheet: every variable line, every fixed
  line, contribution, margin. Include labour honestly at provincial minimum wage even if the
  founders are not paying themselves.
- **Close (15):** Post margins; discuss whose is too thin to absorb a mistake.
- **Artifact:** Unit economics spreadsheet.

### S2 · MAKE — Break-even and strategy

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Raise or Cut?*
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit. Draw the graph.
  Then pricing strategy: penetration, premium, bundle, tiered — with a real example of each.
- **Work (50):** Calculate break-even. Model three price scenarios and their break-even points.
  Write which strategy you are choosing and why, referencing your Course 2 positioning line.
- **Close (15):** State break-even in units and in days of selling.
- **Artifact:** Break-even + three-scenario model + strategy note.

### S3 · SHOW — The ledger and a real transaction

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Find the Error.* A ledger with three planted mistakes; teams race.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Date, description, in, out, running balance. Receipts for everything. Why
  cash ≠ profit — a worked example where a profitable order bankrupts a team because they paid
  the supplier 30 days before the customer paid them.
- **Work (50):** Set up the venture ledger. Record the float and any spend. Then run a real small
  transaction — a pre-order, a deposit, a first sale — and record it properly.
- **Close (15):** Ledgers cross-checked against receipts by a partner team.
- **Artifact:** Ledger with ≥ 3 real entries and matching receipts.

### S4 · CHANGE — Cash plan and decisions

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Runway.* You have $80 and spend $12 a week. Now add a $30 sale in week 3.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** A six-week cash plan: opening balance, expected in, expected out, closing
  balance, week by week. The week the balance goes negative is the week you must act.
- **Work (50):** Build the cash plan. Identify the pinch week. Write two decisions that would fix
  it — cut a cost, raise the price, pre-sell, change payment terms — and pick one.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Money Smart**, **Number Cruncher**. Split rule signed.
- **Artifact:** Six-week cash plan + decision note + signed split rule.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Money Smart** (core) | Sprouts: sorts in/out and explains save vs. spend · Builders: correct cost per unit, defended price, reconciled takings · Founders: margin + tested price + forecast · CEOs: unit economics + break-even + clean ledger | 25 |
| **Number Cruncher** | A correct, self-checked calculation that changed a decision, walked through unaided | 25 |
| **Kind Business** | Contributed to the split rule with a reason beyond self-interest | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the course).

**Money Smart is a prerequisite for Course 9** from Junior Builders upward. A learner who is
short runs the S1–S2 catch-up (cost sheet + defended price) with a Helper.

**Rubric — the money artifact:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Costs | Missing obvious inputs | All inputs listed, per-unit correct | Includes time, waste, and a contingency line |
| Price | Guessed | Cost-plus with a reason | Triangulated across methods and tested with real people |
| Accuracy | Errors change the conclusion | Minor errors, conclusion holds | Correct and self-checked before submission |
| Judgement | Reports numbers only | Says what they mean | Names the decision the numbers force, including bad news |

## Differentiation & access

- **Calculators and spreadsheets are allowed at every tier.** This course assesses reasoning
  about money, not arithmetic fluency.
- **Maths anxiety:** Start from the two-jar model at any age — it works at 15 as well as 6. Give
  the cost sheet pre-filled with two worked lines.
- **Money-sensitive families:** Never use family spending as an example. All scenarios are
  business scenarios. See the safeguarding note on money conversations.
- **Extension (Builders):** Model what happens if a key ingredient doubles in price.
- **Extension (Founders):** Add a bundle option and compare its margin to the single unit.
- **Extension (CEOs):** Model the venture with a 20% refund rate, or add a second revenue stream.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Free labour.** Teams price as if their time costs nothing, then are shocked in Course 9 when
  the work is exhausting. Insist on a labour line even at a symbolic rate.
- **The bulk-buying trap.** "The pack was $10 so it cost $10" is the most common Junior Builders
  error. Catch it on the cost sheet, before they price.
- **Underpricing to be nice.** Very common where the customers are friends. Frame it as fairness:
  an unsustainable price is a promise you cannot keep.
- **Profit treated as pocket money.** Settle the split rule *before* Course 9, or it becomes an
  argument on Market Day when everyone is tired.
- **Founders and CEOs modelling in their heads.** Require the sheet. Mental models hide the pinch
  week.
- **Real money handling.** Two count, one adult witnesses, everyone initials. Every time. Rule,
  not suggestion.

## Materials

Canadian coins and small bills (real or realistic) · paper jars and envelopes · cost sheet
templates · calculators · spreadsheet access (Founders, CEOs) · receipt book · cash tin with a
two-signature log · price test recording sheets · ledger template.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**🌱 Sprouts:** *Save Jar.* Choose one small thing to save for. Draw it on a jar and add a mark
each time something goes in.

**🔨 Builders:** *Shelf Detective.* Find one item at home. Estimate what it cost to make. Compare
with its price. Where does the difference go?

**🚀 Young Founders:** *The Price Question.* Ask an adult about something they thought was
overpriced and bought anyway. Why did they?

**👑 Founder CEOs:** *Receipt Audit.* Ask to see a receipt for something bought often. Which parts
of the price are likely cost, and which are margin?

## Family note (after Session 4)

> Money Smarts changes how children look at prices. Your child worked out what their product
> costs to make, set a price they can defend, and tested it on real people.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What does one of your things cost you to make — and what are you
> charging?"*
>
> If the second number is not comfortably bigger than the first, ask what they plan to do about
> it. That is exactly the conversation we want them having.
