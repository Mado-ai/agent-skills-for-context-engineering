# Module 3 — Money Smarts

**Icon:** Growth chart · **Colour:** Teal · **Core badge:** Money Smart · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Modules 1–2 · **Unlocks:** counts toward Leadership Lab; required for Module 9

> Money in, money out, money kept. Everything else is detail.

## Big idea

Price is not a guess and profit is not the same as the pile of cash in your hand. If you know
what one unit costs you and what one customer pays, you know whether you have a business.

## Essential questions

- Where does money come from and where does it go?
- What does one of my things actually cost me to make?
- How much should I charge, and how do I explain that number to someone who thinks it is too
  much?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Sort money into *in* and *out*, count and make small amounts, and explain saving vs. spending with their own example |
| **Builder** | Calculate the cost of one unit, set a price, work out profit per unit, and defend the price with a reason |
| **Founder** | Build unit economics, calculate break-even, keep a ledger, and explain why a profitable business can still run out of cash |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Money in / money out | What you receive / what you spend | Spark |
| Saving | Money you keep for later on purpose | Spark |
| Cost | What it takes to make one | Builder |
| Price | What the customer pays | Builder |
| Profit | Price minus cost. The money you keep | Builder |
| Fixed / variable cost | Costs that stay the same / change with how many you make | Founder |
| Break-even | How many you must sell before you stop losing money | Founder |
| Cash flow | Money actually moving, and when | Founder |
| Margin | Profit as a share of price | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Two jars

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Coin Rain.* Sort a pile of coins by size and colour, then by value with help.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Two jars on the table: **IN** (Teal) and **OUT** (Energy). Guide narrates
  a day at a lemonade stand, dropping coins into each jar.
- **Work (25):** **My Two Jars.** Learners decorate their own paper jars, then play a
  five-round game: a card is drawn ("you sold three cakes", "you bought flour"), coins move.
  Count the IN jar at the end.
- **Close (8):** Which jar do you want to be bigger? Why?
- **Artifact:** Decorated jars + final count drawing.

### Session 2 · NOTICE — Where money goes

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Price Guess.* Guess the price of five everyday objects. Reveal. Celebrate
  the wildest guess.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Everything you sell costs you something first — even lemonade needs
  lemons.
- **Work (25):** **What Does It Cost?** Using picture-cards with prices in whole coins, learners
  build a simple recipe for their product and count what it costs to make one.
- **Close (8):** Hands up: whose thing costs more than three coins to make?
- **Artifact:** Picture recipe with total cost.

### Session 3 · MAKE / SHOW — Setting a price

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Too Much, Too Little, Just Right.* Guide names a price for a familiar item;
  learners shout which it is.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Your price has to be *more* than your cost, or you are giving your things
  away. Show it with the two jars.
- **Work (25):** Learners choose a price for their product using coin cards, then run a mini
  market with play money — five buyers each. Count what came in.
- **Close (8):** Compare the IN pile to the cost. Bigger or smaller?
- **Artifact:** Price card + sales tally.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Saving and choosing

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Want or Need?* Sort ten picture cards.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Three choices for money you keep: spend it, save it, share it.
- **Work (25):** Learners split their play earnings into three envelopes — **Spend**, **Save**,
  **Share** — and draw what each is for. The Share envelope is the group's first taste of
  Module 8.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Money Smart**.
- **Artifact:** Three envelopes with drawings.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Cost of one

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Hidden Costs.* List everything a birthday party costs. Race to 20 items.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Cost of one unit. Bulk buying is a trap for beginners: you bought 100
  cups, but one cup costs you the total ÷ 100 — and the other 99 are money sitting on a shelf.
- **Work (35):** Teams build a **Cost Sheet**: every input, quantity, pack price, cost per unit.
  Include time as a line item at a rate the group agrees (e.g. $5/hour) — it is not free.
- **Close (10):** Post each team's cost-per-unit. Reactions are usually loud.
- **Artifact:** Cost sheet with cost per unit.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Setting and defending a price

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Price Ladder.* Same item at four prices; where do you stop buying?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Three ways to price — **cost-plus** (cost × a number), **compare** (what
  similar things cost), **value** (what it is worth to them). Best answer usually uses all
  three.
- **Work (35):** Each team sets a price by all three methods, compares the three numbers, and
  picks one with a written reason. Then calculate profit per unit and profit if they sell 20.
- **Close (10):** Price defence — one team presents, the room challenges. Guide protects the
  presenter from pile-on.
- **Artifact:** Price with three-method working and a written defence.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Test the price

- **Warm-Up (5):** *The Wince.* Practise noticing the face people make when a price is too high.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Never ask "would you buy this?" Ask "what would you expect to pay?" then
  "would you buy it at [your price]?" and watch the face.
- **Work (35):** Price test with at least 10 people outside the team. Record: expected price,
  yes/no at your price, and the wince. Then recalculate profit at the price people actually
  accepted.
- **Close (10):** Which teams' prices survived? Which have to change?
- **Artifact:** Price test log, 10+ responses.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Money map and the split rule

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Profit or Cash?* Two short scenarios; which team has money problems?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Money map: float in → costs out → sales in → profit. Introduce the group
  **split rule** for Market Day proceeds: what share is reinvested, shared, and given away. The
  group votes; the decision is recorded and holds through Module 9.
- **Work (35):** Teams rebuild the cost sheet and price after testing, then forecast: if we sell
  10 / 25 / 50, how much do we keep? Simple three-column table.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Money Smart**, **Number Cruncher**. Split rule posted on the wall.
- **Artifact:** Revised cost sheet + 10/25/50 forecast + signed split rule.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Unit economics

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Guess the Margin.* Five real products; guess what share of the price is
  profit. Reveal typical figures. Coffee shocks everyone.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Fixed vs. variable costs. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost.
  Margin = contribution ÷ price.
- **Work (50):** Build a unit economics sheet in a spreadsheet: every variable cost line, every
  fixed cost line, contribution, margin. Include labour honestly at minimum wage even if the
  founders are not paying themselves.
- **Close (15):** Post margins. Discuss: whose margin is too thin to survive a mistake?
- **Artifact:** Unit economics spreadsheet.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Break-even and pricing strategy

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Raise or Cut?* A team has thin margins and slow sales. Argue both moves.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit. Draw the graph.
  Then pricing strategy: penetration, premium, bundle, tiered — with a real example of each.
- **Work (50):** Calculate break-even. Then model three price scenarios and their break-even
  points. Write which strategy they are choosing and why, referencing their Module 2
  positioning line.
- **Close (15):** Each team states its break-even number in units and in days of selling.
- **Artifact:** Break-even calculation + three-scenario model + strategy note.

### Session 3 · SHOW — The ledger and a real transaction

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Find the Error.* A ledger with three planted mistakes; teams race.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Keeping records: date, description, in, out, running balance. Receipts
  for everything. Why cash ≠ profit — a worked example where a profitable order bankrupts a
  team because they paid the supplier 30 days before the customer paid them.
- **Work (50):** Set up the venture ledger. Record the float, record any spend so far. Then run
  a real, small transaction — a pre-order, a deposit, a first sale — and record it properly.
- **Close (15):** Ledgers checked against receipts by a partner team.
- **Artifact:** Ledger with ≥ 3 real entries and matching receipts.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Cash plan and decisions

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Runway.* You have $80 and spend $12 a week. How many weeks? Now add a $30
  sale in week 3.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** A 6-week cash plan: opening balance, expected in, expected out, closing
  balance, week by week. The week the balance goes negative is the week you must act.
- **Work (50):** Build the cash plan. Identify the pinch week. Write two decisions that would
  fix it (cut a cost, raise price, pre-sell, change payment terms) and pick one.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Money Smart**, **Number Cruncher**. Split rule agreed and
  recorded for Module 9 proceeds.
- **Artifact:** Six-week cash plan + decision note + signed split rule.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Money Smart** (core) | Spark: sorts in/out and explains save vs. spend · Builder: correct cost-per-unit and a defended price · Founder: unit economics + break-even + a clean ledger | 25 |
| **Number Cruncher** | Produced a correct calculation that changed a decision, and can walk through it unaided | 25 |
| **Kind Business** | Contributed to the group's split rule with a reason beyond self-interest | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**Money Smart is a prerequisite for Module 9 at Builder and Founder tiers.** A learner who is
short runs the Session 1–2 catch-up (cost sheet + defended price) with a Helper.

**Rubric — the money artifact:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Costs | Missing obvious inputs | All inputs listed, per-unit correct | Includes time, waste, and a contingency line |
| Price | Guessed | Cost-plus with a reason | Triangulated across three methods and tested with real people |
| Accuracy | Arithmetic errors change the conclusion | Minor errors, conclusion holds | Correct, and self-checked before submission |
| Judgement | Reports numbers only | Says what the numbers mean | Names the decision the numbers force, including bad news |

## Differentiation & access

- **Calculators and spreadsheets are allowed at every tier.** This module assesses reasoning
  about money, not arithmetic fluency.
- **Learners with maths anxiety:** Start from the two-jar model at any age — it works at 15 as
  well as 6. Give the cost sheet pre-filled with two lines as a worked start.
- **Money-sensitive families:** Never use real family spending as an example. All scenarios are
  business scenarios. See the safeguarding note on money conversations in
  [05-standards-and-safeguarding.md](../05-standards-and-safeguarding.md).
- **Extension (Builder):** Model what happens if a key ingredient doubles in price.
- **Extension (Founder):** Add a second revenue stream and compare its margin; or model the
  business with a 20% refund rate.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Free labour.** Teams will price as if their time costs nothing, then be shocked in Module 9
  when the work is exhausting. Insist on a labour line even if the rate is symbolic.
- **The bulk-buying trap.** "The pack was $10 so it cost $10" is the single most common Builder
  error. Catch it on the cost sheet, not after they have priced.
- **Underpricing to be nice.** Very common at Builder tier, especially where the customers are
  friends. Frame it as fairness: an unsustainable price is a promise you cannot keep.
- **Profit treated as pocket money.** Settle the split rule *before* Module 9 or it becomes an
  argument on Market Day when everyone is tired.
- **Founders modelling in their heads.** Require the spreadsheet. Mental models hide the pinch
  week.
- **Real money handling.** Two people count, both sign, one adult witnesses. Every time. This is
  a rule, not a suggestion.

## Materials

Coins (real or realistic play) · paper jars and envelopes · cost sheet templates · calculators ·
spreadsheet access (Founder) · receipt book and cash tin with two-signature log · price test
recording sheets · ledger template.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Save Jar.* Choose one small thing to save for. Draw it on a jar and add a mark each
time something goes in.

**Builder:** *Shelf Detective.* Find one item at home. Estimate what it cost to make. Compare
with its price. Where does the difference go?

**Founder:** *Receipt Audit.* Ask to see a receipt for something the household buys often.
Identify which parts of the price are likely cost and which are likely margin.

## Family note (send after Session 4)

> Money Smarts is the module that changes how children look at prices. Your child worked out
> what their product costs to make, set a price they can defend, and tested it on real people.
>
> **Ask at dinner:** *"What does one of your things cost you to make — and what are you
> charging?"*
>
> If the second number is not comfortably bigger than the first, ask what they plan to do
> about it. That is exactly the conversation we want them having.
