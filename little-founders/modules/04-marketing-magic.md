# Module 4 — Marketing Magic

**Icon:** Megaphone · **Colour:** Energy · **Core badge:** Marketing Magician · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Modules 1–2 · **Unlocks:** counts toward Leadership Lab

> Marketing is being useful, out loud, to the right person.

## Big idea

Marketing is not tricking people. It is making sure the person who needs your thing can find
it, understand it in three seconds, and believe you. If you have to shout, your message is
probably wrong.

## Essential questions

- Who exactly am I talking to, and what do they already care about?
- What is the one thing they must understand?
- Where will they actually see it?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Make a sign that another child understands without help, and say who it is for |
| **Builder** | Describe a customer in detail, write a one-line message, and design a name, logo, and poster that match it |
| **Founder** | Write positioning for a defined segment, choose a channel with a reason, run a measured test, and report a response rate |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Sign / poster | Something that tells people about your thing | Spark |
| Customer | The person it is for | Spark |
| Message | The one thing you want them to remember | Builder |
| Brand | How people recognise and feel about you | Builder |
| Channel | Where the message reaches them | Builder |
| Segment | A group of customers who are alike in a way that matters | Founder |
| Response rate | How many acted, out of how many saw it | Founder |
| Call to action | What you are asking them to do next | Founder |

## Spark (6–8) — 4 × 45 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Signs that work

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Spot the Sign.* Photos of shop signs; which ones can you read from far away?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Good signs are **big**, **clear**, and **say one thing**. Show a cluttered
  sign next to a simple one.
- **Work (25):** **Sign Safari.** Walk the building photographing or drawing signs. Sort into
  "I understood it straight away" and "I had to work it out."
- **Close (8):** What did the good ones have in common?
- **Artifact:** Sorted sign collection.

### Session 2 · MAKE — My sign

- **Warm-Up (5):** *One Word.* If your business could only use one word, what is it?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Draw the picture, then the word, then the price. In that order — pictures
  are read first.
- **Work (25):** Make a poster on A3 for their product using the brand colours: Sunshine for the
  background block, Navy for words. Rule: no more than six words.
- **Close (8):** Posters on the wall, viewed from across the room. Anything unreadable comes back.
- **Artifact:** Six-word poster.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Does it work?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Three Seconds.* Guide flashes a poster for three seconds; learners say what
  it sold.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** If they cannot say it in three seconds, it is not their fault.
- **Work (25):** **Three-Second Test.** Take posters to another class. Flash each for three
  seconds, ask "what was it for?" Tally right and wrong.
- **Close (8):** Whose poster scored highest? What was different?
- **Artifact:** Three-second test tally.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Poster v2

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Make It Bigger.* Guide shrinks and enlarges text; vote on what works.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Fix one thing: bigger words, fewer words, or a clearer picture.
- **Work (25):** Remake the poster with one improvement. Test once more with a Helper.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Marketing Magician**.
- **Artifact:** Poster v2 with the change named.

## Builder (9–12) — 4 × 60 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Who exactly?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Same Ad, Wrong Person.* Show an advert clearly aimed at someone else. Why
  does it not land?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** From "everyone" to *someone*. A customer sketch: age, what their day
  looks like, what they already use, what would make them care, where they are at 4pm.
- **Work (35):** Each team builds two customer sketches for their Module 2 idea, then interviews
  two real people who match one sketch to check it.
- **Close (10):** Read one sketch aloud; the room says whether it is a real person or a
  stereotype.
- **Artifact:** Two customer sketches, one validated.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Message, name, look

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Name Game.* Rate five made-up business names on "can you spell it after
  hearing it once?"
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The one-line message: *[Product] helps [customer] [benefit].* Then name
  (short, sayable, spellable) and look (two colours, one font, one shape). Reference the
  Little Founders system as a worked example of consistency.
- **Work (35):** Draft five names, pick one by team vote, write the one-line message, sketch a
  logo, choose two colours. Everything on one A3 **brand sheet**.
- **Close (10):** Brand sheets displayed; each team reads its message in exactly one breath.
- **Artifact:** Brand sheet (name, message, logo sketch, colours).

### Session 3 · SHOW — Poster and channel test

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Where Would They See It?* List every place your customer looks in a day.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Channels: poster, word of mouth, a note home, a stall, a class
  announcement, a school newsletter. Cheapest reliable channel wins for a first test.
- **Work (35):** Build the poster and one other channel piece (a 20-second spoken announcement,
  a flyer, a sign-up sheet). Run the three-second test on the poster with 10 people, and deliver
  the spoken piece to one class.
- **Close (10):** Record results: how many understood, how many asked a question, how many
  signed up.
- **Artifact:** Poster + second channel piece + results tally.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Sharpen it

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Cut It in Half.* Take any message and remove half the words. Better or worse?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Honest marketing. You may make it appealing; you may not make it untrue.
  Three examples of claims that cross the line.
- **Work (35):** Revise message and poster from the test data. Add a call to action. Run an
  honesty check against the group's fairness rule from Module 2.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Marketing Magician**, **Storyteller**, **Design Eye**.
- **Artifact:** Final brand sheet + poster v2 + honesty check.

## Founder (13–16) — 4 × 90 min

### Session 1 · NOTICE — Segments and insight

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Split the Market.* Take a broad market (school lunches) and split it four
  ways that actually change behaviour.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Segmentation that matters is behavioural, not demographic. Then
  *insight* — the true, uncomfortable, specific thing your customer feels but has not said.
- **Work (50):** Choose one segment. Mine the Module 1 interview quotes for an insight and write
  it in the customer's voice. Test the insight with three people from that segment: read it and
  ask "is that true for you?"
- **Close (15):** Read insights aloud. The room flags anything that sounds like a marketer wrote
  it.
- **Artifact:** Segment definition + validated insight.

### Session 2 · MAKE — Positioning and creative

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Steal the Line.* Read five real taglines; identify which promise something
  specific and which are noise.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** From positioning (Module 2) to message hierarchy: headline (the promise),
  support (the proof), call to action (the next step). One promise per piece.
- **Work (50):** Produce two different creative executions of the same message — deliberately
  different, not variations — using brand-consistent type and colour. Each must carry a
  measurable call to action.
- **Close (15):** Pin both; predict which will win and write the prediction down.
- **Artifact:** Two creative executions with a written prediction.

### Session 3 · SHOW — Measured channel test

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Denominator Discipline.* "We got 12 likes" — 12 out of what?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Response rate = actions ÷ reach. Set up an A/B test properly: same
  channel, same time, one variable, pre-stated sample size.
- **Work (50):** Run the test — two posters in two locations with separate sign-up sheets, two
  versions of a message to two class groups, or a supervised online post with distinct links.
  Minimum reach 50 per version.
- **Close (15):** Collect raw numbers only. No interpretation yet.
- **Artifact:** Test setup + raw results with reach and actions.

### Session 4 · CHANGE — Read the data, decide the plan

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Small Numbers Lie.* 3/10 vs. 25/100 — which do you trust?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Reading a result honestly: was the difference big enough to act on, and
  did anything else differ? Then the Module 9 marketing plan: message, two channels, timing,
  budget, and what you will measure.
- **Work (50):** Calculate response rates, compare to the prediction, and write the marketing
  plan for Startup Challenge. Include an ethics line: what you will not do to get attention.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Marketing Magician**, **Storyteller**, **Design Eye**,
  **Digital Safe** where an online channel was used.
- **Artifact:** Response rate analysis + one-page marketing plan.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing Magician** (core) | Produced a message a stranger understood unaided, aimed at a named customer | 25 |
| **Storyteller** | Explained why the customer should care in a way that used the customer's own words | 25 |
| **Design Eye** | Applied colour, type, and hierarchy so the most important thing is read first | 25 |
| **Digital Safe** | Used an online channel following every rule in the online conduct agreement, with no personal information disclosed | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the module).

**Rubric — the marketing artifact:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience | Not stated or "everyone" | Named and described | Specific segment with evidence and an insight in their words |
| Message | Describes the product | States a benefit | One clear promise, understood by strangers in 3 seconds |
| Craft | Hard to read | Clean, legible, consistent | Hierarchy deliberate; brand system used correctly |
| Evidence | No testing | Tested, results recorded | Measured with a denominator; decision changed by the result |
| Honesty | Claim not supportable | Claims are true | Actively removed an appealing claim they could not stand behind |

## Differentiation & access

- **Drawing-averse learners:** Photograph, collage, or type. Craft is assessed on clarity, not
  drawing ability.
- **Colour vision differences:** Every poster must work in greyscale. Print one in greyscale as
  a check — a genuinely useful discipline for all learners.
- **No online access:** All Founder channel tests can be run entirely offline with sign-up
  sheets. Nothing in the badge criteria requires the internet.
- **Extension (Builder):** Design the same message for two different customer sketches and
  compare.
- **Extension (Founder):** Calculate cost per acquisition — money spent ÷ people who acted — and
  compare it to the margin from Module 3.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Logo fever.** Teams will spend three sessions on a logo and none on the message. Time-box
  the logo to 15 minutes and be firm.
- **Marketing before there is anything to market.** If the product is still vague, the poster
  will be vague. Send the team back to Module 1's problem statement rather than polishing.
- **"Everyone loved it."** Ask for the denominator every single time. It is the habit this
  module exists to build.
- **Copying real brands.** Distinguish inspiration from imitation early — a logo that is a
  known brand with one letter changed does not pass.
- **Overclaiming.** "The best in the world." Introduce the honesty check before it appears, not
  after.
- **Online safety drift.** Any online channel needs the conduct agreement signed, an adult
  account, no faces, no surnames, no location. No exceptions for "just this once."

## Materials

A3 paper and card · thick markers in brand colours · brand sheet templates · customer sketch
templates · camera/tablet · sign-up sheets · greyscale printer access · three-second test
timer cards.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**Spark:** *Best Sign Wins.* On your next walk, pick the best sign you see and say why.

**Builder:** *Advert Autopsy.* Watch or read one advert. What is the promise? Who is it for?
What do they want you to do?

**Founder:** *Denominator Hunt.* Find any marketing claim with a number in it. What is missing
from the number? Bring the worst example you can find.
