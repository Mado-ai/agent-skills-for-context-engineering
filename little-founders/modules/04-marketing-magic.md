# Course 4 — Marketing Magic

**Icon:** Megaphone · **Colour:** Energy · **Core badge:** Marketing Magician · **Sessions:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Courses 1–2 · **Français:** La Magie du Marketing

> Marketing is being useful, out loud, to the right person.

## Big idea

Marketing is not tricking people. It is making sure the person who needs your thing can find it,
understand it in three seconds, and believe you. If you have to shout, your message is probably
wrong.

## Essential questions

- Who exactly am I talking to, and what do they already care about?
- What is the one thing they must understand?
- Where will they actually see it?

## Objectives by tier

| | A learner can… |
|---|---|
| 🌱 **Little Sprouts** | Make a sign another child understands without help, and say who it is for |
| 🔨 **Junior Builders** | Describe a customer, write a one-line message, and design a name, logo, and poster that match |
| 🚀 **Young Founders** | Write positioning for a named group, run two channels, and report how many people responded out of how many saw it |
| 👑 **Founder CEOs** | Find a segment insight, run a measured A/B test, calculate cost per customer, and write a marketing plan |

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning | From tier |
|---|---|---|
| Sign / poster | Something that tells people about your thing | Sprouts |
| Customer | The person it is for | Sprouts |
| Message | The one thing you want them to remember | Builders |
| Brand | How people recognise and feel about you | Builders |
| Channel | Where the message reaches them | Builders |
| Call to action | What you are asking them to do next | Founders |
| Response rate | How many acted, out of how many saw it | Founders |
| Segment | A group of customers alike in a way that matters | CEOs |
| Cost per customer | Money spent ÷ people who actually bought | CEOs |

## 🌱 Little Sprouts (5–7) — 4 × 45 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Signs that work

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Spot the Sign.* Photos of shop signs; which can you read from far away?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Good signs are **big**, **clear**, and **say one thing**. Cluttered sign
  next to a simple one.
- **Work (25):** **Sign Safari.** Walk the building photographing or drawing signs. Sort into "I
  understood it straight away" and "I had to work it out."
- **Close (8):** What did the good ones have in common?
- **Artifact:** Sorted sign collection.

### S2 · MAKE — My sign

- **Warm-Up (5):** *One Word.* If your business could use one word, what is it?
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Picture first, then the word, then the price. Pictures are read first.
- **Work (25):** A3 poster in brand colours — Sunshine block, Navy words. Rule: six words
  maximum.
- **Close (8):** Posters on the wall, viewed from across the room. Anything unreadable comes back.
- **Artifact:** Six-word poster.

### S3 · SHOW — Does it work?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Three Seconds.* Guide flashes a poster for three seconds; learners say what
  it sold.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** If they cannot say it in three seconds, it is not their fault.
- **Work (25):** **Three-Second Test** on another class. Flash each poster; ask "what was it
  for?" Tally right and wrong.
- **Close (8):** Whose scored highest? What was different?
- **Artifact:** Three-second test tally.

### S4 · CHANGE — Poster v2

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Make It Bigger.* Guide shrinks and enlarges text; vote on what works.
- **Mini-Lesson (7):** Fix one thing: bigger words, fewer words, or a clearer picture.
- **Work (25):** Remake the poster with one improvement. Test once more with a Helper.
- **Close (8):** Badge check for **Marketing Magician**.
- **Artifact:** Poster v2 with the change named.

## 🔨 Junior Builders (8–10) — 4 × 60 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Who exactly?

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Same Ad, Wrong Person.* Show an advert clearly aimed at someone else. Why
  does it not land?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** From "everyone" to *someone*. A customer sketch: age, what their day
  looks like, what they already use, where they are at 4 pm.
- **Work (35):** Build two customer sketches, then interview two real people who match one to
  check it.
- **Close (10):** Read one sketch; the room says whether it is a real person or a stereotype.
- **Artifact:** Two customer sketches, one validated.

### S2 · MAKE — Message, name, look

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Name Game.* Rate five made-up names on "can you spell it after hearing it
  once?"
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** The one-line message: *[Product] helps [customer] [benefit].* Then name
  (short, sayable, spellable) and look (two colours, one font, one shape). Little Founders itself
  is the worked example of consistency.
- **Work (35):** Draft five names, vote, write the message, sketch a logo, choose two colours —
  all on one A3 **brand sheet**.
- **Close (10):** Read your message in exactly one breath.
- **Artifact:** Brand sheet.

### S3 · SHOW — Poster and a second channel

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Where Would They See It?* List everywhere your customer looks in a day.
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Channels: poster, word of mouth, a note home, a stall, a class
  announcement. Cheapest reliable channel wins for a first try.
- **Work (35):** Build the poster and one other channel piece (a 20-second spoken announcement, a
  flyer, a sign-up sheet). Run the three-second test on 10 people; deliver the spoken piece to
  one class.
- **Close (10):** Record: how many understood, how many asked a question, how many signed up.
- **Artifact:** Poster + second channel piece + results tally.

### S4 · CHANGE — Sharpen it

- **Warm-Up (5):** *Cut It in Half.* Remove half the words. Better or worse?
- **Mini-Lesson (10):** Honest marketing. You may make it appealing; you may not make it untrue.
  Three examples of claims that cross the line.
- **Work (35):** Revise message and poster from the results. Add a call to action. Run the honesty
  check against the Course 2 fairness rule.
- **Close (10):** Badge check: **Marketing Magician**, **Storyteller**, **Design Eye**.
- **Artifact:** Final brand sheet + poster v2 + honesty check.

## 🚀 Young Founders (11–13) — 4 × 75 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Positioning for a named group

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Split the Market.* Take school lunches and split it four ways that actually
  change behaviour.
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Positioning: *"For [who], we are the only [category] that [difference],
  because [proof]."* Proof comes from Courses 1–3.
- **Work (42):** Choose one group. Write the positioning line and find its proof. Test the line on
  five people from that group: read it and ask "is that true for you?"
- **Close (13):** The room flags any line a competitor could also say.
- **Artifact:** Group definition + tested positioning line.

### S2 · MAKE — Message hierarchy and creative

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Slide or Sentence?* Which information needs a visual, and which does not?
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Headline (the promise), support (the proof), call to action (the next
  step). One promise per piece.
- **Work (42):** Produce two different pieces for two different channels, both carrying a
  measurable call to action, both brand-consistent.
- **Close (13):** Pin both; write down which you predict will work better.
- **Artifact:** Two pieces + a written prediction.

### S3 · SHOW — Count the denominator

- **Warm-Up (8):** *"We got 12 likes."* Twelve out of what?
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Response rate = actions ÷ reach. Same time, same audience, one variable,
  a minimum sample agreed in advance.
- **Work (42):** Run both channels for real. Minimum reach 30 per channel. Separate sign-up
  sheets or distinct codes so the results cannot blur.
- **Close (13):** Collect raw numbers only. No interpretation yet.
- **Artifact:** Raw results with reach and actions for both channels.

### S4 · CHANGE — Read it and plan

- **Warm-Up (8):** *Small Numbers Lie.* 3/10 vs. 25/100 — which do you trust?
- **Mini-Lesson (12):** Was the difference big enough to act on? Did anything else differ? Then
  the Course 9 marketing plan.
- **Work (42):** Calculate response rates, compare to the prediction, write the one-page marketing
  plan: message, two channels, timing, budget, what you will measure, and an ethics line.
- **Close (13):** Badge check: **Marketing Magician**, **Storyteller**, **Design Eye**, **Digital
  Safe** where an online channel was used.
- **Artifact:** Response rate analysis + one-page marketing plan.

## 👑 Founder CEOs (14–17) — 4 × 90 min

### S1 · NOTICE — Segments and insight

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Split the Market*, behaviourally, not demographically.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Segmentation that matters is about what people *do*. Then *insight* — the
  true, uncomfortable, specific thing your customer feels but has not said.
- **Work (50):** Choose one segment. Mine the Course 1 quotes for an insight and write it in the
  customer's voice. Test it with three people from that segment: "is that true for you?"
- **Close (15):** Read insights; the room flags anything that sounds like a marketer wrote it.
- **Artifact:** Segment definition + validated insight.

### S2 · MAKE — Two real executions

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Steal the Line.* Five real taglines; which promise something specific?
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Message hierarchy, then creative discipline: two deliberately different
  executions, not two variations of one. Each carries a measurable call to action.
- **Work (50):** Produce both, brand-consistent in type and colour. Write down which you predict
  will win and why.
- **Close (15):** Pin both with the prediction.
- **Artifact:** Two executions + written prediction.

### S3 · SHOW — Measured A/B test

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Denominator Discipline.*
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Setting up an A/B test properly: same channel, same time, one variable,
  pre-stated sample size, and a rule for when you stop looking.
- **Work (50):** Run the test — two posters in two locations with separate sign-up sheets, two
  message versions to two class groups, or a supervised online post with distinct links. Minimum
  reach 50 per version.
- **Close (15):** Raw numbers on the board.
- **Artifact:** Test setup + raw results.

### S4 · CHANGE — Cost per customer and the plan

- **Warm-Up (10):** *Was It Worth It?* Spent $20, got 4 customers. Now compare to your margin.
- **Mini-Lesson (15):** Cost per customer = money spent ÷ people who bought. Compare to
  contribution per unit from Course 3. If it costs more to get them than you make, you do not
  have a marketing problem — you have a business model problem.
- **Work (50):** Calculate response rates and cost per customer. Write the Course 9 marketing
  plan with a budget, a measurement plan, and an ethics line naming what you will not do for
  attention.
- **Close (15):** Badge check: **Marketing Magician**, **Storyteller**, **Design Eye**, **Digital
  Safe**, **Number Cruncher**.
- **Artifact:** Response rate + cost per customer + one-page marketing plan.

## Evidence & badge criteria

| Badge | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing Magician** (core) | Produced a message a stranger understood unaided, aimed at a named customer | 25 |
| **Storyteller** | Explained why the customer should care using the customer's own words | 25 |
| **Design Eye** | Used colour, type, and hierarchy so the most important thing is read first | 25 |
| **Digital Safe** | Used an online channel following every rule in the online conduct agreement, disclosing no personal information | 25 |
| **Number Cruncher** | Calculated a response rate or cost per customer that changed the plan | 25 |

Session completion: 10 Little Points each (40 for the course).

**Rubric — the marketing artifact:**

| | Emerging | Secure | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience | Not stated, or "everyone" | Named and described | Specific segment with evidence and an insight in their words |
| Message | Describes the product | States a benefit | One clear promise, understood by strangers in three seconds |
| Craft | Hard to read | Clean, legible, consistent | Hierarchy deliberate; brand system used correctly |
| Evidence | No testing | Tested, results recorded | Measured with a denominator; the decision changed because of it |
| Honesty | Claim not supportable | Claims are true | Removed an appealing claim they could not stand behind |

## Differentiation & access

- **Drawing-averse learners:** Photograph, collage, or type. Craft is assessed on clarity, not
  drawing ability.
- **Colour vision differences:** Every poster must work in greyscale. Print one as a check — a
  genuinely useful discipline for everyone.
- **Bilingual cohorts:** Produce the message in both languages and three-second test each
  separately. The gap between them is often the most interesting result of the course.
- **No online access:** Every Founders and CEOs channel test can be run offline with sign-up
  sheets. Nothing in the badge criteria requires the internet.
- **Extension (Builders):** Design the same message for two customer sketches and compare.
- **Extension (Founders):** Add a third channel and rank all three by response rate.

## Guide watch-outs

- **Logo fever.** Teams will spend three sessions on a logo and none on the message. Time-box the
  logo to 15 minutes and be firm.
- **Marketing before there is anything to market.** If the product is vague, the poster will be
  vague. Send them back to the Course 1 problem statement rather than polishing.
- **"Everyone loved it."** Ask for the denominator every single time. It is the habit this course
  exists to build.
- **Copying real brands.** A known logo with one letter changed does not pass. Distinguish
  inspiration from imitation early.
- **Overclaiming.** "The best in the world." Introduce the honesty check before it appears.
- **Online safety drift.** Any online channel needs the signed conduct agreement, a staff-owned
  account, no faces, no surnames, no location. No exceptions for "just this once."

## Materials

A3 paper and card · thick markers in brand colours · brand sheet templates · customer sketch
templates · camera/tablet · sign-up sheets · greyscale printer access · three-second test timer
cards · distinct link codes or coloured sign-up sheets for A/B tests.

## Home extension — 10 minutes

**🌱 Sprouts:** *Best Sign Wins.* On your next walk, pick the best sign you see and say why.

**🔨 Builders:** *Advert Autopsy.* Watch or read one advert. What is the promise? Who is it for?
What do they want you to do?

**🚀 Young Founders:** *Where Do They Look?* Follow one adult's day and list every place a message
could have reached them. Which would you pick?

**👑 Founder CEOs:** *Denominator Hunt.* Find a marketing claim with a number in it. What is
missing from the number? Bring the worst example you can find.
