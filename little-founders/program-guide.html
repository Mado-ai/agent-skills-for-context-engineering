The learning model

One cycle, run ten times

Every course at every tier runs the same four beats, one per session. Learners meet the content ten times — but they learn the shape of entrepreneurial work by repetition.

Notice
Observe, question, gather. Find the problem before the solution. Session 1
Make
Build the smallest version that someone can react to. Session 2
Show
Put it in front of someone who is not you. Session 3
Change
Act on what you heard. Decide what to keep. Session 4

A journey that grows with them

Four tiers, four certifications

The same ten courses run at every tier, and each pass raises what the learner is responsible for. Nothing is finished at a tier — it comes back harder. That is the spiral.

The pair most often blurred: Junior Builders make things and sell them — the learning is in production, pricing, and the moment a stranger hands over money. Delivery

Four ways to run it

FormatCadenceDurationNotes
Weekly club1 session/week~42 weeksDefault. Fits a Canadian school year with breaks
Semester course2 sessions/week21 weeksKeep Startup Challenge inside one week
March Break / summer camp3 sessions/day14 daysCourses 1–5 in week one, 6–10 in week two
Single-course taster4 sessions4 weeksCourses 1 or 5 stand alone. Courses 3, 7, 9 do not

Never compress Startup Challenge below five sessions. The build–sell–learn loop is the part learners remember, and the dry run is where every Market Day failure shows up first.

Running it in Canada

What has to be checked before you trade

Education, child protection, food safety, and privacy are largely provincial, and boards add their own rules. These checks belong in Startup Challenge Session 1 — not in Market Day week.

CheckWhy it matters
Permission to tradeMany boards require written approval for any sale to the public
Municipal stall permitA market, park, or sidewalk stall may need one even for a school group
Food handlingHome-prepared food is prohibited for public sale in most jurisdictions. This can end a venture concept — check early
ScreeningPolice record checks including vulnerable sector checks for every adult, before contact
PrivacyPIPEDA plus provincial law (Quebec Law 25, BC and Alberta PIPA) and board student-records rules
CASLMarketing email and texts are regulated. Posters and in-person selling are the simplest compliant path
Sales taxGST/HST registration is not required below the $30,000 small-supplier threshold. Provincial rules on the goods still apply
ContractsMinors cannot enter binding contracts. Partnership agreements here are learning agreements, countersigned by an adult
French-language requirementsIn Quebec, public signage and communications are subject to the Charter of the French Language

This is guidance, not legal advice. Confirm with your board, municipality, and insurer before any public trading. The program also runs fully in English, in French, or bilingually — fixed terms carry both names on every learner-facing surface.
