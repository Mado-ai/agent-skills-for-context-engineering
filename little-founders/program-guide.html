The learning model

One cycle, run ten times

Every module at every tier runs the same four beats, one per session. Learners meet the content ten times — but they learn the shape of entrepreneurial work by repetition.

Notice
Observe, question, gather. Find the problem before the solution. Session 1
Make
Build the smallest version that someone can react to. Session 2
Show
Put it in front of someone who is not you. Session 3
Change
Act on what you heard. Decide what to keep. Session 4

Tiers

Same ten modules, three real levels

Tiers are not the same lesson made easier. Each one changes what the learner is responsible for, what counts as evidence, and how much of the work is theirs alone.

Spark

Ages 6–8

“What would make things better?”

  • Session45 min, movement every 12–15
  • MoneyPlay money only, never real
  • CustomerClassmates and family
  • EvidenceDrawings and scribed sentences
  • Ratio1 adult per 6 learners

Builder

Ages 9–12

“Who is it for, and does it work?”

  • Session60 min
  • MoneyReal, capped at a $50 team float
  • CustomerOther classes, staff, families
  • EvidenceOne-page plan, cost sheet, prototype
  • Ratio1 adult per 8 learners

Founder

Ages 13–16

“Is this a business, and can I run it?”

  • Session90 min
  • MoneyReal, with a ledger and unit economics
  • CustomerStrangers and the public
  • EvidenceCanvas, financial model, pitch on video
  • Ratio1 adult per 12 learners

Inside a session

Four blocks, every time

The mini-lesson cap is the important number. The most common way this program fails is a Guide explaining entrepreneurship for 25 minutes to children who have not yet made anything.

BlockSparkBuilderFounderPurpose
Warm-Up5 min5 min10 minA game that plants today's idea before it is named
Mini-Lesson7 min10 min15 minHard cap. If it runs long, cut it — not the Work
Work25 min35 min50 minMake, test, calculate, or interview. The centre of the session
Close8 min10 min15 minShare one thing, log it, award points, name what comes next

Assessment

28 badges, no tests, no ranking

Every badge is criterion-referenced, so every learner can earn every badge. Several can only be earned after something went wrong — which is the point.

Points measure participation and effort — the things a learner controls — never quality. They are never deducted as punishment: a system that spends children's earned credit teaches that effort is confiscatable.

Delivery

Four ways to run it

FormatCadenceDurationNotes
Weekly club1 session/week~42 weeksDefault. Fits a school year with holidays
Semester course2 sessions/week21 weeksKeep Startup Challenge inside one week
Camp intensive3 sessions/day14 daysModules 1–5 in week one, 6–10 in week two
Single-module taster4 sessions4 weeksModules 1 or 5 stand alone. Modules 3, 7, 9 do not

Never compress Startup Challenge below five sessions. The build–sell–learn loop is the part learners remember, and the dry run is where every Market Day failure shows up first.
