# Standards, Safeguarding & Canadian Operations

This program puts children in front of real customers and, from Junior Builders upward, real
money. That is what makes it work, and it is also why this document is required reading before
Course 3.

**Nothing here is legal advice.** Education, child protection, food safety, consumer protection,
and privacy are largely provincial in Canada, and school boards add their own rules on top. Where
this document and your setting's policy differ, **your policy wins** — and where your policy is
silent, treat this as the minimum and confirm with your board, municipality, or insurer before
trading.

## Safeguarding

### Adult supervision

| Tier | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 🌱 Little Sprouts | 1 : 6 | Plus one adult on money at any trading event |
| 🔨 Junior Builders | 1 : 8 | Plus one adult on money |
| 🚀 Young Founders | 1 : 10 | Plus one adult on money |
| 👑 Founder CEOs | 1 : 12 | Plus one adult on money |

All adults — including parent volunteers, mentors, and alumni Helpers — must clear the checks your
setting requires **before** any contact with learners. In most Canadian jurisdictions that means a
police record check including a vulnerable sector check; boards commonly add their own volunteer
screening. An unchecked mentor may attend only as a member of a public audience.

### Contact with people outside the setting

Courses 8, 9, and 10 involve real external contact.

- No learner is ever alone with an adult who is not checked staff.
- All partnership approaches, visits, and calls happen with a checked adult present.
- No exchange of personal contact details between a learner and an external adult. Follow-up runs
  through the setting's address.
- Off-site visits follow your trip procedure, including consent and risk assessment.
- Mentors are briefed in writing: their role is questions and encouragement. They do not collect
  learners' contact details, and they do not offer employment, investment, or commercial
  arrangements.

### Photography, video, and publishing

- Maintain a **consent list** — photo, video, internal use, external/social use, each consented
  separately. Check it before every recording, not at publishing time.
- Course 5 at Founder CEOs requires video for self-assessment. A learner without consent
  self-assesses from audio or peer notes. No exceptions, no pressure.
- Demo Day has a marked no-photography zone.
- Never publish a learner's surname, school, class, or location alongside their image.

### Online activity

Any online channel in Course 4 or Course 9 requires a signed **online conduct agreement**:

- The account is created, owned, and monitored by a staff member. Learners never hold credentials.
- No surnames, no school name, no uniform, no location, no faces without consent.
- No direct messaging with the public. Comments moderated or off.
- Any contact from a stranger is reported to the Guide immediately and not answered.
- The account is closed or archived at the end of the program.

**Canadian anti-spam legislation (CASL)** applies to commercial electronic messages — email, text,
and some social messaging. Practically, for this program: do not send marketing email or texts to
anyone who has not clearly asked for it, always identify who is sending, and always include a way
to unsubscribe. The simplest compliant path at every tier is to market with posters, in person,
and on a staff-run public page rather than by direct message. Confirm the current requirements
before any email campaign.

The **Digital Safe** badge is awarded for meeting all of this. It is a real standard, not a
participation sticker.

### Money-related safeguarding

Talking about money is not neutral for every family.

- **Never use a learner's family finances as an example.** All scenarios are business scenarios.
- Watch for disclosures. A child who says "we can't afford that" in a pricing discussion may be
  telling you something that needs following up through your safeguarding route.
- No learner contributes their own or their family's money to a venture. Floats come from the
  program.
- Anything a learner keeps follows the group's split rule, agreed in Course 3, and is the same for
  everyone regardless of individual sales.
- A learner anxious about money may take a non-money role without explanation to the group.

## Handling real money

Applies from Course 3 (Junior Builders upward) and throughout Course 9. All amounts CAD.

**The four rules**

1. **Two learners count, one adult witnesses.** All three initial the sheet. Every count, every
   time.
2. **Count at three fixed points** — open, midpoint, close. Never reconstruct a count later.
3. **The tin is never unattended and never held by one person alone.** It lives in a named, locked
   place between sessions.
4. **A discrepancy is investigated, never adjusted.** Write the actual figure and the gap, then
   look at the process.

**When money goes missing.** It is almost always a process failure — miscounted float, unlogged
purchase, wrong change. Investigate the process out loud, in front of everyone, without naming
anyone. Never question a child about theft; that is a matter for your setting's procedures and its
designated child-protection lead, not for a business lesson.

**Float limits.** Junior Builders $50 · Young Founders $100 · Founder CEOs $150 per team. The float
is signed out and signed back.

**Proceeds.** Governed by the group's split rule from Course 3 — typically reinvest / share / give
back. Decided before Market Day, recorded, and honoured even when the result disappoints someone.
Where your setting requires proceeds to go to a school fund or charity, say so at the start of
Course 3, not after learners have imagined otherwise.

## Canadian operating checks

Run these in **Course 9, Session 1**. Discovering a blocker in Market Day week is a preventable
failure.

| Check | Who confirms | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Permission to trade on the premises | Board / school leadership | Many boards require written approval for any sale to the public |
| Municipal rules for a public stall | Guide | A market, park, or sidewalk stall may need a municipal permit even for a school group |
| Food handling and allergen rules | Guide + public health unit | **Home-prepared food is prohibited for public sale in most Canadian jurisdictions.** Many boards also have allergen policies stricter than public health. Check in Session 1 — this can end a venture concept |
| Allergen labelling | Team | Required for any food, even where preparation is permitted |
| Product safety | Guide | No small parts where under-3s may be present; no electricals; Health Canada consumer product rules apply to anything sold |
| Insurance / liability for a public event | Board or operator | Usually covered by existing policy; confirm in writing |
| Sales tax | Guide | GST/HST registration is not required below the $30,000 small-supplier threshold, which no cohort will approach. Provincial sales tax rules on the goods themselves still apply — confirm locally |
| Contracts and registration | Guide | Minors generally cannot enter binding contracts, and business registration requires the age of majority. Partnership agreements in this program are **learning agreements between people, not legal contracts**, and are countersigned by an adult |
| Privacy — customer names, emails, sign-ups | Guide | Collect the minimum, store on the setting's systems, delete at program end. Never on a learner's personal device. Federal PIPEDA plus provincial law (Quebec's Law 25, BC and Alberta PIPA) and your board's student-records rules all bear on this |
| French-language requirements | Guide | In Quebec, commercial signage and public communications are subject to the Charter of the French Language. Plan bilingual or French-predominant signage for any public-facing Market Day and confirm current requirements |
| Risk assessment | Guide | Per your template; covers crowd, stalls, power, weather, cash security |

**Things this program does not do:** register companies, open bank accounts in a learner's name,
take deposits for goods that do not yet exist beyond a supervised pre-order, sell online to the
public without staff-owned accounts, or make claims about earnings to families.

## Inclusion

- **Cost never gates participation.** No learner supplies their own materials. If a home extension
  needs anything beyond a pencil, it has been written wrongly.
- **Every assessment route has a non-writing, non-public-speaking equivalent** carrying identical
  credit — see [04-assessment-and-badges.md](04-assessment-and-badges.md).
- **Teams are assigned, not chosen**, from Junior Builders upward. Self-selection reliably leaves
  the same children last.
- **Roles rotate.** Nobody is permanently the money person, and nobody is permanently the tidier.
- **Public presentation is never compulsory in a single format.** Options are offered to everyone
  so no learner is visibly opted out.
- **Language.** Learners may work in English or French throughout. Guides do not correct language
  errors during business work — it competes with the thinking the session is for.
- **First Nations, Inuit, and Métis learners and communities.** Where a venture touches traditional
  knowledge, imagery, or land, involve the appropriate community voice before it is built, not as
  a review afterwards. Course 8's stakeholder analysis is the right place for this conversation,
  and it should be led by someone with standing to lead it.
- **Newcomer families.** Consent forms, family notes, and Market Day invitations should be
  available in the languages your cohort's families actually read.

## Emotional safety

Course 9 is genuinely stressful and Course 10 is emotionally exposing. Plan for it.

- Name feelings before numbers in Course 9, Session 5. Some cohorts cry; that is not a sign the
  course went wrong.
- Never compare teams publicly on takings. Badges are criterion-based precisely so the team that
  lost money can still hold every badge.
- A learner who wants to withdraw from a public-facing role on the day may do so immediately,
  without justifying it. Have a behind-the-scenes role ready.
- Announce the alumni route — Helper, Mentor, next cohort — at Demo Day, so the energy has
  somewhere to go.

## Pre-program checklist

Complete before Session 1 of Course 1:

- [ ] Adult ratios confirmed for every planned session and event
- [ ] All adults cleared per policy, including vulnerable sector checks where required
- [ ] Consent list created — photo, video, internal, external
- [ ] Consent forms and family letters available in the languages your families read
- [ ] Online conduct agreement drafted, ready for Course 4
- [ ] Money handling procedure agreed with leadership, ready for Course 3
- [ ] Float source and limits confirmed
- [ ] Split rule constraints known — does the setting require proceeds to go anywhere?
- [ ] Market Day date, venue, and permissions provisionally booked
- [ ] Food, municipal permit, trading, and insurance questions raised with leadership
- [ ] Privacy approach agreed for any customer data
- [ ] Risk assessment template located
- [ ] Child-protection lead briefed that the program includes money conversations and external
      contact
- [ ] Family letter sent explaining what the program involves, including trading and photography
